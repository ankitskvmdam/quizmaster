## What a percentage is

"Per cent" means "out of a hundred". So $x\%$ is just the fraction $\dfrac{x}{100}$.

$$
x\% \text{ of } N = \frac{x}{100} \times N
$$

> **Example**
> $15\%$ of $240 = \dfrac{15}{100} \times 240 = 36$.

## Fractions worth knowing by heart

Most exam questions get faster if you can switch between a percentage and a simple fraction without thinking.

| Fraction | Percentage | Fraction | Percentage |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\tfrac{1}{2}$ | $50\%$ | $\tfrac{1}{8}$ | $12.5\%$ |
| $\tfrac{1}{3}$ | $33\tfrac{1}{3}\%$ | $\tfrac{3}{8}$ | $37.5\%$ |
| $\tfrac{1}{4}$ | $25\%$ | $\tfrac{1}{9}$ | $11\tfrac{1}{9}\%$ |
| $\tfrac{1}{5}$ | $20\%$ | $\tfrac{1}{10}$ | $10\%$ |
| $\tfrac{1}{6}$ | $16\tfrac{2}{3}\%$ | $\tfrac{1}{12}$ | $8\tfrac{1}{3}\%$ |

> **Shortcut**
> $12.5\%$ of $640$? That is $\tfrac{1}{8}$ of $640 = 80$. No multiplication needed.

## "What percent is A of B?"

Divide the part by the whole, then multiply by $100$:

$$
\frac{A}{B} \times 100
$$

> **Example**
> $45$ is what percent of $180$? $\dfrac{45}{180} \times 100 = 25\%$.

## Finding the whole from a part

If $x\%$ of a number is $P$, the number is $P \div \dfrac{x}{100}$.

> **Example**
> $20\%$ of a number is $36$, so the number is $36 \div 0.2 = 180$.

## Percentage change

Always measure change against the **original** value:

$$
\text{change \%} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{original}}{\text{original}} \times 100
$$

> **Example**
> A price goes from ₹400 to ₹460. The change is $60$, and $\dfrac{60}{400} \times 100 = 15\%$.

> **Trap**
> "A is 25% more than B" does **not** mean "B is 25% less than A". The base is different. If $B = 100$, then $A = 125$, and B is $\dfrac{25}{125} = 20\%$ less than A.
