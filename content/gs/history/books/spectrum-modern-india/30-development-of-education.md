## Under the Company

### Early efforts

For its first 60 years the Company took no interest in education. There were only individual efforts.

| Institution | Founded by | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calcutta Madrasah | Warren Hastings, 1781 | Muslim law |
| Sanskrit College, Benaras | Jonathan Duncan (the resident), 1791 | Hindu law and philosophy |
| Fort William College | Wellesley, 1800 | To train the Company's civil servants. It closed in 1802. |

The Serampore missionaries were keen promoters of education.

### Charter Act of 1813

- The Act directed the Company to spend one lakh rupees a year on education.
- The money was not made available till 1823.
- **1817:** The Calcutta College was set up by educated Bengalis. It got a grant.
- There were three Sanskrit colleges, at Calcutta, Delhi and Agra.

### Macaulay's Minute (1835)

- There was an Orientalist–Anglicist controversy over education. Macaulay's Minute settled it in favour of the Anglicists.
- Western sciences and literature were to be taught through English alone.
- The aim was to create a class "Indian in blood and colour but English in tastes".
- Knowledge was to filter down from this class to the masses. This was the **downward filtration theory**: educate a small upper and middle class, who would pass knowledge on to the masses.

### Wood's Despatch (1854)

Wood's Despatch was the first comprehensive education plan. It is called the "Magna Carta of English Education in India".

What the Despatch laid down:

- The state was made responsible for mass education.
- A ladder of institutions was set up, from vernacular schools in the villages to universities in Calcutta, Bombay and Madras.
- English was to be used for higher studies. The vernaculars were to be used at school level.
- It stressed female education, vocational education and teacher education.
- Teaching in government institutions was to be secular.
- It provided for grants-in-aid (government grants to schools).

Developments of these years:

1. **1849:** J.E.D. Bethune founded the Bethune School at Calcutta. He was the president of the Council of Education.
2. **1857:** The universities at Calcutta, Bombay and Madras were set up.
3. After Wood's Despatch, an Agriculture Institute was started at Pusa (Bihar) and an Engineering Institute at Roorkee.

## Under the Crown

### Hunter Commission (1882–83)

- W.W. Hunter headed the commission. It focused on primary and secondary education.
- Primary education was to be given through the vernacular. It was to be under district and municipal boards.
- Secondary education was to have two streams: literary and vocational.

### Raleigh Commission and the Indian Universities Act

1. **1902:** The Raleigh Commission was set up. It led to the Indian Universities Act.
2. **1904:** Curzon's Indian Universities Act was passed. It brought more official control.
3. Five lakh rupees a year for five years were given for higher education.
4. Gokhale called the 1904 Act a "retrograde measure".

### Saddler University Commission (1917–19)

The commission was on Calcutta University. It proposed three things:

- a 12-year school course;
- an intermediate stage;
- a three-year degree.

### Hartog Committee (1929)

- It opposed hasty expansion and compulsion.
- Average students were to be diverted to vocational courses after Class VIII.
- University admissions were to be restricted.

### Wardha Scheme of Basic Education (1937)

- The Zakir Hussain committee drew up the scheme.
- It was based on Gandhi's ideas, set out in his articles in Harijan.
- It rested on 'learning through activity'.
- Hindi was to be taught from Class II to Class VII. English was to come only after Class VIII.
- **October 1939:** The Congress ministries resigned. The scheme was stalled by this and by the Second World War.

### Sergeant Plan (1944)

The Central Advisory Board of Education made this plan. It proposed:

- free compulsory education for the ages 6–11;
- abolishing the intermediate stage;
- ending adult illiteracy in 20 years;
- reaching England's level of education in 40 years.

### Other milestones (1870–1921)

1. **1870:** Education was shifted to the provinces.
2. **1882:** Punjab University was set up.
3. **1887:** Allahabad University was set up.
4. **1906:** Baroda introduced compulsory primary education.
5. **1913 Resolution:** It refused compulsory education. It wanted a university in each province.
6. **1916–1921:** Seven universities came up: Mysore, Patna, Benaras, Aligarh, Dacca, Lucknow and Osmania.
7. Under dyarchy (Montagu-Chelmsford), education went to the provincial ministries. The grants given since 1902 were stopped.

## Vernacular education

1. **1835, 1836, 1838:** William Adam wrote reports on vernacular education in Bengal and Bihar.
2. **1843–53:** James Thomson was lieutenant-governor of the North-Western Provinces (NW Provinces). He ran vernacular schools in the villages. They trained staff for the Revenue and Public Works Department.
3. **1853:** Lord Dalhousie's minute favoured vernacular education.
4. **1854–71:** The number of vernacular schools grew more than five-fold.

## Technical education

1. **1835:** A medical college was set up in Calcutta. Medical training began with it.
2. **1847:** The engineering college at Roorkee was set up.
3. **1856:** The Calcutta College of Engineering was set up.
4. By 1857 there were three medical colleges, at Calcutta, Bombay and Madras.
5. **1858:** The Poona College of Engineering was set up. It was affiliated to Bombay University.
6. Curzon set up the agriculture college at Pusa. It became the parent of similar institutions in other provinces.

## Evaluation of the education policy

1. The policy served two ends: a cheap supply of clerks, and loyalty.
2. After 1844 knowledge of English was required for government employment.
3. Mass education and women's education were neglected.
4. By 1857 Roorkee was open only to Europeans and Eurasians.
