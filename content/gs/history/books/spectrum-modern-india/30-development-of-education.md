## Gist
*Book pages 563–573.*

For its first 60 years the Company took no interest in education, apart from individual efforts: the **Calcutta Madrasah (Warren Hastings, 1781)** for Muslim law, the **Sanskrit College at Benaras (Jonathan Duncan, 1791)** for Hindu law, and **Fort William College (Wellesley, 1800)** to train civil servants (closed **1802**). **Serampore missionaries** were keen promoters of education.

The **Charter Act of 1813** directed the Company to spend **one lakh rupees a year** on education, but the money was not available till **1823**. In the **Orientalist–Anglicist controversy**, **Macaulay's Minute (1835)** favoured the Anglicists: Western sciences and literature through **English alone**. The aim was a class "**Indian in blood and colour but English in tastes**", from whom knowledge would filter to the masses — the **'downward filtration theory'**. **James Thomson**, lieutenant-governor of the **NW Provinces (1843–53)**, ran vernacular village schools.

**Wood's Despatch (1854)**, the **"Magna Carta of English Education in India"**, made the state responsible for mass education, set up a ladder from village vernacular schools to **universities in Calcutta, Bombay and Madras** (founded **1857**), used **English for higher studies and vernaculars at school level**, stressed female, vocational and teacher education, **secular** government teaching and **grants-in-aid**. **J.E.D. Bethune** founded the **Bethune School (1849)**; an **Agriculture Institute at Pusa (Bihar)** and an Engineering Institute at **Roorkee** came up.

**After the Crown:** the **Hunter Commission (1882–83)** under **W.W. Hunter** focused on primary (through vernacular, under **district and municipal boards**) and secondary education. The **Raleigh Commission (1902)** led to **Curzon's Indian Universities Act, 1904** — more official control, which **Gokhale** called a **"retrograde measure"**. **Baroda** made primary education compulsory in **1906**; the **1913 Resolution** refused compulsion. The **Saddler Commission (1917–19)** on **Calcutta University** proposed a **12-year school course**, an intermediate stage and a **three-year degree**. Between **1916 and 1921** seven universities came up, including **Patna**. Under **dyarchy** education went to provincial ministries. The **Hartog Committee (1929)** opposed hasty expansion. The **Wardha Scheme (1937)**, drawn up by the **Zakir Hussain committee** from Gandhi's ideas, rested on **'learning through activity'**. The **Sergeant Plan (1944)** of the **Central Advisory Board of Education** proposed free compulsory schooling for ages **6–11** and reaching England's level in **40 years**.

**Vernacular and technical education:** **William Adam's reports (1835, 1836, 1838)** covered **Bengal and Bihar**; **Dalhousie's 1853 minute** backed vernacular education. **Roorkee engineering college (1847)**, **Calcutta College of Engineering (1856)** and a **Calcutta medical college (1835)** were set up; **Curzon** founded the **agriculture college at Pusa**, parent of similar provincial institutions.

**Evaluation:** education policy served cheap clerical supply and loyalty; after **1844** English became necessary for government jobs; mass and women's education were neglected; by 1857 Roorkee was open only to **Europeans and Eurasians**.

## Key facts
- **Calcutta Madrasah** — **Warren Hastings, 1781**, for Muslim law.
- **Sanskrit College, Benaras** — **Jonathan Duncan** (the resident), **1791**, for Hindu law and philosophy.
- **Fort William College** — **Wellesley, 1800**, to train Company civil servants; closed in **1802**.
- **Charter Act of 1813** — **one lakh rupees annually** for education; not made available till **1823**.
- **Calcutta College (1817)**, set up by educated Bengalis, got a grant; three Sanskrit colleges at **Calcutta, Delhi and Agra**.
- **Macaulay's Minute (1835)** — settled the **Orientalist–Anglicist** row for the Anglicists; English medium.
- **Downward filtration theory** — educate a small upper/middle class who would pass knowledge to the masses.
- **James Thomson**, lieutenant-governor of **NW Provinces (1843–53)** — vernacular village schools to train staff for the **Revenue and Public Works Department**.
- **Wood's Despatch (1854)** — **"Magna Carta of English Education in India"**; first comprehensive education plan.
- Wood's Despatch: **English for higher studies**, **vernaculars at school level**; **secular** government education; **grants-in-aid**.
- Universities at **Calcutta, Bombay and Madras** set up in **1857**.
- **Bethune School, Calcutta (1849)** — **J.E.D. Bethune**, president of the **Council of Education**.
- **Agriculture Institute at Pusa (Bihar)** and **Engineering Institute at Roorkee** started after Wood's Despatch.
- Education shifted to the **provinces in 1870**.
- **Hunter Commission (1882–83)** — **W.W. Hunter**; primary education through vernacular, under **district and municipal boards**; secondary in **literary and vocational** streams.
- **Punjab University (1882)** and **Allahabad University (1887)**.
- **Raleigh Commission (1902)** → **Indian Universities Act, 1904**; **five lakh rupees a year for five years** for higher education.
- Gokhale called the 1904 Act a **"retrograde measure"**.
- **Baroda** introduced **compulsory primary education in 1906**.
- **1913 Resolution** — refused compulsory education; a **university in each province**.
- **Saddler University Commission (1917–19)** — on **Calcutta University**; **12-year school course**, intermediate stage, **three-year degree**.
- Seven universities **1916–1921**: **Mysore, Patna, Benaras, Aligarh, Dacca, Lucknow, Osmania**.
- Under **dyarchy** (Montagu-Chelmsford), education went to provincial ministries; grants given since 1902 were stopped.
- **Hartog Committee (1929)** — no hasty expansion or compulsion; divert average students to vocational courses **after Class VIII**; restrict university admissions.
- **Wardha Scheme of Basic Education (1937)** — **Zakir Hussain committee**; **'learning through activity'**; based on Gandhi's articles in **Harijan**.
- Wardha: **Hindi from Class II to VII**, **English only after Class VIII**; stalled by WWII and resignation of Congress ministries (**October 1939**).
- **Sergeant Plan (1944)** — **Central Advisory Board of Education**; free compulsory education for **6–11**; abolish intermediate; adult illiteracy gone in **20 years**; England's level in **40 years**.
- **William Adam's reports (1835, 1836, 1838)** on vernacular education in **Bengal and Bihar**.
- **Lord Dalhousie's minute (1853)** favoured vernacular education; vernacular schools grew **more than five-fold** in **1854–71**.
- **Roorkee** engineering college **1847**; **Calcutta College of Engineering 1856**; **Poona College of Engineering** (1858), affiliated to Bombay University.
- Medical training began with a **medical college in Calcutta (1835)**; by 1857 three medical colleges — **Calcutta, Bombay, Madras**.
- **Curzon** set up the **agriculture college at Pusa**, parent of similar institutions in other provinces.
- After **1844**, knowledge of **English** was required for government employment.
