## At a glance: five stages of British policy

| Stage | Years | Known for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Struggle for equality from subordination | 1740–1765 | Arcot, Plassey and the Diwani |
| Ring fence | 1765–1813 | Buffer zones |
| Subordinate isolation | 1813–1857 | Theory of paramountcy |
| Subordinate union | 1857–1935 | Punish or depose but not annex |
| Equal federation | 1935–1947 | A non-starter |

## Background: the states

- The princely states were also called the Indian states. They numbered no fewer than 562.
- They covered 7,12,508 square miles.
- A very small state was Bilbari, with a population of 27.
- Hyderabad was as large as Italy. It had 14 million people.
- The Company took the fertile coasts and river valleys. The states were generally "the inaccessible and less fertile tracts".

## British policy: stage by stage

### Struggle for equality from subordination (1740–1765)

1. **1751:** The Company captured Arcot.
2. **1757:** The Battle of Plassey was fought.
3. **1765:** The Company got the Diwani (the right to collect revenue) of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa.

### Ring fence (1765–1813)

- The policy was seen in Warren Hastings' wars against the Marathas and Mysore.
- It created buffer zones.
- The Company organised the defence of Awadh. The aim was to safeguard Bengal.
- **Subsidiary alliance:** Wellesley's subsidiary alliance was an extension of the ring fence. Hyderabad, Awadh and the Marathas accepted it.

### Subordinate isolation (1813–1857)

- **Paramountcy:** The theory of paramountcy (British supremacy over the states) came in this stage.
- **Sovereignty:** The states gave up external sovereignty. They kept internal sovereignty.
- **Residents:** Residents were British officers posted at the rulers' courts. They became executive and controlling officers.
- **1833:** The Charter Act ended the commercial functions of the Company. Its political functions were retained.
- **1834:** The Board of Directors issued guidelines to annex states whenever possible.
- **Dalhousie:** The policy culminated under Dalhousie. He usurped eight states, including Satara and Nagpur.

### Subordinate union (1857–1935)

- **New policy:** Annexation was dropped because the princes had been loyal in 1857. The new policy was to punish or depose but not annex.
- **Kaiser-i-Hind:** The Queen's title was 'Kaiser-i-Hind'. The rulers now inherited the gaddi (throne) as a gift from the paramount power.
- **Curzon:** He followed a policy of patronage and 'intrusive surveillance'. Relations were neither feudal nor federal.
- **1921:** The Chamber of Princes (Narendra Mandal) was set up under the Montford Reforms. It was only a consultative and advisory body.
- **1927:** The Butler Committee gave its view on paramountcy. Its points are listed below.

Representation in the Chamber of Princes:

- 109 states were directly represented.
- 127 states were represented through representatives.
- The rest were treated as feudal holdings or jagirs.

What the Butler Committee (1927) said:

1. Paramountcy must remain supreme.
2. The states should not be handed to an Indian government responsible to an Indian legislature without their consent.
3. For international relations, state territory was British territory and state subjects were British subjects.
4. It left paramountcy undefined.

### Equal federation (1935–1947)

- **1935:** The Government of India Act offered the princes 125 of 375 seats in the Federal Assembly. It offered them 104 of 160 seats in the Council of States.
- **September 1939:** World War II began. The scheme was dropped after this. The federation never came into being.
- **Cripps:** He said the British did not plan to transfer the paramountcy of the Crown to any party in India.
- **Mountbatten:** He refused sovereign status to the states.

## Integration of the states

### Accession

- Sardar Patel held the States Ministry. His secretary was V.P. Menon.
- They got the rulers to accede on three subjects: defence, communication and external affairs.
- **15 August 1947:** 136 states had joined by this date.

### Junagarh

- The Nawab was a Muslim. The population had a Hindu majority.
- A plebiscite (a direct vote of the people) favoured India. Junagarh then joined India.

### Hyderabad

1. **November 1947:** Hyderabad signed a Standstill Agreement.
2. The Razakkars are linked with Hyderabad in this period.
3. The Nizam wanted an outlet to the sea, at Goa.
4. **1948:** A 'police action' was taken against the state.
5. **November 1949:** Hyderabad acceded.

### Kashmir

1. **October 1947:** The ruler signed the Instrument of Accession. Sheikh Abdullah endorsed it.
2. The ceasefire left 84,000 sq km under Pakistani occupation.
3. Article 370 gave the state special status.

⚠ May be outdated: the special status of Jammu and Kashmir under Article 370. The book describes it as current. It may have changed since the book was written.

### Reorganising the states

| Group | Number of states | Details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Merged into provinces (Part A) | 216 small states | For example, 39 states of Orissa and Chhattisgarh went into Central Provinces and Orissa. The Gujarat states went into Bombay. |
| Part C, centrally administered | 61 states | Himachal Pradesh, Vindhya Pradesh, Manipur, Tripura, Bhopal and others |
| Five unions | — | United States of Kathiawar, United States of Matsya, Patiala and East Punjab States Union (PEPSU), Rajasthan, United States of Travancore-Cochin (later Kerala) |

- **Five unions and Mysore:** The jurisdiction of the Union over them was subject to Article 238. The Union was to supervise them for ten years.
- **1956:** The Seventh Amendment abolished the Part B states as a class.
