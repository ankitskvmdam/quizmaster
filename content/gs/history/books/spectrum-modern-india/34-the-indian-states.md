## Gist
*Book pages 603–609.*

The princely (Indian) states numbered **no fewer than 562** and covered **7,12,508 square miles**. They ranged from **Bilbari (population 27)** to **Hyderabad**, as large as **Italy**, with **14 million** people. The Company took the fertile coasts and river valleys; the states were generally "the inaccessible and less fertile tracts".

British relations with the states passed through stages:

- **Struggle for equality from subordination (1740–1765):** capture of **Arcot (1751)**, **Plassey (1757)**, and the **Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa (1765)**.
- **Ring fence (1765–1813):** seen in **Warren Hastings'** wars against the Marathas and Mysore, creating **buffer zones**; the Company organised **Awadh's** defence to secure Bengal. **Wellesley's subsidiary alliance** extended it; **Hyderabad, Awadh and the Marathas** accepted.
- **Subordinate isolation (1813–1857):** the theory of **paramountcy**; states gave up external sovereignty but kept internal sovereignty; **Residents** became controlling officers. The **Charter Act of 1833** ended the Company's commercial functions; the **1834** directors' guidelines urged annexation, culminating in **Dalhousie's** takeover of **eight states** including **Satara and Nagpur**.
- **Subordinate union (1857–1935):** annexation was dropped — **punish or depose but not annex** — because of the princes' loyalty in 1857. With the Queen as **'Kaiser-i-Hind'**, rulers held the gaddi as a **gift of the paramount power**. **Curzon** followed **patronage and 'intrusive surveillance'**. The **Chamber of Princes (Narendra Mandal)**, set up under the **Montford Reforms (1921)**, was only advisory (**109** states directly represented, **127** through representatives). The **Butler Committee (1927)** left paramountcy **undefined** and said states could not be handed to an Indian government **without their consent**.
- **Equal federation (1935–1947), a non-starter:** the **1935 Act** offered princes **125 of 375** Federal Assembly seats and **104 of 160** Council of States seats; the scheme was dropped after **World War II began (September 1939)**.

**Integration:** **Sardar Patel** (States Ministry), aided by secretary **V.P. Menon**, got rulers to accede on **defence, communication and external affairs**; **136 states** had joined by **August 15, 1947**. **Junagarh** joined India after a **plebiscite**. **Hyderabad** signed a **Standstill Agreement (November 1947)**, wanted an outlet to the sea (**Goa**), faced a **'police action' (1948)** and acceded in **November 1949**. **Kashmir's** ruler signed the **Instrument of Accession (October 1947)**, endorsed by **Sheikh Abdullah**; the ceasefire left **84,000 sq km** under Pakistani occupation, and **Article 370** gave the state special status.

Then **216** small states merged into provinces (**Part A**), **61** became centrally administered **Part C** states (Himachal Pradesh, Vindhya Pradesh, Manipur, Tripura, Bhopal), and **five unions** were formed (Kathiawar, Matsya, **PEPSU**, Rajasthan, **Travancore-Cochin**). The **Seventh Amendment (1956)** abolished **Part B** states as a class.

## Key facts
- Princely states: **no fewer than 562**, covering **7,12,508 sq miles**; smallest example **Bilbari (27 persons)**; **Hyderabad** — as large as **Italy**, **14 million** people.
- Stage I (**1740–1765**): Company's capture of **Arcot (1751)**; **Plassey (1757)**; **Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa (1765)**.
- **Ring fence (1765–1813)** — **Warren Hastings'** wars against the Marathas and Mysore; **buffer zones**; Company organised **Awadh's** defence to safeguard Bengal.
- **Wellesley's subsidiary alliance** — an extension of ring fence; accepted by **Hyderabad, Awadh and the Marathas**.
- **Subordinate isolation (1813–1857)** — theory of **paramountcy**; states kept **internal** but lost **external** sovereignty; Residents became executive and controlling officers.
- **Charter Act, 1833** — ended the Company's **commercial** functions; political functions retained.
- **1834** — Board of Directors' guidelines to annex states whenever possible; **Dalhousie** usurped **eight states**, including **Satara and Nagpur**.
- **Subordinate union (1857–1935)** — policy to **punish or depose but not annex**; Queen's title **'Kaiser-i-Hind'**; rulers inherited the gaddi as a **gift from the paramount power**.
- Butler (1927): for international relations, **state territory = British territory** and **state subjects = British subjects**.
- **Curzon** — policy of **patronage and 'intrusive surveillance'**; relations **neither feudal nor federal**.
- **Chamber of Princes (Narendra Mandal)** — **Montford Reforms (1921)**; consultative/advisory only; **109** states directly represented, **127** through representatives, the rest as feudal holdings or jagirs.
- **Butler Committee (1927)** — paramountcy to remain supreme; states not to be handed to an Indian government responsible to an Indian legislature **without their consent**; paramountcy left **undefined**.
- **Government of India Act, 1935** — princes' share: **125 of 375** Federal Assembly seats and **104 of 160** Council of States seats; federation never came into being, dropped after **September 1939**.
- **Cripps** — the British did not plan to transfer the Crown's paramountcy to any party in India; **Mountbatten** refused sovereign status to the states.
- **Sardar Patel** (States Ministry) and secretary **V.P. Menon** — accession on **defence, communication, external affairs**; **136 states** joined by **August 15, 1947**.
- **Junagarh** — Muslim Nawab, Hindu-majority population; **plebiscite** favoured India.
- **Hyderabad** — **Standstill Agreement (November 1947)**; **Razakkars**; Nizam wanted an outlet to the sea (**Goa**); **'police action' (1948)**; acceded **November 1949**.
- **Kashmir** — **Instrument of Accession (October 1947)**, endorsed by **Sheikh Abdullah**; ceasefire left **84,000 sq km** under Pakistani occupation; special status under **Article 370**.
- ⚠ May be outdated: special status of Jammu and Kashmir under **Article 370** — the book describes it as current; it may have changed since the book was written.
- **216** small states merged into provinces (**Part A**), e.g. **39 states of Orissa and Chhattisgarh** into Central Provinces and Orissa; **Gujarat states into Bombay**.
- **Part C** (centrally administered): **61 states** — Himachal Pradesh, Vindhya Pradesh, Manipur, Tripura, Bhopal, etc.
- **Five unions**: **United States of Kathiawar, United States of Matsya, PEPSU, Rajasthan, United States of Travancore-Cochin** (later Kerala).
- Five unions and Mysore: Union jurisdiction subject to **Article 238** and Union supervision for **ten years**.
- **Seventh Amendment (1956)** — abolished **Part-B** states as a class.
