## Gist
*Book pages 257–281.*

A militant trend emerged in the **1890s** and took concrete shape by **1905**. Causes: the exploitative nature of British rule (famines killed **90 lakh between 1896 and 1900**; plague in the Deccan; Natu brothers deported **1897**; **IPC 156A (1898)**; **Official Secrets Act and Indian Universities Act (1904)**); growing self-confidence urged by **Tilak, Aurobindo and Bipin Chandra Pal**; educated unemployment; international events — **Japan's rise after 1868**, **Ethiopia's defeat of Italy (1896)**, the **Boer wars (1899–1902)**, **Japan's victory over Russia (1905)**; reaction to westernisation (**Dayananda: 'India for the Indians'**); scorn for the Moderates' **'Three P's' — prayer, petition, protest — as 'political mendicancy'**; and **Curzon's** reactionary rule, above all the **partition of Bengal**.

The partition, made public in **December 1903**, created **Bengal (western Bengal plus Bihar and Orissa)** with capital **Calcutta**, and **Eastern Bengal and Assam** with capital **Dacca**. The official reason was Bengal's size (**78 million**, a quarter of British India); the real aim was to weaken Bengal, the **nerve centre of nationalism**, by dividing Bengalis by **language** and **religion**. The Moderate-led campaign (**1903–05**: Surendranath Banerjea, K.K. Mitra, Prithwishchandra Ray) used petitions and papers like **Hitabadi, Sanjibani, Bengalee**. Partition was announced in **July 1905**; the **Boycott Resolution** at the **Calcutta Townhall on 7 August 1905** formally proclaimed the **Swadeshi Movement**. On **16 October 1905** partition came into force — a day of mourning with **Bande Mataram**, Tagore's **'Amar Sonar Bangla'** and **rakhis**.

Under the Extremists the movement added **passive resistance**, **samitis** (**Swadesh Bandhab Samiti, Ashwini Kumar Dutta, Barisal**), festivals, **atma shakti**, national education (**Bengal National College** under **Aurobindo**; **National Council of Education, 15 August 1906**) and swadeshi enterprise (**V.O. Chidambaram Pillai's Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company, Tuticorin**). Students, women and workers joined, but most Muslims stayed away; the **Muslim League** was floated with **Nawab Salimullah of Dacca**.

Congress sessions: **Benaras 1905 (Gokhale)**; **Calcutta 1906 (Dadabhai Naoroji)** declared the goal **'swarajya or self-government'** — the word swaraj used for the first time; **Surat (December 1907)** split the Congress after the Moderates chose Surat to keep **Tilak** out and proposed **Rashbehari Ghosh**. Repression followed: the **Seditious Meetings Act (1907)** and other laws up to the **Indian Press Act (1910)**. **Tilak** was tried in **1909** for his **Kesari** writing on the **Muzaffarpur bomb** (Bihar) and sent to **Mandalay** for six years. The movement fizzled out by **1908** but was a **"leap forward"**. Partition was **annulled in 1911**, mainly to curb revolutionary terrorism; **Bihar and Orissa were taken out of Bengal**, Assam made separate, and the capital moved to **Delhi**.

The government's **'carrot and stick'** (**John Morley's 'rallying them'**) led to the **Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act 1909)**, after the **Simla Deputation (October 1906, Agha Khan)**: **separate electorates for Muslims**, indirect elections, budget items votable, and **Satyendra Sinha** as the first Indian on the viceroy's executive council — "a shadow rather than substance".

## Key facts
- Militant trend emerged in the **1890s**, took concrete shape by **1905**.
- Famines killed **90 lakh persons between 1896 and 1900**; **bubonic plague** hit the Deccan.
- **1897**: Natu brothers deported without trial; Tilak imprisoned for sedition. **1898**: **IPC Section 124A** amplified by **Section 156A**. **1899**: Indian members of the **Calcutta Corporation** reduced. **1904**: **Official Secrets Act** and **Indian Universities Act**.
- International influences: **Japan's progress after 1868**; **Ethiopians defeated the Italian army (1896)**; **Boer wars (1899–1902)**; **Japan's victory over Russia (1905)**.
- **Dayananda Saraswati's** political message: **'India for the Indians'**.
- Moderate methods — **'Three P's': prayer, petition and protest** — derided as **'political mendicancy'**.
- Curzon described nationalist activities as **"letting off of gas"**; his rule was "full of missions, commissions and omissions".
- Militant school: **Raj Narain Bose, Ashwini Kumar Datta, Aurobindo Ghosh, Bipin Chandra Pal** (Bengal); **Vishnu Shastri Chiplunkar, Tilak** (Maharashtra); **Lala Lajpat Rai** (Punjab); **Tilak** the most outstanding.
- Partition plan made public **December 1903**; announced **July 1905**; came into force **16 October 1905**.
- New **Bengal** = **western Bengal + Bihar and Orissa** (capital **Calcutta**); **Eastern Bengal and Assam** (capital **Dacca**).
- Bengal's population **78 million** (about **a quarter** of British India); Bengal proper to have **17 million Bengalis** and **37 million Hindi and Oriya speakers**.
- Western half: Hindu majority (**42 of 54 million**); eastern half: Muslim majority (**18 of 31 million**).
- **Risley** (home secretary, 1904): "**Bengal united is a power. Bengal divided will pull in several different ways.**"
- Moderate anti-partition campaign (**1903–05**): **Surendranath Banerjea, K.K. Mitra, Prithwishchandra Ray**; papers **Hitabadi, Sanjibani, Bengalee**.
- **Boycott Resolution**, **Calcutta Townhall, 7 August 1905** — formal proclamation of the **Swadeshi Movement**; boycott of **Manchester cloth and Liverpool salt**.
- **'Amar Sonar Bangla'** by **Rabindranath Tagore** — later national anthem of **Bangladesh**; **Bande Mataram** became the theme song.
- Spread: **Poona and Bombay — Tilak**; **Punjab — Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh**; **Delhi — Syed Haider Raza**; **Madras — Chidambaram Pillai**.
- Congress sessions: **Benaras 1905 — Gokhale**; **Calcutta 1906 — Dadabhai Naoroji** (goal **'swarajya or self-government'**, word swaraj first mentioned); **Surat 1907 — split**.
- **Swadesh Bandhab Samiti** — **Ashwini Kumar Dutta, Barisal**; **Swadeshi Sangam** — **V.O. Chidambaram Pillai and Subramania Siva, Tirunelveli**.
- **Bengal National College** — principal **Aurobindo Ghosh**; **National Council of Education — 15 August 1906**; **Bengal Institute of Technology**.
- **Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company, Tuticorin** — **V.O. Chidambaram Pillai**.
- **Sudesha Geetham** — **Subramania Bharati**; **Nandalal Bose** — first scholarship of the **Indian Society of Oriental Art (founded 1907)**.
- Labour: **Burn Company, Howrah** clerks' walkout (**September 1905**); **East Indian Railway** strike (**July 1906**) led to a **Railwaymen's Union**; **Rawalpindi** strike led by **Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh**.
- Muslims: **Nawab Salimullah of Dacca** supported partition; the **Muslim League** was floated by him with **Mohsin-ul-Mulk and Waqar-ul-Mulk** (book gives both December 1905 and December 1906).
- Partition **annulled in 1911**, mainly to curb **revolutionary terrorism**; capital shifted to **Delhi**; **Bihar and Orissa taken out of Bengal**; **Assam** made a separate province.
- Swadeshi's open phase was almost over by **1908**; it was a **"leap forward"** and a turning point.
- Moderates: social base **zamindars and upper middle classes**, inspired by **western liberal thought**, believed in England's **providential mission**; Extremists: **educated middle and lower middle classes**, inspired by **Indian history and Hindu symbols**, used **boycott and passive resistance**.
- For **Tilak** swaraj meant some sort of **self-government**; for **Aurobindo** **complete independence**.
- **Age of Consent Bill** (marriageable age for girls **10 to 12**) — opposed by **Tilak**.
- Surat: Moderates chose **Surat** to exclude **Tilak** (host-province rule; Surat in Bombay province) and wanted **Rashbehari Ghosh** as president; Extremists wanted **Nagpur**.
- Repressive laws (1907–1911): **Seditious Meetings Act 1907**, **Indian Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act 1908**, **Criminal Law Amendment Act 1908**, **Explosive Substances Act 1908**, **Indian Press Act 1910**.
- Tilak tried in **1909** for **Kesari** articles on the **Muzaffarpur bomb** (two European women killed); **six years' transportation, fine Rs. 1,000**, **Mandalay jail**; released **1914**.
- Government strategy: **'rallying them' (John Morley)** / **'carrot and stick'** — **repression-conciliation-suppression**, to isolate the Extremists.
- **Simla Deputation (October 1906)**, led by the **Agha Khan**, met **Lord Minto** to demand **separate electorates** for Muslims.
- **Morley-Minto Reforms = Indian Councils Act 1909**: **separate electorates for Muslims** (first time), **indirect elections**, non-official majority in provincial councils, separate budget items could be voted, supplementaries allowed.
- Sumit Sarkar: Imperial council **69 members — 37 officials, 32 non-officials (5 nominated, 27 elected)**; elected seats: **8 Muslims, 4 British capitalists, 2 landlords, 13 general**.
- Muslim voters had a **lower income qualification** than Hindus.
- **Satyendra Sinha** — **first Indian on the viceroy's executive council (1909)**.
- 1909 reforms gave **"a shadow rather than substance"** — **'benevolent despotism'**; Morley rejected a parliamentary system for India.
