## Why militant nationalism grew

A militant trend emerged in the 1890s. It took concrete shape by 1905. It had seven causes.

1. **Exploitative British rule:** People saw the exploitative nature of British rule. The events that showed it are listed below.
2. **Growing self-confidence:** Tilak, Aurobindo and Bipin Chandra Pal urged Indians to have confidence in themselves.
3. **Educated unemployment:** Educated Indians could not find work.
4. **International events:** Events abroad also influenced Indians. They are listed below.
5. **Reaction to westernisation:** There was a reaction against westernisation. The political message of Dayananda Saraswati was 'India for the Indians'.
6. **Scorn for the Moderates' methods:** The methods of the Moderates were called the 'Three P's': prayer, petition and protest. They were derided as 'political mendicancy', that is, political begging.
7. **Curzon's reactionary rule:** Curzon described nationalist activities as "letting off of gas". His rule was "full of missions, commissions and omissions". Above all, he partitioned Bengal.

### What British rule did

1. **1896 to 1900:** Famines killed 90 lakh persons between 1896 and 1900. Bubonic plague hit the Deccan.
2. **1897:** The Natu brothers were deported without trial. Tilak was imprisoned for sedition.
3. **1898:** Section 124A of the IPC was amplified by Section 156A.
4. **1899:** The number of Indian members of the Calcutta Corporation was reduced.
5. **1904:** The Official Secrets Act and the Indian Universities Act were passed.

### International events

1. **After 1868:** Japan made progress and rose.
2. **1896:** The Ethiopians defeated the Italian army.
3. **1899–1902:** The Boer wars were fought.
4. **1905:** Japan won a victory over Russia.

## The partition of Bengal

### The plan

The partition plan was made public in December 1903. It made two provinces.

| Province | Made up of | Capital |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bengal | Western Bengal, Bihar and Orissa | Calcutta |
| Eastern Bengal and Assam | Eastern Bengal and Assam | Dacca |

### The official reason and the real aim

- **Official reason:** Bengal was too big. It had 78 million people, about a quarter of the population of British India.
- **Real aim:** The government wanted to weaken Bengal, the nerve centre of nationalism. It did this by dividing the Bengalis in two ways, by language and by religion.
- **Division by language:** Bengal proper was to have 17 million Bengalis and 37 million Hindi and Oriya speakers.
- **Division by religion:** The western half had a Hindu majority, 42 of 54 million. The eastern half had a Muslim majority, 18 of 31 million.
- **Risley, the home secretary, in 1904:** "Bengal united is a power. Bengal divided will pull in several different ways."

### Dates of the partition

1. **December 1903:** The plan was made public.
2. **July 1905:** The partition was announced.
3. **16 October 1905:** The partition came into force.
4. **1911:** The partition was annulled.

## The anti-partition campaign under the Moderates (1903–05)

- **Leaders:** Surendranath Banerjea, K.K. Mitra and Prithwishchandra Ray.
- **Methods:** The campaign used petitions and newspapers.
- **Newspapers:** Hitabadi, Sanjibani and Bengalee.

## The Swadeshi and Boycott Movement under the Extremists

### How it began

1. **7 August 1905:** The Boycott Resolution was passed at the Calcutta Townhall. This was the formal proclamation of the Swadeshi Movement.
2. **16 October 1905:** The day the partition came into force was kept as a day of mourning. People sang Bande Mataram and 'Amar Sonar Bangla'. They tied rakhis (threads tied on the wrist) on one another.

Two songs belong to this day:

- **Bande Mataram:** It became the theme song of the movement.
- **'Amar Sonar Bangla':** Rabindranath Tagore wrote it. It later became the national anthem of Bangladesh.

### Methods

- **Boycott:** Manchester cloth and Liverpool salt were boycotted.
- **Passive resistance:** Under the Extremists the movement added passive resistance.
- **Samitis:** Samitis were volunteer groups. The Swadesh Bandhab Samiti of Ashwini Kumar Dutta worked at Barisal. The Swadeshi Sangam of V.O. Chidambaram Pillai and Subramania Siva worked at Tirunelveli.
- **Festivals:** Festivals were used in the movement.
- **Atma shakti:** The movement stressed atma shakti, that is, self-reliance.

### National education

- **Bengal National College:** Aurobindo Ghosh was its principal.
- **15 August 1906:** The National Council of Education was set up.
- **Bengal Institute of Technology:** It was part of national education.

### Swadeshi enterprise and culture

- **Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company:** V.O. Chidambaram Pillai started it at Tuticorin.
- **Sudesha Geetham:** Subramania Bharati wrote it.
- **Nandalal Bose:** He won the first scholarship of the Indian Society of Oriental Art. The society was founded in 1907.

### Who took part

Students, women and workers joined the movement. Most Muslims stayed away.

Workers:

1. **September 1905:** The clerks of the Burn Company at Howrah walked out.
2. **July 1906:** A strike on the East Indian Railway led to a Railwaymen's Union.
3. A strike at Rawalpindi was led by Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh.

Muslims:

- Nawab Salimullah of Dacca supported the partition.
- The Muslim League was floated by him with Mohsin-ul-Mulk and Waqar-ul-Mulk. Book inconsistency: the book gives both December 1905 and December 1906 for this. Verify.

### How it ended

The movement fizzled out by 1908. Its open phase was almost over by then. Still, it was a "leap forward" and a turning point.

## Moderates and Extremists

| Point | Moderates | Extremists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social base | Zamindars and upper middle classes | Educated middle and lower middle classes |
| Inspired by | Western liberal thought | Indian history and Hindu symbols |
| Belief and method | Believed in England's providential mission | Used boycott and passive resistance |

- **Meaning of swaraj:** For Tilak swaraj meant some sort of self-government. For Aurobindo it meant complete independence.
- **Age of Consent Bill:** It raised the marriageable age for girls from 10 to 12. Tilak opposed it.

## Congress sessions and the Surat split

| Session | President | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Benaras 1905 | Gokhale | — |
| Calcutta 1906 | Dadabhai Naoroji | The goal was declared to be 'swarajya or self-government'. The word swaraj was mentioned for the first time. |
| Surat, December 1907 | Rashbehari Ghosh was the Moderates' choice | The Congress split. |

How the split came about:

1. The Extremists wanted the session at Nagpur.
2. The Moderates chose Surat to exclude Tilak. Under the host-province rule, a leader of the host province could not be president. Surat was in Bombay province, so the rule kept Tilak out.
3. The Moderates wanted Rashbehari Ghosh as president.
4. **December 1907:** The Congress split at Surat.

## The government's response

### The strategy

- **Names:** John Morley called the strategy 'rallying them'. It is also called 'carrot and stick'.
- **Three steps:** repression, conciliation and suppression.
- **Aim:** to isolate the Extremists.

### Repressive laws (1907–1911)

1. **1907:** Seditious Meetings Act.
2. **1908:** Indian Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act.
3. **1908:** Criminal Law Amendment Act.
4. **1908:** Explosive Substances Act.
5. **1910:** Indian Press Act.

### The trial of Tilak

1. **1909:** Tilak was tried for his articles in Kesari on the Muzaffarpur bomb. Muzaffarpur is in Bihar. The bomb had killed two European women.
2. He was sentenced to six years' transportation and a fine of Rs 1,000. He was sent to Mandalay jail.
3. **1914:** He was released.

### Partition annulled (1911)

- The partition was annulled mainly to curb revolutionary terrorism.
- Bihar and Orissa were taken out of Bengal.
- Assam was made a separate province.
- The capital was shifted to Delhi.

## The Morley-Minto Reforms (1909)

The Morley-Minto Reforms are the Indian Councils Act 1909.

### The Simla Deputation

- **October 1906:** The Simla Deputation met Lord Minto. The Agha Khan led it. It demanded separate electorates for Muslims, in which only Muslims would vote for Muslim members.

### Main provisions

1. Separate electorates were given to Muslims. This was the first time.
2. Elections were indirect.
3. The provincial councils got a non-official majority.
4. Separate items of the budget could be voted on.
5. Supplementaries (follow-up questions) were allowed.
6. Muslim voters had a lower income qualification than Hindus.
7. **1909:** Satyendra Sinha became the first Indian on the viceroy's executive council.

### The Imperial council

Sumit Sarkar gives the make-up of the Imperial council. It had 69 members.

| Members | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Officials | 37 |
| Non-officials | 32 (5 nominated, 27 elected) |

| Elected seats | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Muslims | 8 |
| British capitalists | 4 |
| Landlords | 2 |
| General | 13 |

### Assessment

- The reforms of 1909 gave "a shadow rather than substance".
- They are described as 'benevolent despotism'.
- Morley rejected a parliamentary system for India.
