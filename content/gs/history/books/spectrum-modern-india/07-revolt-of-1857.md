## Gist
*Book pages 167–187.*

The Revolt of 1857 grew out of a century of Company rule after **Plassey (1757)**. **Economic causes**: heavy revenue demands, money-lenders taking peasants' land, ruined handicrafts and one-way free trade. In **Awadh**, the storm centre, **21,000 taluqdars** had their estates confiscated. **Political causes**: annexation through **Subsidiary Alliance** and the **Doctrine of Lapse**. **Socio-religious causes**: missionaries, the tax on mosque and temple lands, and the **Religious Disabilities Act, 1856**. **Outside events**: the First Afghan War, the Punjab Wars and the **Crimean War (1854–56)**. **Sepoy grievances**: **Lord Canning's General Service Enlistment Act (1856)** and the loss of **foreign service allowance (bhatta)**. The sepoy was a **'peasant in uniform'**.

The **spark** was the **Enfield rifle**, whose greased cartridges were said to contain beef and pig fat. The **19th N.I. at Berhampore** mutinied in February 1857. **Mangal Pande** of the **34th N.I.** fired at his sergeant major at **Barrackpore** and was executed on **April 8**. At **Meerut** (58 km from Delhi), 85 men of the **3rd Native Cavalry** were jailed. On **May 10, 1857** their comrades rose and marched to Delhi. There **Bahadur Shah Zafar** was proclaimed **Emperor of India**, and the mutiny became a revolutionary war. Civilians joined in large numbers: in Awadh, **over 1,00,000 of about 1,50,000** who died fighting were civilians.

**Leaders**: Delhi, **General Bakht Khan** (real command); Kanpur, **Nana Saheb**; Lucknow, **Begum Hazrat Mahal** (son **Birjis Qadir** made nawab); Bareilly, **Khan Bahadur**; Faizabad, **Maulvi Ahmadullah**; Jhansi, **Rani Laxmibai**; Baghpat, **Shah Mal**. In **Bihar**, **Kunwar Singh**, **zamindar of Jagdishpur**, was in his seventies and angry at losing his estates. He joined the sepoys when they reached **Arrah from Dinapore (Danapur)**.

**Suppression**: Delhi fell on **September 20, 1857**; **John Nicholson** died of his wounds and **Lt. Hudson** shot the princes. Bahadur Shah was exiled to **Rangoon** and died there in **1862**. **Sir Colin Campbell** took Kanpur (**December 6, 1857**), and Lucknow was recovered in **March 1858**. Laxmibai died in battle in **June 1858** and **Sir Hugh Rose** recaptured Jhansi. **Tantia Tope** was captured in **April 1859** and Nana Saheb fled to **Nepal**. By 1859 **Kunwar Singh** was dead, and **Colonel Neill** crushed Benaras.

**Why it failed**: it was limited in area (the east, south and west were largely unaffected) and many rulers stayed loyal (Sindhia, Holkar, Patiala, Kashmir). Arms were poor, with no coordination or common ideology. **Hindu–Muslim unity** was complete.

**Nature**: **Seeley**, a 'sepoy mutiny'; **K. Datta**, mainly a military outbreak; **Savarkar**, the **first war of Indian independence**; **S.N. Sen**, it began as a fight for religion and ended as a war of independence; **R.C. Majumdar**, 'neither First, nor National, nor War of Independence'.

**Consequences**: the **Act for the Better Government of India (August 2, 1858)** ended Company rule and created a **Secretary of State for India**. **Canning** announced the **Queen's Proclamation** at **Allahabad (November 1, 1858)**, and the Governor-General became **'Viceroy'**. The army was reorganised on **'division and counterpoise'** (Army Amalgamation Scheme **1861**). Other effects: the **Indian Civil Service Act, 1861**, **divide and rule**, the **White Mutiny** and lasting racial hatred.

## Key facts
- In **Awadh**, the storm centre of the revolt, **21,000 taluqdars** had their estates confiscated.
- After **Prince Faqiruddin's** death (**1856**), **Lord Canning** said the next prince must renounce the regal title and ancestral Mughal palaces.
- **Religious Disabilities Act, 1856** — a change of religion did not debar a son from inheriting his 'heathen' father's property.
- Outside events: **First Afghan War (1838–42)**, **Punjab Wars (1845–49)**, **Crimean War (1854–56)**.
- **General Service Enlistment Act (1856)** — **Lord Canning**; Bengal Army recruits had to serve anywhere.
- The sepoy was a **'peasant in uniform'**; "The Army voiced grievances other than its own" — **S. Gopal**.
- Spark: greased cartridges of the **Enfield rifle** (beef and pig fat) and rumours of **bone dust in atta**.
- **19th N.I., Berhampore** — refused the Enfield rifle; mutinied **February 1857**, disbanded **March 1857**.
- **Mangal Pande**, **34th N.I.**, **Barrackpore** — executed **April 8, 1857**.
- **3rd Native Cavalry, Meerut** — 90 refused cartridges (**April 24**); **85 sentenced to 10 years** (**May 9**); revolt began **May 10, 1857**; Meerut is **58 km from Delhi**.
- At Delhi the rebels killed political agent **Simon Fraser**; **Lt. Willoughby** (magazine) resisted.
- **Bahadur Shah Zafar** — proclaimed **Emperor of India**; symbolic head; called the **weakest link** of the leadership.
- Delhi: real command with **General Bakht Khan**, who headed a **court of ten** (**six army, four civilian**).
- Awadh: of about **1,50,000** who died fighting, **over 1,00,000 were civilians**.
- **Nana Saheb** — adopted son of **Baji Rao II**; **Sir Hugh Wheeler** surrendered at Kanpur on **June 27, 1857**; aide **Azimullah**.
- Lucknow rising **June 4, 1857**; **Begum Hazrat Mahal**; **Birjis Qadir** proclaimed nawab; **Henry Lawrence** killed; **Brigadier Inglis** held the residency; **Colin Campbell** evacuated Europeans with **Gorkha regiments**; city recovered **March 1858**.
- Bareilly — **Khan Bahadur**, descendant of the former ruler of Rohilkhand; army of **40,000**.
- **Bihar — Kunwar Singh, zamindar of Jagdishpur**, in his **seventies**, deprived of his estates; joined the sepoys at **Arrah** who came from **Dinapore (Danapur)**; dead by **1859**.
- **Maulvi Ahmadullah of Faizabad** — a native of **Madras**.
- **Rani Laxmibai** — Jhansi annexed under the **Doctrine of Lapse**; "main apni Jhansi nahin doongi"; died in battle **June 1858**; Jhansi recaptured by **Sir Hugh Rose**.
- "Here lay the woman who was the only man among the rebels" — **Hugh Rose** on the Rani.
- **Gwalior** — the **Sindhia** sided with the English; Gwalior recaptured **June 1858**.
- **Shah Mal** — **Pargana Baraut (Baghpat)**; organised **84 villages (chaurasi desh)**; killed by **Dunlap**, July 1857.
- Delhi captured **September 20, 1857**; **John Nicholson** died of wounds; princes shot by **Lt. Hudson**; Bahadur Shah exiled to **Rangoon**, died **1862**.
- **Colin Campbell** occupied Kanpur **December 6, 1857**; **Nana Saheb** escaped to **Nepal** (early 1859); **Tantia Tope** captured **April 1859** and executed.
- Benaras rebellion suppressed by **Colonel (James) Neill**.
- Rulers who did not join: **Sindhia of Gwalior, Holkar of Indore, Patiala, Sindh, other Sikh chiefs, Maharaja of Kashmir**.
- The revolt affected not more than **one-fourth of the area** and **one-tenth of the population**.
- **Maulana Azad** stressed **Hindu–Muslim unity** and loyalty to the Mughal Crown; **cow slaughter was banned** where the rebels won.
- **Sir John Seeley** — "wholly unpatriotic and selfish **Sepoy Mutiny**".
- **Dr K. Datta** — "in the main a **military outbreak**" exploited by discontented princes and landlords.
- **V.D. Savarkar** — *The Indian War of Independence, 1857*; **first war of Indian independence**.
- **Dr S.N. Sen** (*Eighteen Fifty-Seven*) — began as a **fight for religion**, ended as a **war of independence**.
- **R.C. Majumdar** — "neither **First**, nor **National**, nor **War of Independence**".
- **Nehru** — essentially a **feudal uprising** (*Discovery of India*); **M.N. Roy** — **last-ditch stand of feudalism against commercial capitalism**.
- **L.E.R. Rees** — war of fanatic religionists against Christians; **T.R. Holmes** — **civilisation vs barbarism**; **James Outram** — "a Mohammedan conspiracy making capital of Hindu grievances".
- **S.B. Chaudhuri** — "the first combined attempt of many classes of people to challenge a foreign power".
- **Stanley Wolpert** — "far more than a mutiny, ... yet much less than a first war of independence"; **Percival Spear** — passions centred on grievances; **Eric Stokes** — elitist in character.
- **Act for the Better Government of India** — **August 2, 1858**; **Queen Victoria** sovereign; **Secretary of State for India** (member of the British cabinet).
- **Queen's Proclamation** — **Lord Canning**, durbar at **Allahabad**, **November 1, 1858**; Governor-General got the title **'Viceroy'**; ended annexations; promised religious freedom.
- Army policy of **'division and counterpoise'**; recruits from the 'martial' races of **Punjab, Nepal and the north-western frontier**.
- **Army Amalgamation Scheme, 1861** — Company's European troops moved to the Crown; **'linked-battalion' scheme**.
- **Thomas Metcalf** — 'conservative brand of liberalism'; **Indian Civil Service Act, 1861**.
- **White Mutiny** — European Company troops resented the transfer of allegiance to the Queen and the stopping of **batta**; they were given **free release with free passage home**.
- **Bernard Cohn** — by the Proclamation, the British turned from 'outsiders' into **'insiders'**.
