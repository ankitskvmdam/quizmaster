## Why the revolt broke out

The Revolt of 1857 grew out of a century of Company rule after Plassey (1757).

### Economic causes

- Revenue demands were heavy.
- Money-lenders took the peasants' land.
- Handicrafts were ruined.
- Free trade was one-way.
- In Awadh, the storm centre of the revolt, 21,000 taluqdars (big landholders) had their estates confiscated.

### Political causes

- States were annexed through the Subsidiary Alliance and the Doctrine of Lapse.
- **1856:** Prince Faqiruddin died. Lord Canning then said the next prince must renounce the regal title and the ancestral Mughal palaces.

### Socio-religious causes

- The missionaries were one cause.
- Mosque and temple lands were taxed.
- **Religious Disabilities Act, 1856:** A change of religion did not debar a son from inheriting his 'heathen' father's property.

### Outside events

- First Afghan War (1838–42).
- Punjab Wars (1845–49).
- Crimean War (1854–56).

### Sepoy grievances

- **General Service Enlistment Act (1856):** This was Lord Canning's Act. Recruits of the Bengal Army now had to serve anywhere.
- The sepoys lost their foreign service allowance (bhatta).
- The sepoy was a 'peasant in uniform'. S. Gopal wrote: "The Army voiced grievances other than its own."

## The spark and the outbreak

The spark was the Enfield rifle. Its greased cartridges were said to contain beef and pig fat. There were also rumours of bone dust in atta (flour).

1. **February 1857:** The 19th N.I. (Native Infantry) at Berhampore refused the Enfield rifle and mutinied.
2. **March 1857:** The 19th N.I. was disbanded.
3. **8 April 1857:** Mangal Pande of the 34th N.I. was executed. He had fired at his sergeant major at Barrackpore.
4. **24 April:** At Meerut 90 men of the 3rd Native Cavalry refused the cartridges.
5. **9 May:** 85 of them were sentenced to 10 years and jailed.
6. **10 May 1857:** Their comrades rose and marched to Delhi. Meerut is 58 km from Delhi.
7. At Delhi the rebels killed the political agent Simon Fraser. Lt. Willoughby resisted at the magazine (arms store).
8. Bahadur Shah Zafar was proclaimed Emperor of India. The mutiny now became a revolutionary war.

Civilians joined in large numbers. In Awadh about 1,50,000 people died fighting. Over 1,00,000 of them were civilians.

## Centres, leaders and suppression

### Delhi

- Bahadur Shah Zafar was the symbolic head. He has been called the weakest link of the leadership.
- Real command lay with General Bakht Khan. He headed a court of ten: six from the army and four civilian.
- **20 September 1857:** Delhi was captured. John Nicholson died of his wounds. Lt. Hudson shot the princes.
- Bahadur Shah was exiled to Rangoon.
- **1862:** He died there.

### Kanpur

- Nana Saheb led the revolt here. He was the adopted son of Baji Rao II. His aide was Azimullah.
- **27 June 1857:** Sir Hugh Wheeler surrendered at Kanpur.
- **6 December 1857:** Sir Colin Campbell occupied Kanpur.
- **Early 1859:** Nana Saheb escaped to Nepal.
- **April 1859:** Tantia Tope was captured and executed.

### Lucknow

- **4 June 1857:** The rising began. Begum Hazrat Mahal led it. Her son Birjis Qadir was proclaimed nawab.
- Henry Lawrence was killed.
- Brigadier Inglis held the residency.
- Colin Campbell evacuated the Europeans with the help of Gorkha regiments.
- **March 1858:** The city was recovered.

### Bihar

- Kunwar Singh was the zamindar of Jagdishpur. He was in his seventies.
- He had been deprived of his estates and was angry at this.
- He joined the sepoys when they reached Arrah from Dinapore (Danapur).
- **1859:** He was dead by this year.

### Jhansi

- Rani Laxmibai led the revolt here. Jhansi had been annexed under the Doctrine of Lapse.
- Her words were: "main apni Jhansi nahin doongi".
- **June 1858:** She died in battle. Sir Hugh Rose recaptured Jhansi.
- Hugh Rose said of her: "Here lay the woman who was the only man among the rebels."

## Why the revolt failed

1. **Limited area:** The east, south and west were largely unaffected. The revolt affected not more than one-fourth of the area and one-tenth of the population.
2. **Loyal rulers:** Many rulers stayed loyal. Those who did not join were the Sindhia of Gwalior, the Holkar of Indore, Patiala, Sindh, other Sikh chiefs and the Maharaja of Kashmir.
3. **Poor arms:** The rebels' arms were poor.
4. **No coordination:** There was no coordination among them.
5. **No common ideology:** They had no common ideology.

## Nature of the revolt

Hindu–Muslim unity was complete. Maulana Azad stressed this unity and the loyalty to the Mughal Crown. Cow slaughter was banned where the rebels won.

## Consequences

### Government

- **2 August 1858:** The Act for the Better Government of India was passed. It ended Company rule and made Queen Victoria the sovereign. It created a Secretary of State for India, who was a member of the British cabinet.
- **1 November 1858:** Lord Canning announced the Queen's Proclamation at a durbar at Allahabad. The Governor-General got the title 'Viceroy'. The Proclamation ended annexations and promised religious freedom.
- Bernard Cohn said that by the Proclamation the British turned from 'outsiders' into 'insiders'.
- **1861:** The Indian Civil Service Act was passed.
- Thomas Metcalf spoke of a 'conservative brand of liberalism'.

### Army

- The army was reorganised on the policy of 'division and counterpoise'.
- Recruits came from the 'martial' races of Punjab, Nepal and the north-western frontier.
- **1861:** The Army Amalgamation Scheme moved the Company's European troops to the Crown. There was also a 'linked-battalion' scheme.
- **White Mutiny:** The Company's European troops resented the transfer of allegiance to the Queen and the stopping of bhatta. They were given free release with free passage home.

### Other effects

- The British followed divide and rule.
- Racial hatred was lasting.
