## Why the peasants became poor

Peasant impoverishment had four causes:

1. Colonial economic policies.
2. The ruin of handicrafts. This led to overcrowding of land.
3. The new land revenue system.
4. The colonial administrative and judicial system.

The burden differed by area:

- **Zamindari areas:** Peasants faced high rents, illegal levies, arbitrary evictions and unpaid labour.
- **Ryotwari areas:** The government itself levied heavy revenue. This pushed peasants to the moneylenders.

## Early movements (1859–1879)

### Indigo Revolt (1859–60)

- **Area:** Bengal.
- **Cause:** The planters forced peasants to grow indigo. The planters were nearly all Europeans. Their retainers were called lathiyals.
- **Leaders:** Digambar Biswas and Bishnu Biswas of Nadia district.
- **Course:** The peasants refused to grow indigo. They went on rent strike and used the courts. The Bengali intelligentsia backed them.
- **Result:** An indigo commission was set up. After it, a government notification of November 1860 said that ryots (peasants) could not be compelled to grow indigo. By the end of 1860 indigo cultivation was virtually wiped out from Bengal.

### Pabna Agrarian Leagues (1870s–80s)

- **Area:** Eastern Bengal.
- **Cause:** The zamindars enhanced rents. They also blocked occupancy rights under Act X of 1859.
- **Supporters:** Bankim Chandra Chatterjee, R.C. Dutt and the Indian Association under Surendranath Banerjea.
- **Course:** The peasants formed an agrarian league and ran rent strikes. The struggle was mainly legal resistance. There was very little violence.
- **Result:** The Bengal Tenancy Act, 1885.

### Deccan Riots (1874)

- **Area:** The Ryotwari areas of the Deccan.
- **Cause:** The movement was against the moneylenders. These were mostly outsiders, Marwaris or Gujaratis. Three things made matters worse: the crash in cotton prices after the American Civil War, a 50% rise in land revenue in 1867, and bad harvests.
- **Course:** A social boycott began in 1874. It spread to Poona, Ahmednagar, Sholapur and Satara. It turned into riots, and debt bonds were burnt.
- **Result:** The Deccan Agriculturists Relief Act, 1879.

### Nature of the movements after 1857

- The demands were almost wholly economic.
- The movements were local.
- They were directed against the immediate enemies of the peasant. Colonialism was not the target.
- There was no continuity and no long-term organisation.
- The peasants had a strong awareness of their legal rights.

## Movements in UP, Malabar and Bardoli

### UP Kisan Sabha (February 1918)

- **Leaders:** Gauri Shankar Mishra and Indra Narayan Dwivedi set it up.
- **Supporters:** Madan Mohan Malaviya.
- **Course:** By June 1919 it had 450 branches. In June 1920 Baba Ramchandra urged Nehru to visit the villages. He brought Nehru to the villages in 1920.

### Awadh Kisan Sabha (October 1920)

- **Area:** Its centres were Rai Bareilly, Faizabad and Sultanpur.
- **Course:** It asked the kisans not to till bedakhali land (land from which a tenant had been evicted). It also asked them not to do hari and begar. Begar was unpaid forced labour.
- **Result:** It declined after repression and the Awadh Rent (Amendment) Act.

### Mappila Revolt (1921)

- **Area:** Malabar.
- **Cause:** The Mappilas were the Muslim tenants of Malabar. They rose against the Hindu landlords, who were called jenmies.
- **Course:** The revolt merged with the Khilafat movement. In August 1921 the arrest of Ali Musaliar sparked riots. Under martial law the riots turned communal.
- **Result:** The resistance ended by December 1921.

### Eka Movement (end of 1921)

Eka means unity.

- **Area:** Hardoi, Bahraich and Sitapur.
- **Cause:** Rents were 50% higher than the recorded rates.
- **Leaders:** Madari Pasi.
- **Result:** The movement ended by March 1922.

### Bardoli Satyagraha

- **Area:** Bardoli, in Surat district.
- **Cause:** Land revenue was raised by 30%.
- **Leaders:** Vallabhbhai Patel led it. The women of Bardoli called him 'Sardar'.
- **Supporters:** K.M. Munshi and Lalji Naranji resigned from the Bombay Legislative Council.
- **Course:** The movement had 13 chhavanis (camps). It also had the Bardoli Satyagraha Patrika.
- **Result:** An inquiry was held. In the end a rise of only 6.03% was allowed.

## The All India Kisan Sabha and the provinces

### All India Kisan Sabha

1. **April 1936:** The All India Kisan Sabha (AIKS) was founded at Lucknow. Swami Sahjanand Saraswati was its president. N.G. Ranga was its general secretary.
2. It had a periodical under Indulal Yagnik.
3. **1936:** The AIKS and the Congress held their sessions at Faizpur.
4. **1937–39:** These years were the high watermark of the peasant movements. The Congress ruled the provinces at this time.
5. During the War the AIKS split on communist lines. The communists followed a pro-War line. Sahjanand, Indulal Yagnik and N.G. Ranga left.

### Movements in the provinces

In Andhra, N.G. Ranga set up the India Peasants' Institute in 1933. Bihar, Punjab and Kerala are given below.

#### Bihar

1. Sahjanand was joined by Karyanand Sharma, Yadunandan Sharma, Rahul Sankritayan, Panchanan Sharma and Jamun Karjiti.
2. **1935:** The Provincial Kisan Conference adopted the anti-zamindari slogan.
3. A rift with the Congress came over bakasht land.
4. **August 1939:** The movement had died out by this month.

#### Punjab

1. **1937:** The Punjab Kisan Committee was formed.
2. The water rate was increased in the canal colonies of Multan and Montgomery.
3. Activity was concentrated in Jullundur, Amritsar, Hoshiarpur, Lyallpur and Shekhupura.
4. Two groups were largely unaffected: the Muslim tenants-at-will of west Punjab and the Hindu peasants of south-eastern Punjab.

#### Kerala

1. The peasants had Karshak Sanghams. These were organised mainly by activists of the Congress Socialist Party.
2. Jaths (marching groups) went to the landlords.
3. **1938:** A campaign was run to amend the Malabar Tenancy Act, 1929.

## Movements of 1946

### Telangana (from July 1946)

Telangana was the biggest peasant guerrilla war of modern India. It covered 3000 villages and 3 million people.

- **Area:** It began in July 1946 in Jangaon taluq of Nalgonda. Forced labour here was called vethi.
- **Course:** Its base was built through the Andhra Mahasabha. It was fought against the Nizam's Razakars. It was at its greatest intensity from August 1947 to September 1948.
- **Result:** It helped pave the way for Andhra Pradesh on linguistic lines.

### Tebhaga (September 1946)

- **Area:** The storm centre was north Bengal, among the Rajbanshis.
- **Demand:** The Bengal Provincial Kisan Sabha demanded a two-thirds share for the bargardars (sharecroppers). The Flood Commission had recommended this.
- **Slogan:** 'nij khamare dhan tolo'.
- **Result:** The movement was weakened by the Bargardari Bill of the League ministry.
