## Gist
*Book pages 574–584.*

Peasant impoverishment came from **colonial economic policies, ruin of handicrafts (overcrowding of land), the new land revenue system and the colonial administrative and judicial system**. In zamindari areas peasants faced high rents, illegal levies, arbitrary evictions and unpaid labour; in **Ryotwari** areas the government itself levied heavy revenue, pushing peasants to moneylenders.

**Indigo Revolt (1859–60):** European planters in Bengal forced peasants to grow indigo. Led by **Digambar Biswas and Bishnu Biswas of Nadia**, peasants refused, went on rent strike and used the courts; the **Bengali intelligentsia** backed them. After an **indigo commission**, a **November 1860 notification** said ryots could not be compelled to grow indigo.

**Pabna Agrarian Leagues (1870s–80s):** in Eastern Bengal zamindars enhanced rents and blocked occupancy rights under **Act X of 1859**. Peasants formed an agrarian league, ran rent strikes and fought mostly through **legal resistance**, backed by **Bankim Chandra Chatterjee, R.C. Dutt and the Indian Association (Surendranath Banerjea)**. Result: **Bengal Tenancy Act, 1885**.

**Deccan Riots (1874):** Ryotwari peasants against outsider **Marwari/Gujarati** moneylenders, worsened by the cotton-price crash after the **American Civil War**, a **50% revenue hike (1867)** and bad harvests. A **social boycott** turned into riots in Poona, Ahmednagar, Sholapur and Satara; debt bonds were burnt. Outcome: **Deccan Agriculturists Relief Act, 1879**. After 1857, peasant movements were economic, local, against immediate enemies, and **did not target colonialism**.

**UP:** the **UP Kisan Sabha (February 1918)** was set up by **Gauri Shankar Mishra and Indra Narayan Dwivedi**, supported by **Madan Mohan Malaviya**; **Baba Ramchandra** brought **Nehru** to the villages (1920). The **Awadh Kisan Sabha (October 1920)** opposed bedakhali, hari and begar; it declined after repression and the **Awadh Rent (Amendment) Act**. The **Eka Movement** (end 1921; Hardoi, Bahraich, Sitapur) was led by **Madari Pasi** and ended by **March 1922**.

**Mappila Revolt (1921):** Muslim tenants of **Malabar** against Hindu landlords (jenmies), merged with Khilafat; the arrest of **Ali Musaliar (August 1921)** sparked riots, which turned communal under martial law.

**Bardoli Satyagraha:** against a **30%** revenue hike in Surat district, led by **Vallabhbhai Patel** (called **'Sardar' by the women of Bardoli**), with **13 chhavanis**; an inquiry finally allowed only **6.03%**.

The **All India Kisan Sabha** was founded at **Lucknow in April 1936** with **Swami Sahjanand Saraswati** as president and **N.G. Ranga** as general secretary. **1937–39** was the high watermark. In **Bihar**, Sahjanand was joined by **Karyanand Sharma, Yadunandan Sharma, Rahul Sankritayan, Panchanan Sharma and Jamun Karjiti**; the **1935 Provincial Kisan Conference adopted the anti-zamindari slogan**, and a rift with the Congress came over **bakasht land**. Kerala had **Karshak Sanghams**; Punjab the **Punjab Kisan Committee (1937)**.

**Tebhaga (1946):** the **Bengal Provincial Kisan Sabha** demanded a **two-thirds share** for **bargardars** (slogan **'nij khamare dhan tolo'**); centred in **north Bengal among Rajbanshis**. **Telangana (from July 1946)**, in **Nalgonda**, was the **biggest peasant guerrilla war** of modern India (**3000 villages, 3 million people**), fought against the Nizam's Razakars.

## Key facts
- Causes of peasant impoverishment: **colonial economic policies, ruin of handicrafts, new land revenue system, colonial administrative and judicial system**.
- **Indigo Revolt (1859–60)** — led by **Digambar Biswas and Bishnu Biswas of Nadia district**; planters were **nearly all Europeans**; planters' retainers were called **lathiyals**.
- Government notification of **November 1860**: ryots **could not be compelled to grow indigo**; indigo cultivation was virtually wiped out from Bengal by **end of 1860**.
- Pabna unrest: zamindars blocked occupancy rights under **Act X of 1859**; struggle mainly **legal resistance, very little violence**; led to the **Bengal Tenancy Act, 1885**.
- Supporters of the Pabna peasants: **Bankim Chandra Chatterjee, R.C. Dutt, Indian Association under Surendranath Banerjea**.
- Deccan moneylenders were mostly outsiders — **Marwaris or Gujaratis**; land revenue was raised by **50% in 1867**.
- Deccan **social boycott (1874)** spread to **Poona, Ahmednagar, Sholapur and Satara**; led to the **Deccan Agriculturists Relief Act, 1879**.
- Post-1857 peasant movements: demands **almost wholly economic**; **colonialism was not the target**; no continuity or long-term organisation; strong awareness of **legal rights**.
- **UP Kisan Sabha** — **February 1918**, **Gauri Shankar Mishra and Indra Narayan Dwivedi**; supported by **Madan Mohan Malaviya**; **450 branches by June 1919**.
- **Baba Ramchandra** urged **Nehru** (June 1920) to visit the villages.
- **Awadh Kisan Sabha** — **October 1920**; asked kisans not to till **bedakhali** land nor do **hari and begar**; centres: **Rai Bareilly, Faizabad, Sultanpur**; declined after the **Awadh Rent (Amendment) Act**.
- **Eka (Unity) Movement** — end 1921 in **Hardoi, Bahraich, Sitapur**; rents **50% higher than recorded rates**; leader **Madari Pasi**; ended by **March 1922**.
- **Mappilas** — **Muslim tenants of Malabar**; arrest of **Ali Musaliar (August 1921)** sparked riots; resistance ended by **December 1921**.
- **Bardoli** (Surat district): revenue hike of **30%**; **Vallabhbhai Patel** led it; **women of Bardoli** called him **'Sardar'**; **13 chhavanis**; **Bardoli Satyagraha Patrika**; **K.M. Munshi and Lalji Naranji** resigned from the Bombay Legislative Council; final rise allowed **6.03%**.
- **All India Kisan Sabha** — **Lucknow, April 1936**; president **Swami Sahjanand Saraswati**; general secretary **N.G. Ranga**; periodical under **Indulal Yagnik**; AIKS and Congress sessions at **Faizpur (1936)**.
- **1937–39** — high watermark of peasant movements under Congress provincial rule.
- Kerala: **Karshak Sanghams**, organised mainly by **Congress Socialist Party** activists; **jaths** marched to landlords; 1938 campaign to amend the **Malabar Tenancy Act, 1929**.
- Andhra: **N.G. Ranga** set up the **India Peasants' Institute in 1933**.
- **Bihar**: Sahjanand joined by **Karyanand Sharma, Yadunandan Sharma, Rahul Sankritayan, Panchanan Sharma, Jamun Karjiti**.
- **Bihar**: the **1935 Provincial Kisan Conference adopted the anti-zamindari slogan**; rift with the Congress over **bakasht land**; movement died out by **August 1939**.
- Punjab: **Punjab Kisan Committee (1937)**; water-rate increase in canal colonies of **Multan and Montgomery**; activity concentrated in **Jullundur, Amritsar, Hoshiarpur, Lyallpur, Shekhupura**; Muslim tenants-at-will of west Punjab and Hindu peasants of south-eastern Punjab largely unaffected.
- During the War the AIKS split on communist lines (communists' **pro-War line**); **Sahjanand, Indulal Yagnik and N.G. Ranga** left.
- **Tebhaga** — **September 1946**, **Bengal Provincial Kisan Sabha**; **two-thirds share** for **bargardars** (Flood Commission recommendation); slogan **'nij khamare dhan tolo'**; storm centre **north Bengal, among Rajbanshis**; weakened by the League ministry's **Bargardari Bill**.
- **Telangana** — **biggest peasant guerrilla war**; **3000 villages, 3 million people**; began **July 1946** in **Jangaon taluq of Nalgonda**; forced labour called **vethi**; greatest intensity **August 1947–September 1948**; base built through the **Andhra Mahasabha**; helped pave the way for **Andhra Pradesh** on linguistic lines.
