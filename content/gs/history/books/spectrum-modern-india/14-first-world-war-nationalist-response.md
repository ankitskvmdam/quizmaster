## Gist
*Book pages 294–304.*

In the **First World War (1914-1919)** Britain, with France, Russia, USA, Italy and Japan, fought Germany, Austria-Hungary and Turkey. The nationalist response was **three-fold**: the **Moderates supported the empire as a matter of duty**; the **Extremists, including Tilak (released June 1914)**, supported the war hoping for self-government in return; the **revolutionaries** used the chance to wage war on British rule (Ghadr, Berlin Committee, Singapore mutiny). White troops in India fell at one point to only **15,000**.

**Home Rule League Movement.** Leaders such as **Tilak, Annie Besant, G.S. Khaparde, Joseph Baptista and Jinnah** wanted a year-round alliance demanding **self-government within the British commonwealth**, on the lines of the **Irish Home Rule League**. Factors: need for popular pressure, Moderate disillusionment with the **Morley-Minto reforms**, wartime taxes and prices, the war exposing the **myth of white superiority**, and Tilak and **Besant (Irish theosophist in India since 1896)** ready to lead. Besant campaigned through **New India and Commonweal**. The **1915 Congress session** decided to admit the Extremists.

**Tilak's Indian Home Rule League** (**April 1916**; first meeting at **Belgaum**; HQ **Poona**) covered **Maharashtra (excluding Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces and Berar**, with **six branches**. **Besant's All-India Home Rule League** (**September 1916, Madras**) covered the rest of India with **200 branches**; **George Arundale** was organising secretary. The campaign reached **Gujarat and Sindh**; **Jinnah led the Bombay division**. Anglo-Indians, most Muslims and southern non-brahmins stayed away.

Repression followed: **Besant, Wadia and Arundale were arrested in June 1917**; **Sir S. Subramaniya Aiyar renounced his knighthood**; Besant was released in **September 1917**. The movement **petered out by 1919**: weak organisation, **communal riots (1917-18)**, Moderates pacified by **Montagu's statement**, **Montford reforms known in July 1918**, and **Tilak abroad (September 1918)** for his libel case against **Valentine Chirol (Indian Unrest)**. In **1920 Gandhi** became president of the League and renamed it **Swarajya Sabha**. Gains: emphasis shifted to the **masses**, a **town–country organisational link**, and preparation for Gandhian politics.

**Lucknow Session (1916)**, presided by **Ambika Charan Majumdar**, **readmitted the Extremists**, helped by the deaths of **Gokhale and Pherozshah Mehta**. The **Lucknow Pact** with the Muslim League saw the **Congress accept separate electorates**. The League had shifted because of Britain's refusal to help **Turkey**, the **annulment of Bengal's partition (1911)**, refusal of an affiliating **Aligarh university**, its **Calcutta session (1912)** goal of self-government, and wartime repression of **Al Hilal** and **Comrade**. Joint demands included **half of the executive councils to be Indians** and a **five-year legislative council term**. Separate electorates made the pact a landmark in the **two-nation theory**.

**Montagu's August Declaration (20 August 1917)** promised the **progressive realisation of responsible government**, making self-government official policy; Indians objected that **no time frame** was given and the government alone would decide its pace.

## Key facts
- Nationalist response to WWI was **three-fold**: Moderates (duty), Extremists (hoping for self-government), revolutionaries (war on British rule).
- **Tilak** was released in **June 1914**.
- During the war, white soldiers in India fell at one point to only **15,000**.
- Home Rule Leagues were modelled on the **Irish Home Rule League**.
- **Annie Besant** — **Irish theosophist**, based in India **since 1896**; newspapers **New India** and **Commonweal**.
- **Tilak's Indian Home Rule League** — **April 1916**; first meeting at **Belgaum**; headquarters **Poona**; **six branches**.
- Tilak's league area: **Maharashtra (excluding Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces and Berar**; demands included **swarajya, linguistic states, vernacular education**.
- **Besant's All-India Home Rule League** — **September 1916, Madras**; rest of India incl. **Bombay city**; **200 branches**; organising secretary **George Arundale**.
- Home Rule campaign attracted the 'politically backward' regions of **Gujarat and Sindh**; **Russian Revolution (1917)** was an added advantage.
- **Mohammad Ali Jinnah** led the **Bombay division** of Besant's league.
- **Anglo-Indians, most Muslims and non-brahmins from the South** did not join.
- **June 1917**: Annie Besant, **B.P. Wadia and George Arundale** arrested; Besant released **September 1917**.
- **Sir S. Subramaniya Aiyar** renounced his **knighthood** in protest.
- Montagu's jibe — Shiva cutting his wife into **fifty-two pieces** — was about the internment of **Mrs Besant**.
- Montagu-Chelmsford reforms became known in **July 1918**.
- Tilak went abroad in **September 1918** for his libel case against **Valentine Chirol**, author of **Indian Unrest**.
- **1920**: **Gandhi** became president of the All India Home Rule League and renamed it **Swarajya Sabha**.
- **Lucknow session (1916)** — presided by Moderate **Ambika Charan Majumdar**; **Extremists readmitted**.
- Deaths of **Gokhale and Pherozshah Mehta** facilitated the Moderate-Extremist reunion.
- **Lucknow Pact**: Congress accepted **separate electorates**; Muslims got a fixed proportion of seats.
- Pact demands: **half of executive council members Indians**; **five-year** legislative council term; Secretary of State's salary from the **British treasury**.
- Lucknow Pact demands were an expanded version of the **Morley-Minto reforms**; a landmark in the **two-nation theory**.
- Muslim League's **Calcutta session (1912)** committed it to self-government suited to India.
- **Al Hilal** — **Maulana Azad**; **Comrade** — **Mohammad Ali**.
- **Montagu's August Declaration** — **20 August 1917**, House of Commons: **progressive realisation of responsible government**.
- Contrast: **Morley (1909)** said reforms were **not** meant to give self-government.
- Indian objections: **no specific time frame**; the government alone to decide nature and timing of advance.
