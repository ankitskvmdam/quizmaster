## Background: the war and the three-fold response

The First World War (1914-1919) was fought between two sides.

| Side | Countries |
| --- | --- |
| Britain and its allies | Britain, France, Russia, USA, Italy and Japan |
| Their enemies | Germany, Austria-Hungary and Turkey |

The nationalist response to the war was three-fold.

| Group | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Moderates | They supported the empire as a matter of duty. |
| Extremists, including Tilak | They supported the war. They hoped for self-government in return. |
| Revolutionaries | They used the chance to wage war on British rule. Examples are the Ghadr, the Berlin Committee and the Singapore mutiny. |

- **June 1914:** Tilak was released.
- During the war the number of white soldiers in India fell at one point to only 15,000.

## The Home Rule League Movement

### Why it began

- **Leaders:** Tilak, Annie Besant, G.S. Khaparde, Joseph Baptista and Jinnah wanted an alliance that would work all the year round.
- **Demand:** self-government within the British commonwealth.
- **Model:** the Irish Home Rule League.

Five factors led to the movement:

1. Popular pressure was needed.
2. The Moderates were disillusioned with the Morley-Minto reforms.
3. There were wartime taxes and high prices.
4. The war exposed the myth of white superiority.
5. Tilak and Annie Besant were ready to lead.

Annie Besant:

- She was an Irish theosophist.
- She had been based in India since 1896.
- She campaigned through two newspapers, New India and Commonweal.

### What happened

1. **1915:** The Congress session decided to admit the Extremists.
2. **April 1916:** Tilak set up his Indian Home Rule League.
3. **September 1916:** Besant set up her All-India Home Rule League.
4. **1917:** The Russian Revolution was an added advantage for the campaign.
5. **June 1917:** Annie Besant, B.P. Wadia and George Arundale were arrested. In protest Sir S. Subramaniya Aiyar renounced his knighthood (the title 'Sir').
6. **September 1917:** Besant was released.

The two leagues compared:

| Point | Tilak's league | Besant's league |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name | Indian Home Rule League | All-India Home Rule League |
| Started | April 1916. The first meeting was at Belgaum. | September 1916, at Madras |
| Area | Maharashtra (excluding Bombay city), Karnataka, Central Provinces and Berar | The rest of India, including Bombay city |
| Branches | Six branches | 200 branches |
| Other facts | Its headquarters was at Poona. Its demands included swarajya, linguistic states and vernacular education. | George Arundale was its organising secretary. Mohammad Ali Jinnah led its Bombay division. |

Who joined and who did not:

- The campaign attracted Gujarat and Sindh. These were called 'politically backward' regions.
- Anglo-Indians, most Muslims and non-brahmins from the South did not join.

Montagu made a jibe about the internment (confinement) of Mrs Besant. He spoke of Shiva cutting his wife into fifty-two pieces.

### Why it declined

The movement petered out by 1919. There were five reasons.

1. **Weak organisation:** The organisation of the leagues was weak.
2. **Communal riots:** There were communal riots in 1917-18.
3. **Montagu's statement:** It pacified the Moderates.
4. **July 1918:** The Montagu-Chelmsford (Montford) reforms became known.
5. **September 1918:** Tilak went abroad. He went for his libel (defamation) case against Valentine Chirol, the author of Indian Unrest.

In 1920 Gandhi became president of the All India Home Rule League. He renamed it Swarajya Sabha.

### Significance

1. The emphasis shifted to the masses.
2. An organisational link was built between town and country.
3. The movement prepared the ground for Gandhian politics.

## The Lucknow session and the Lucknow Pact (1916)

### Why it happened

The deaths of Gokhale and Pherozshah Mehta facilitated the reunion of the Moderates and the Extremists.

The Muslim League had also changed its stand. There were five reasons.

1. **1911:** The annulment of the partition of Bengal came in this year.
2. **1912:** At its Calcutta session the League committed itself to self-government suited to India.
3. **Turkey:** Britain refused to help Turkey.
4. **Aligarh university:** An affiliating university at Aligarh was refused.
5. **Repression during the war:** Al Hilal and Comrade were suppressed. Al Hilal was the paper of Maulana Azad. Comrade was the paper of Mohammad Ali.

### What happened

- **President:** Ambika Charan Majumdar, a Moderate, presided over the Lucknow session.
- **Reunion:** The Extremists were readmitted to the Congress.
- **Lucknow Pact:** The Congress made this pact with the Muslim League. The Congress accepted separate electorates. Muslims got a fixed proportion of seats.

The joint demands of the pact:

1. Half of the members of the executive councils were to be Indians.
2. The legislative council was to have a term of five years.
3. The salary of the Secretary of State was to be paid from the British treasury.

### Significance

- The demands of the pact were an expanded version of the Morley-Minto reforms.
- The pact accepted separate electorates. This made it a landmark in the two-nation theory.

## Montagu's August Declaration (1917)

### What happened

- **20 August 1917:** Montagu made the declaration in the House of Commons. It promised the progressive realisation of responsible government.

### Significance

- **Official policy:** Self-government now became official policy.
- **Contrast with 1909:** Morley had said in 1909 that the reforms were not meant to give self-government.

Indians raised two objections:

1. The declaration gave no specific time frame.
2. The government alone would decide the nature and the timing of each advance.
