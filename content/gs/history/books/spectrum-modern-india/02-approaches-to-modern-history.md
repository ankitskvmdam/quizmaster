## Historiography and the approaches

- **Historiography:** It is the study of historical interpretation. The study of a society's history starts with its historiography.
- **Four broad approaches:** The book reads modern Indian history under the Colonial (Imperialist), Nationalist, Marxist and Subaltern approaches.
- **Other influential approaches:** These are the Communalist, Cambridge, Liberal/Neo-liberal and Feminist approaches.
- **Percival Spear:** He said that histories of India call for reinterpretation for two reasons. One is changes in the Indian scene. The other is changes in historians' attitudes.

## The four broad approaches

### Colonial (Imperialist) approach

#### When

- The Colonial School dominated for the major part of the 19th century.

#### Main ideas

- This history was written by colonial officials to justify domination.
- It criticised indigenous society and praised Western values.
- Today the term 'colonial approach' is used mainly for works influenced by the colonial ideology of domination.

The approach had five features:

1. **Orientalist representation:** It gave an 'Orientalist' representation of India.
2. **Unity:** It claimed that the British brought unity.
3. **Social Darwinism:** It held that the English were the fittest to rule.
4. **White Man's burden:** It saw India as a stagnant society needing British guidance.
5. **Pax Britannica:** It was to bring law and order.

#### Writers and works

- **Examples:** James Mill, Mountstuart Elphinstone and Vincent Smith.

### Nationalist approach

#### When

- **Before 1947:** Nationalist historians of modern India did not exist before 1947. Nationalist history then dealt mainly with ancient and medieval India.

#### Main ideas

- It developed as a response to and in confrontation with the colonial approach.
- It sought to unify people across religion, caste, language and class.

#### Writers and works

- **Economic critique of colonialism:** It was built in the last quarter of the 19th century. Its authors were Dadabhai Naoroji, M.G. Ranade, G.V. Joshi, R.C. Dutt, K.T. Telang, G.K. Gokhale and D.E. Wacha.
- **Accounts of the national movement:** These came from leaders, not historians. The leaders were R.G. Pradhan, A.C. Mazumdar, J.L. Nehru and Pattabhi Sitaramayya.
- **Noted nationalist historians of modern India:** R.C. Majumdar and Tara Chand.

### Marxist approach

#### Main ideas

- Marxists see the primary contradiction between colonial masters and subject people.
- They also see the inner contradictions within Indian society.
- They tend to treat the national movement as a **bourgeois** movement, that is, a movement of the capitalist class.

#### Writers and works

The Marxist approach began with two books.

| Book | Author | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| India Today | Rajni Palme Dutt | Written for the Left Book Club. Published in England in 1940 and in India in 1947. |
| Social Background of Indian Nationalism | A.R. Desai | First published in 1948. Desai traced the national movement in five phases. |

#### Criticism

- **Sumit Sarkar:** He called R.P. Dutt's paradigm a "simplistic version of the Marxian class approach".

### Subaltern approach

Subaltern means of lower rank.

#### When

- **Early 1980s:** The Subaltern school began under the editorship of Ranajit Guha.

#### Main ideas

- It holds that all Indian historiography had an elitist bias.
- It sees the basic contradiction between the elite (Indian and foreign) and subaltern groups. It does not see it between colonialism and the Indian people.
- It rejects a class-only reading because capitalism was then nascent, that is, at an early stage.
- It sees two streams. One is the real anti-imperialist stream of the subalterns. The other is the 'bogus' national movement of the elite.

#### Criticism

- **Bipan Chandra:** He criticised the subaltern trend for dismissing all earlier writing as 'elite historiography'.

## Other approaches

### Communalist approach

- It treated Hindus and Muslims as permanently hostile groups.
- It saw medieval history as one long Hindu-Muslim conflict.
- It relied on the colonial historiography of medieval India.
- This view ended in riots and partition.

### Cambridge School

- It held that the fundamental contradiction was among Indians themselves.
- In its view, the leaders sought power and material benefits.
- It is criticised for reducing nationalism to 'animal politics'.

### Liberal/Neo-liberal approach

- **Proponents:** Patrick O'Brian, Hopkins and Cain.
- They argue that colonial exploitation did not benefit the British people as a whole.

### Feminist approach

- **1887:** Pandita Ramabai wrote The High Caste Hindu Woman.
- **1927:** Katherine Mayo wrote Mother India.
- These two works of the colonial era drew international attention.
- **1970s:** Feminist historiography grew from the women's movement of the 1970s.
- It later broadened into gender history.
