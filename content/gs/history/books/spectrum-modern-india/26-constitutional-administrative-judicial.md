## Background: why Parliament stepped in

1. **1764:** The Battle of Buxar was fought. After it the Company got the Diwani (the right to collect revenue) of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa.
2. Two deputy diwans were appointed. Mohammad Reza Khan was the deputy diwan for Bengal. Raja Shitab Rai was the deputy diwan for Bihar.
3. **1765–72:** The dual system of government lasted these seven years. It bred corruption and the bankruptcy of the Company.
4. So Parliament stepped in.

## Acts under the Company (1773–1853)

### Regulating Act, 1773

- **Governor-general and council:** Bengal got a governor-general and a council of 4. Decisions went by the majority. Warren Hastings was named in the Act.
- **Supreme Court:** A Supreme Court was set up in Bengal. It had original and appellate jurisdiction.
- **Control from Britain:** For the first time the British cabinet got the right to control Indian affairs.
- **Amendments of 1781:** These defined the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court. Within Calcutta it was to administer the personal law of the defendant.

### Pitt's India Act, 1784

- **Board of Control:** The Act created a Board of Control. Its members were the chancellor of exchequer, a secretary of state and four members of the Privy Council. This was a dual system of control.
- **Council:** The council of the governor-general was to have three members.
- **Bombay and Madras:** They were made subordinate to the governor-general.
- **'British possessions':** The territories were called 'British possessions'.

### Act of 1786

- Cornwallis got the powers of both the governor-general and the commander-in-chief.
- He could override his council.

### Charter Act, 1793

- **Privileges:** The privileges of the Company were renewed for 20 years.
- **Payment to Britain:** The Company had to pay 5 lakh pounds a year to the British government.
- **Appointments:** Royal approval was needed to appoint the governor-general, the governors and the commander-in-chief.
- **'Privilege' or 'country trade':** Licences were given for this trade. This paved the way for opium to go to China.
- **Home Government:** Its members were paid from Indian revenues. This went on until 1919.

### Charter Act, 1813

- **Trade monopoly:** The Company's trade monopoly in India ended. The China trade and the trade in tea were kept out of this.
- **Dividend:** The dividend was to be 10.5 per cent.
- **Education:** One lakh rupees a year was set aside for education.
- **Missionaries:** Christian missionaries were permitted to come to India.
- **Constitutional position:** The constitutional position of the British territories was defined for the first time.

### Charter Act, 1833

- **Trade monopoly:** The monopoly over the China trade and tea ended.
- **European immigration:** The restrictions on it were removed.
- **Centralisation:** The governor-general was given control over all civil and military affairs. Madras and Bombay lost their legislative powers.
- **Law member:** A law member was added.
- **Employment:** No Indian was to be denied employment on the ground of religion, colour, birth or descent.
- **Slavery:** Slavery was abolished in 1843.

### Charter Act, 1853

- **Court of Directors:** Its strength was reduced to 18.
- **Services:** The services were thrown open to a competitive examination. This was open competition.
- **Law member:** He was made a full member.
- **Legislation:** Six additional members were added for making laws. This body was the Indian Legislative Council.

## Acts under the Crown (1858–1935)

### Act for Better Government of India, 1858

- **Crown rule:** Rule passed from the Company to the Crown. The Crown ruled through a secretary of state with a council of 15. This council was advisory.
- **Viceroy:** The governor-general became the viceroy.
- **Dual system:** Pitt's dual system of control ended.

### Indian Councils Act, 1861

- **Portfolio system:** Lord Canning's portfolio system was brought in. Under it each member took charge of a department. It was the foundation of cabinet government.
- **Fifth member:** A fifth member, a jurist, was added.
- **Additional members:** The viceroy could add 6 to 12 additional members. At least half of them had to be non-officials. This brought non-officials into the legislatures.
- **Ordinances:** An ordinance stayed valid for 6 months.
- **Madras and Bombay:** Their legislative powers were returned.

### Indian Councils Act, 1892

- **Representation:** The principle of representation was introduced.
- **Election:** Indirect election was accepted. The word 'election' was avoided.
- **Budget:** Members could discuss the financial statements.
- **Questions:** Questions could be asked after six days' notice.

### Indian Councils Act, 1909 (Morley-Minto)

- **First Indian member:** Satyendra Prasad Sinha (S.P. Sinha) became the first Indian in the Viceroy's Executive Council. He was the law member.
- **Separate electorates:** Muslims got separate electorates. The income qualification was lower for Muslim voters.

### Government of India Act, 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford)

In August 1917 the British government declared for the first time that its aim was responsible government in India.

- **Centre:** The central legislature became bicameral, that is, it got two houses. They were the Council of State and the Legislative Assembly.
- **Election:** Direct election was introduced. The franchise was restricted.
- **Separate electorates:** They were extended to Sikhs, Christians and Anglo-Indians.
- **Provinces:** Dyarchy was introduced in the provinces. The provincial legislatures were unicameral, with one house each.
- **Budgets:** The provincial and central budgets were separated.
- **High Commissioner:** A High Commissioner was appointed in London for six years.
- **Secretary of State:** He was now paid by the British Exchequer.

### From the Simon Commission to the 1935 Act

1. **November 1927:** The Simon Commission was announced, two years early. Its other name was the Indian Statutory Commission.
2. **1930:** The Commission gave its report. It wanted dyarchy abolished, a federation set up and communal electorates continued.
3. Three Round Table Conferences (RTCs) followed.
4. **March 1933:** A White Paper on reforms was issued.
5. **1934:** A joint committee under Lord Linlithgow reported. It wanted a federation if at least 50 per cent of the princely states joined.

### Government of India Act, 1935

- **Size:** The Act had 451 clauses and 15 schedules.
- **Federation:** The Act provided for an All-India Federation. Joining it was through an instrument of accession.
- **Centre:** Dyarchy was introduced in the federal executive. The Council of States was a permanent body.
- **Division of powers:** There were three lists. The residuary powers lay with the governor-general.
- **Provinces:** Provincial autonomy began on 1 April 1937. Six provinces got bicameral legislatures: Madras, Bombay, Bengal, United Provinces, Bihar and Assam.
- **Franchise:** About 10 per cent of the people got the vote.
- **Federal Court:** A Federal Court was set up in 1937.
- **India Council:** The India Council of the Secretary of State was abolished.

## Civil services

1. **1786–93:** Cornwallis was the first to organise the civil services. He said, "Every native of Hindustan is corrupt."
2. **1800:** Wellesley set up Fort William College.
3. **1806:** The East India College at Haileybury replaced it.
4. **1863:** Satyendra Nath Tagore became the first Indian to qualify for the Indian Civil Service (ICS).
5. **1878-79:** Lytton started the Statutory Civil Service. One-sixth of the covenanted posts were to be filled by nominating Indians of high families.
6. **1886:** The Aitchison Committee was set up by Dufferin. It dropped the terms 'covenanted' and 'uncovenanted'. It proposed Imperial, Provincial and Subordinate services. It put the age limit at 23.
7. **1893:** The House of Commons passed a resolution for simultaneous examination. It was never implemented.
8. **Montford (Montagu-Chelmsford) reforms:** One-third of the recruitment was to be made in India. This share was to rise by 1.5 per cent every year.
9. **1924:** The Lee Commission wanted 50:50 parity between Indians and Europeans in the ICS within 15 years. It also wanted a Public Service Commission at once.
10. **1935 Act:** It provided for a Federal Public Service Commission and Provincial Public Service Commissions.

The maximum age for the ICS examination kept falling.

| 1859 | 23 |
| 1860 | 22 |
| 1866 | 21 |

## Police

1. **1770:** The posts of faujdars and amils were abolished.
2. **1774:** Warren Hastings restored the faujdars.
3. **1775:** Faujdar thanas (police stations) were set up.
4. **1791:** Cornwallis placed the thanas under a daroga. A Superintendent of Police (SP) was put over a district. The zamindars were relieved of police duties.
5. **Bentinck:** He abolished the office of the SP. The collector/magistrate now headed the police.
6. **1860:** A Police Commission was appointed. It led to the Indian Police Act, 1861. Under the Act an Inspector General (IG) headed a province, a Deputy IG (DIG) a range and an SP a district. There was no All-India Police.
7. **1902:** The Police Commission of this year wanted a CID in the provinces. It wanted a Central Intelligence Bureau at the Centre.

## Army

1. **1859 and 1879:** The commissions of these years wanted one-third of the army to be white. Before 1857 the share was 14%. The ratio of Europeans to Indians was 1:2 in the Bengal Army. It was 2:5 in the Madras and Bombay armies.
2. **1887–92:** Lord Roberts was the commander-in-chief. The theory of 'martial races' grew under him. Soldiers from Awadh, Bihar, Central India and South India were declared non-martial.
3. **1914:** Until this year the highest rank an Indian could reach was subedar.
4. **1918:** Commissioned ranks were opened to Indians from this year.
5. **1926:** The Indian Sandhurst Committee visualised 50% Indianised officers by 1952.

## Judiciary

In 1726 Mayor's Courts were set up at Madras, Bombay and Calcutta. They began the common law system.

### Under Warren Hastings

- **District Diwani Adalats:** These were civil courts. They were under the collector.
- **District Fauzdari Adalats:** These were criminal courts.
- **Sadar Nizamat Adalat:** It sat at Murshidabad under a deputy nizam.

### Under Cornwallis

- **Circuit courts:** They were set up at Calcutta, Dacca, Murshidabad and Patna.
- **Sadar Nizamat Adalat:** It was shifted to Calcutta.
- **Collector:** He was left with only revenue work.
- **Cornwallis Code:** It stood for the separation of revenue and justice. It also stood for the sovereignty of law.

Cornwallis graded the civil courts. From the lowest to the highest they were:

1. Munsiff's court
2. Registrar's court
3. District court
4. Circuit court
5. Sadar Diwani Adalat
6. King-in-Council, for appeals of 5000 pounds and above

### Under Bentinck

- The circuit courts were abolished.
- A Sadar Diwani Adalat and a Sadar Nizamat Adalat were set up at Allahabad.
- English replaced Persian in the Supreme Court.

### Codes and High Courts

1. **1833:** A Law Commission was set up under Macaulay. Its work led to three codes.
2. **1859:** The Civil Procedure Code was made.
3. **1860:** The Indian Penal Code (IPC) was made.
4. **1861:** The Criminal Procedure Code (CrPC) was made.
5. **1865:** The Supreme Court and the Sadar Adalats were merged into High Courts at Calcutta, Bombay and Madras.

## Finance and local bodies

### Financial decentralisation

1. **1870:** Mayo fixed grants for police, jails, education and other heads.
2. **1877:** Lytton transferred land revenue, excise and other heads.
3. **1882:** The revenues were divided three ways.

### Local bodies

1. **1864–68:** Local bodies were first formed.
2. **1870:** Mayo's Resolution began local finance.
3. **1882:** Ripon's Resolution wanted a non-official majority and non-official chairpersons. Ripon is called the father of local self-government.
4. **Lord Curzon:** He increased official control over local bodies.
5. **1908:** The Royal Commission on Decentralisation wanted more powers for village panchayats. It wanted sub-district boards in every taluka/tehsil.
6. **May 1918:** The Resolution of May 1918 reviewed local self-government. It came after the announcement of 20 August 1917.
7. **1919:** Under the Act of this year local self-government became a transferred subject. Finance stayed a reserved subject.
8. **May 1930:** The Simon Commission found that village panchayats had progressed only in UP, Bengal and Madras.
9. **Constitution of India:** Article 40 directs the states to organise village panchayats. The 73rd and 74th Amendments strengthen local bodies.
