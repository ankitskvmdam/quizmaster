## Gist
*Book pages 501–531.*

After **Buxar (1764)** the Company got the **Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa**, with **Mohammad Reza Khan** (Bengal) and **Raja Shitab Rai** (Bihar) as deputy diwans. The **dual system (1765–72)** bred corruption and the Company's bankruptcy, so Parliament stepped in.

The **Regulating Act (1773)** set up a **governor-general and council of 4** in Bengal (majority rule) and a **Supreme Court** in Bengal. **Pitt's India Act (1784)** created the **Board of Control** — a **dual system of control** — and made Bombay and Madras subordinate. The **1786 Act** let Cornwallis override his council. The **Charter Act of 1793** renewed privileges for 20 years and required **5 lakh pounds** a year for Britain; **1813** ended the trade monopoly in India (not China/tea), set aside **one lakh rupees** for education and admitted missionaries; **1833** ended the China/tea monopoly, centralised power under the governor-general, added a **law member** and barred discrimination in employment; **1853** introduced **open competition** for services and **six additional legislative members**. The **1858 Act** transferred rule to the Crown via a **secretary of state with a council of 15**; the governor-general became **viceroy**.

**Indian Councils Act 1861** brought non-officials into legislatures and Canning's **portfolio system**; **1892** introduced the principle of representation, budget discussion and questions; **1909 (Morley-Minto)** brought the first Indian (**S.P. Sinha**) to the Executive Council and **separate electorates for Muslims**. The **1919 Act (Montagu-Chelmsford)** gave a **bicameral centre**, direct election, **dyarchy in provinces**, separate budgets and a **High Commissioner** in London. The **Simon Commission** (announced **Nov 1927**, report **1930**) led, via three RTCs and the **1933 White Paper**, to the **1935 Act** (**451 clauses, 15 schedules**): an All-India Federation, **dyarchy at the Centre**, provincial autonomy from **1 April 1937**, bicameral legislatures in six provinces including **Bihar**, about **10%** franchise and a **Federal Court (1937)**.

**Civil services**: Cornwallis organised them; Wellesley's **Fort William College (1800)** gave way to **Haileybury (1806)**; **Satyendra Nath Tagore** was the first Indian ICS (**1863**); Lytton's **Statutory Civil Service (1878-79)**; **Aitchison Committee (1886)**; **Lee Commission (1924)**. **Police**: Cornwallis's **thanas under darogas (1791)**, the **Police Commission (1860)** and **Police Act 1861**, the **1902 Commission** (CID). **Army**: one-third white army, the **'martial races'** theory under **Lord Roberts**; soldiers of **Awadh and Bihar** were labelled non-martial.

**Judiciary**: Warren Hastings's **District Diwani and Fauzdari Adalats**; Cornwallis's **circuit courts at Calcutta, Dacca, Murshidabad and Patna** and the **Cornwallis Code**; Bentinck's adalats at **Allahabad**; **Macaulay's Law Commission (1833)**; **High Courts (1865)**. **Local bodies**: **Mayo's Resolution (1870)**, **Ripon's Resolution (1882)** — Ripon is the **father of local self-government** — and the **Royal Commission on Decentralisation (1908)**.

## Key facts
- After Buxar, deputy diwans: **Mohammad Reza Khan (Bengal)** and **Raja Shitab Rai (Bihar)**.
- The **dual system** of government lasted **seven years (1765–72)**.
- **Regulating Act 1773**: governor-general + **council of 4** in Bengal, majority rule; **Warren Hastings** named in the Act; **Supreme Court** in Bengal with original and appellate jurisdiction; for the first time the **British cabinet** got the right to control Indian affairs.
- **Amendments of 1781** defined the Supreme Court's jurisdiction — within Calcutta it administered the **personal law of the defendant**.
- **Pitt's India Act 1784**: **Board of Control** (chancellor of exchequer, a secretary of state, four Privy Council members) — **dual system of control**; GG's council of **three**; Bombay and Madras made **subordinate** to the GG; territories called **'British possessions'**.
- **Act of 1786**: Cornwallis got the powers of both governor-general and **commander-in-chief** and could **override his council**.
- **Charter Act 1793**: privileges renewed for **20 years**; **5 lakh pounds** a year to the British government; **royal approval** for appointing GG, governors and C-in-C; licences for **'privilege' or 'country trade'** (paved way for opium to China); Home Government members paid from Indian revenues **until 1919**.
- **Charter Act 1813**: ended trade monopoly in India except **China trade and tea**; **10.5 per cent dividend**; **one lakh rupees** a year for education; **Christian missionaries** permitted; constitutional position of British territories defined for the first time.
- **Charter Act 1833**: ended **China and tea** monopoly; removed restrictions on **European immigration**; GG given control over all civil and military affairs; Madras and Bombay lost legislative powers; **law member** added; no Indian to be denied employment on religion, colour, birth, descent; **slavery abolished in 1843**.
- **Charter Act 1853**: Court of Directors reduced to **18**; services thrown open to **competitive examination**; law member made full member; **six additional members** for legislation — the **Indian Legislative Council**.
- **Act for Better Government of India 1858**: Crown rule through a **secretary of state + council of 15** (advisory); GG became **viceroy**; Pitt's dual system ended.
- **Indian Councils Act 1861**: **portfolio system** of **Lord Canning** (foundation of cabinet government); fifth (jurist) member; viceroy could add **6 to 12** additional members, at least half non-officials; **ordinances valid 6 months**; legislative powers returned to **Madras and Bombay**.
- **Indian Councils Act 1892**: principle of **representation**; indirect election accepted (the word 'election' avoided); discussion of **financial statements**; questions after **six days' notice**.
- **Indian Councils Act 1909 (Morley-Minto)**: **Satyendra Prasad Sinha** — first Indian in the Viceroy's Executive Council (law member); **separate electorates for Muslims**, with lower income qualification for Muslim voters.
- **August 1917**: British government first declared its aim of **responsible government** in India.
- **GoI Act 1919**: central **Council of State + Legislative Assembly**; **direct election** (restricted franchise); separate electorates extended to **Sikhs, Christians and Anglo-Indians**; **dyarchy in provinces**; unicameral provincial legislatures; provincial and central **budgets separated**; **High Commissioner in London** (six years); Secretary of State paid by **British Exchequer**.
- **Simon Commission (Indian Statutory Commission)**: announced **November 1927** (two years early); report **1930** — abolish dyarchy, federation, continue communal electorates.
- **White Paper** on reforms **March 1933**; joint committee under **Lord Linlithgow** reported **1934** (federation if at least **50 per cent** princely states joined).
- **GoI Act 1935**: **451 clauses, 15 schedules**; All-India Federation (**instrument of accession**); **dyarchy at the federal executive**; Council of States a **permanent body**; three lists; **residuary powers** with the governor-general; provincial autonomy (**April 1, 1937**); bicameral legislatures in **Madras, Bombay, Bengal, United Provinces, Bihar and Assam**; about **10 per cent** got the vote; **Federal Court (1937)**; **India Council of Secretary of State abolished**.
- **Cornwallis (1786–93)** first organised the civil services; called "Every native of Hindustan is corrupt."
- **Wellesley** set up **Fort William College (1800)**; replaced by **East India College, Haileybury (1806)**.
- ICS maximum age: **23 (1859) → 22 (1860) → 21 (1866) → 19 (1878)**.
- **Satyendra Nath Tagore** — first Indian to qualify for the ICS (**1863**).
- **Statutory Civil Service (1878-79)** by **Lytton** — **one-sixth** of covenanted posts by nomination of Indians of high families.
- **Aitchison Committee (1886)**, set up by **Dufferin**: dropped 'covenanted/uncovenanted'; Imperial, Provincial and Subordinate services; age limit **23**.
- **1893**: House of Commons resolution for **simultaneous examination** — never implemented.
- **Montford reforms**: **one-third** of recruitment in India, raised by **1.5 per cent** annually.
- **Lee Commission (1924)**: **50:50** Indian-European parity in ICS within **15 years**; immediate **Public Service Commission**.
- **1935 Act**: **Federal** and **Provincial Public Service Commissions**.
- Police: **1770** faujdars and amils abolished; **1774** Warren Hastings restored faujdars; **1775** faujdar thanas.
- **Cornwallis (1791)**: thanas under a **daroga**, SP over a district; zamindars relieved of police duties.
- **Bentinck** abolished the office of **SP**; collector/magistrate headed police.
- **Police Commission (1860)** → **Indian Police Act 1861**: IG (province), DIG (range), SP (district); no All-India Police.
- **Police Commission 1902**: **CID** in provinces, **Central Intelligence Bureau** at the Centre.
- Army: commissions of **1859 and 1879** — one-third white army (against **14%** before 1857); Europeans to Indians **1:2 in Bengal Army**, **2:5 in Madras and Bombay**.
- Highest rank for an Indian until **1914** was **subedar**; commissioned ranks from **1918**; **Indian Sandhurst Committee (1926)** visualised 50% Indianised officers by **1952**.
- **'Martial races'** theory under **Lord Roberts** (C-in-C 1887–92); soldiers from **Awadh, Bihar**, Central India and South India declared non-martial.
- **Mayor's Courts** at Madras, Bombay and Calcutta (**1726**) began the common law system.
- **Warren Hastings**: District **Diwani** Adalats (civil, under collector) and District **Fauzdari** Adalats (criminal); **Sadar Nizamat Adalat at Murshidabad** under a deputy nizam.
- **Cornwallis**: circuit courts at **Calcutta, Dacca, Murshidabad and Patna**; Sadar Nizamat Adalat shifted to **Calcutta**; collector left with only revenue work; **Cornwallis Code** — separation of revenue and justice, sovereignty of law.
- Civil court gradation (Cornwallis): **Munsiff → Registrar → District → Circuit → Sadar Diwani Adalat → King-in-Council** (appeals of 5000 pounds and above).
- **Bentinck**: circuit courts abolished; Sadar Diwani and Sadar Nizamat Adalats at **Allahabad**; English replaced Persian in the Supreme Court.
- **Law Commission (1833)** under **Macaulay** → **Civil Procedure Code (1859)**, **IPC (1860)**, **CrPC (1861)**.
- **1865**: Supreme Court and Sadar Adalats merged into **High Courts at Calcutta, Bombay and Madras**.
- Financial decentralisation: **Mayo (1870)** fixed grants for police, jails, education etc.; **Lytton (1877)** transferred land revenue, excise etc.; **1882** three-way division of revenues.
- Local bodies first formed **1864–68**; **Mayo's Resolution (1870)** began local finance.
- **Ripon's Resolution (1882)** — **father of local self-government**; non-official majority and non-official chairpersons.
- **Lord Curzon** increased official control over local bodies.
- **Royal Commission on Decentralisation (1908)**: more powers to village panchayats; **sub-district boards** in every taluka/tehsil.
- **Resolution of May 1918** reviewed local self-government after the **August 20, 1917** announcement.
- Under **1919** local self-government became a **transferred** subject (finance stayed **reserved**).
- **Simon Commission (May 1930)**: village panchayats progressed only in **UP, Bengal and Madras**.
- Article **40** of the Constitution directs states to organise village panchayats; **73rd and 74th Amendments** strengthen local bodies.
