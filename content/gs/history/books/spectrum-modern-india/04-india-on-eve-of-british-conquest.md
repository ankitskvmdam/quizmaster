## Background: the decline begins

1. **1658–1707:** The reign of Aurangzeb marked the beginning of the end of Mughal rule.
2. Wars of succession and weak rulers sped up the decline.
3. The first half of the 18th century saw the decline of the Mughals.

## External challenges: Nadir Shah and Abdali

### Nadir Shah

Nadir Shah was the emperor of Persia.

1. **1738–39:** He attacked India and took Lahore.
2. **13 February 1739:** He defeated the Mughals at Karnal. He captured Muhammad Shah.
3. He looted Delhi. He carried off the Peacock Throne, the Kohinoor and about seventy crore rupees.
4. He took the territory west of the Indus including Kabul.

### Ahmad Shah Abdali

Ahmad Shah Abdali was also called Durrani.

1. **1747:** He was elected the successor of Nadir Shah.
2. **1748 to 1767:** He invaded India several times between these years.
3. **1751–52:** The Mughals ceded Punjab to him.
4. **1757:** He captured Delhi and recognised Alamgir II. He made the Rohilla chief Najib-ud-Daula the Mir Bakhshi (head of the army department).
5. **1758:** Raghunath Rao expelled Najib-ud-Daula and captured Punjab.
6. **1761:** Abdali defeated the Marathas in the Third Battle of Panipat.
7. **1767:** His last invasion came in this year.

### Why battles were fought at Panipat

Two battles had been fought at Panipat earlier:

- **First Battle of Panipat (1526):** Babur fought Ibrahim Lodi.
- **Second Battle of Panipat (1556):** Akbar fought Hemu.

Panipat was favoured for four reasons:

1. It was near Delhi.
2. Its flat ground suited cavalry.
3. It lay on the Grand Trunk Road of Sher Shah Suri (1540–45).
4. The monsoon there is short.

## The later Mughals: one by one

### Bahadur Shah I

- He was Prince Muazzam before he became emperor. He became emperor at the age of 63.
- Khafi Khan gave him the title Shah-i-Bekhabar.
- He released Shahu.
- **February 1712:** He died.

### Jahandar Shah

- His prime minister Zulfikar Khan introduced the izara system (revenue farming).
- Jaziya (a tax on non-Muslims) was abolished in his reign.

### Farrukhsiyar

- He abolished jaziya and the pilgrimage tax.
- **1717:** He gave farmans to the British.
- **1719:** The Sayyid brothers dethroned him with the help of Peshwa Balaji Vishwanath. He was the first Mughal emperor killed by his nobles.
- The Sayyid brothers were Abdulla Khan and Hussain Ali. They were known as the 'King Makers'.

### Rafi-ud-Darajat

- He reigned from February to June 1719. This was the shortest reign among the Mughals.

### Rafi-ud-Daula

- He took the title Shah Jahan II. He was an opium addict.

### Muhammad Shah

- He ruled for 29 years (1719–48). He was called 'Rangeela'.
- He was an incompetent ruler.
- Hyderabad, Bengal, Awadh and Punjab became independent in his reign.
- **1737:** Peshwa Baji Rao I invaded Delhi with 500 horsemen.

### Ahmad Shah

- He reigned from 1748 to 1754.
- He left the affairs of state to Udham Bai. Her title was Qibla-i-Alam. She ruled with the eunuch Javid Khan.

### Alamgir II

- He was a grandson of Jahandar Shah.
- **June 1757:** The Battle of Plassey was fought in his reign.

### Shah Alam II

- He reigned from 1759 to 1806.
- The Third Battle of Panipat (1761) and the Battle of Buxar (1764) were fought in his reign.
- **August 1765:** The Treaty of Allahabad was signed. He came under the protection of the Company and lived at Allahabad. By a farman he granted the Company the Diwani (the right to collect revenue) of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa in perpetuity.
- **1772:** The Marathas took him to Delhi.
- **1803:** He accepted English protection again. This was after the defeat of Daulat Rao Scindia.

### Akbar II

- He reigned from 1806 to 1837.
- He gave the title of Raja to Rammohan Rai.
- **1835:** Coins bearing the names of the Mughals stopped.

### Bahadur Shah II

- He was also called Zafar. He was the last Mughal emperor.
- **1 November 1858:** The Mughal Empire legally ended.
- **1862:** He died at Rangoon.

## Regional states

The regional states were of three kinds.

| Kind | States |
| --- | --- |
| Successor states | Bengal, Awadh, Hyderabad |
| Independent kingdoms | Mysore, Rajput states |
| New states | Marathas, Sikhs, Jats |

### Bengal

1. **1717:** Murshid Quli Khan founded the state of Bengal.
2. **1727:** Shuja-ud-din followed him.
3. Sarfaraz Khan came after Shuja-ud-din.
4. **1740:** Alivardi Khan killed Sarfaraz Khan at Gheria. Alivardi Khan was the deputy governor of Bihar.

### Awadh

1. **1722:** Saadat Khan, titled Burhan-ul-Mulk, founded Awadh. He was a Shia.
2. He committed suicide under pressure from Nadir Shah.
3. Safdar Jang succeeded him.

### Hyderabad

1. Zulfikar Khan was the first to conceive of an independent state in the Deccan. He died in 1713.
2. **1724:** Kilich Khan, titled Nizam-ul-Mulk, killed Mubariz Khan at Shakr-Kheda. The state of Hyderabad is dated from this year.
3. **1725:** He became viceroy and took the title Asaf-Jah.

### Mysore

- Mysore lay at the junction of the Eastern and Western Ghats.
- It was ruled by the Wodeyars and then by Haider Ali.

### Rajput states

- **1708:** Bahadur Shah I marched against Ajit Singh. Ajit Singh had allied with Jai Singh II and Durgadas Rathor.

### Marathas

- By 1740 Maratha influence had spread over Gujarat, Malwa and Bundelkhand.

### Sikhs

1. The Sikhs were organised into 12 misls (confederacies).
2. Ranjit Singh was the son of Mahan Singh of the Sukarchakiya misl.
3. **1799:** Ranjit Singh took Lahore.
4. **1802:** He took Amritsar.
5. **1838:** He signed the Tripartite Treaty with Shah Shuja and the Company.
6. **1839:** He died.

### Jats

1. Churaman and Badan Singh set up the Jat state of Bharatpur.
2. Jat power reached its zenith under Suraj Mal.
3. **1763:** Suraj Mal died.

### Other states

- **Kerala:** Martanda Varma built a state with its capital at Travancore. He extended it from Kanyakumari to Cochin.
- **Rohilakhand:** Ali Muhammad Khan founded it. It lay between Kumaon and the Ganga.
- **Farrukhabad:** Mohammad Khan Bangash set up a kingdom around Farrukhabad, east of Delhi.

## Economy and society

### Trade and industry

India's exports exceeded its imports. The trade was balanced by gold and silver. So India was called a 'sink of precious metals'.

| Imported from | Goods |
| --- | --- |
| Arabia | coffee |
| China | tea, sugar, porcelain, silk |
| Tibet | gold, musk, woollen cloth |
| Africa | ivory |

- Patna was an important textile centre.
- Kashmir was known for woollen manufactures.
- The Zamorin of Calicut used the Kunjali Maraikkars for his navy.

### Education

- **Elementary schools:** pathshalas for Hindus and maktabs for Muslims.
- **Higher learning:** Chatuspathis or Tols in Bihar and Bengal.
- **Persian and Arabic:** taught in Madrasahs.
- **Sanskrit centres:** Kasi, Tirhut (Mithila), Nadia and Utkala.
- **Persian education:** Azimabad (Patna) was a famous centre.

### Social life

- Sharif Muslims looked down on ajlaf Muslims, the lower class.
- Dowry was widespread in Bengal and Rajputana.
- Sawai Jai Singh and Prashuram Bhau tried to promote widow remarriage. They failed.
- European companies bought slaves from the markets of Bengal, Assam and Bihar.
- Abyssinian slaves were sold at Surat, Madras and Calcutta.

### Culture

- **1784:** Asaf-ud-Daula built the Bada Imambara at Lucknow.
- Sawai Jai Singh is linked with the pink city of Jaipur.
- His five observatories were at Delhi, Jaipur, Benares, Mathura and Ujjain.
- His time-tables were called Jij Muhammad-shahi.
- Urdu poetry grew in this period.

## Why the Mughal Empire declined

The decline had eight causes.

1. **Weak rulers:** The empire was a personal despotism, and the rulers were weak.
2. **No law of succession:** There was no law to settle who would succeed an emperor.
3. **Jagirdari crisis:** There was a shortage of jagirs. This shortage was called bejagiri. Mughal rule has been called "the rule of the nobility".
4. **Zamindars:** The allegiance of the zamindars kept shifting.
5. **Aurangzeb's policies:** His religious policy and his Deccan policy weakened the empire.
6. **Army:** The army deteriorated.
7. **Foreign invasions:** Invasions from outside hit the empire.
8. **Economy:** The economy declined.
