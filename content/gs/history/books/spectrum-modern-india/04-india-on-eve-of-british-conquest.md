## Gist
*Book pages 59–80.*

The first half of the 18th century saw the **decline of the Mughals**. **Aurangzeb's reign (1658–1707)** marked the beginning of the end; wars of succession and weak rulers sped up the decline. **Muhammad Shah** ruled for **29 years (1719–48)** but was incompetent; his reign saw **Hyderabad, Bengal, Awadh and Punjab** become independent.

**External challenges:** **Nadir Shah** (Persian emperor) attacked in **1738–39**, took Lahore, defeated the Mughals at **Karnal (13 February 1739)**, captured Muhammad Shah and looted Delhi, carrying off the **Peacock Throne**, the **Kohinoor** and about **seventy crore rupees**; he took the territory **west of the Indus including Kabul**. **Ahmad Shah Abdali (Durrani)**, elected Nadir's successor in **1747**, invaded several times between **1748 and 1767**: Punjab was ceded to him in **1751–52**; in **1757** he took Delhi, recognised **Alamgir II** and made the Rohilla **Najib-ud-Daula Mir Bakhshi**; in **1758 Raghunath Rao** expelled Najib and captured Punjab; Abdali defeated the Marathas at the **Third Battle of Panipat (1761)**; his last invasion came in **1767**. Panipat was favoured for its **flat ground suited to cavalry**, nearness to Delhi and position on Sher Shah's **Grand Trunk Road**.

**Later Mughals:** **Bahadur Shah I** (titled **Shah-i-Bekhabar** by **Khafi Khan**) released **Shahu**; **Jahandar Shah** — **Zulfikar Khan** introduced the **izara system**; **Farrukhsiyar** gave **farmans to the British (1717)** and was dethroned by the **Sayyid brothers ('King Makers')** with Peshwa **Balaji Vishwanath** — the **first emperor killed by his nobles**; **Rafi-ud-Darajat** had the **shortest reign**; Muhammad Shah **'Rangeela'**; **Ahmad Shah** left affairs to **Udham Bai (Qibla-i-Alam)**; **Shah Alam II (1759–1806)** saw **Panipat (1761)** and **Buxar (1764)**, and under the **Treaty of Allahabad (August 1765)** granted the Company the **Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa**; **Akbar II** gave **Rammohan** the title **Raja**; **Bahadur Shah II** died in **Rangoon (1862)**. The empire legally ended on **1 November 1858**.

**Causes of decline:** personal despotism under weak rulers, no law of succession, **jagirdari crisis (bejagiri)**, shifting allegiance of zamindars, Aurangzeb's religious and Deccan policies, a deteriorating army, foreign invasions and economic decline.

**Regional states** were of three kinds: **successor states** (**Hyderabad, Bengal, Awadh**), **independent kingdoms** (**Mysore, Rajput states**) and **new states** (**Marathas, Sikhs, Jats**). **Nizam-ul-Mulk (Kilich Khan)** killed Mubariz Khan at **Shakr-Kheda (1724)** and took the title **Asaf-Jah**; **Saadat Khan Burhan-ul-Mulk** founded Awadh; **Murshid Kuli Khan** founded Bengal, where **Alivardi Khan, deputy governor of Bihar**, killed **Sarfaraz Khan (1740)** at **Gheria**. **Martanda Varma** ruled Kerala from **Travancore**; Jat power peaked under **Suraj Mal** (Bharatpur); **Ranjit Singh** of the **Sukarchakiya misl** took **Lahore (1799)** and **Amritsar (1802)**.

**Society and economy:** exports exceeded imports and India was a **'sink of precious metals'**; **Patna** was a textile centre. Higher learning was given in **Chatuspathis or Tols (Bihar and Bengal)**; **Tirhut (Mithila)** was a Sanskrit centre and **Azimabad (Patna)** a centre of **Persian** education. European companies bought slaves in **Bengal, Assam and Bihar**. Culture: **Bada Imambara (1784)**, **Sawai Jai Singh's** five observatories, growth of **Urdu** poetry, **Heer Ranjha** (Warris Shah) and **Risalo** (Shah Abdul Latif).

## Key facts
- **Aurangzeb (1658–1707)**: his reign signified the **beginning of the end** of Mughal rule.
- **Muhammad Shah** ruled **29 years (1719–48)**; called **'Rangeela'**; his reign saw independent **Hyderabad, Bengal, Awadh and Punjab**.
- **Nadir Shah** defeated the Mughals at **Karnal on 13 February 1739**; took the **Peacock Throne**, **Kohinoor** and about **seventy crore rupees**; annexed lands **west of the Indus including Kabul**.
- **Ahmad Shah Abdali (Durrani)** was elected Nadir Shah's successor in **1747**; invaded between **1748 and 1767**; Mughals ceded **Punjab** to him in **1751–52**.
- In **1757** Abdali captured Delhi, recognised **Alamgir II** and made Rohilla chief **Najib-ud-Daula Mir Bakhshi**; in **1758 Raghunath Rao** expelled Najib and captured Punjab.
- **Third Battle of Panipat (1761)**: Abdali defeated the Marathas; Abdali's **last invasion: 1767**.
- **First Panipat (1526)**: Babur vs Ibrahim Lodi; **Second (1556)**: Akbar vs Hemu.
- Panipat favoured: proximity to Delhi, **flat ground for cavalry**, on the **Grand Trunk Road** of **Sher Shah Suri (1540–45)**, short monsoon.
- **Bahadur Shah I** (Prince Muazzam, became emperor at **63**) was called **Shah-i-Bekhabar** by **Khafi Khan**; released **Shahu**; died **February 1712**.
- **Jahandar Shah**: PM **Zulfikar Khan** introduced the **izara system**; abolished jaziya.
- **Farrukhsiyar** abolished jaziya and pilgrimage tax; gave **farmans to the British in 1717**; dethroned (1719) by the **Sayyid brothers** with Peshwa **Balaji Vishwanath** — **first Mughal emperor killed by his nobles**.
- **Sayyid brothers — Abdulla Khan and Hussain Ali** — known as **'King Makers'**.
- **Rafi-ud-Darajat** (Feb–June 1719): **shortest reign** among the Mughals; **Rafi-ud-Daula** took the title **Shah Jahan II** (opium addict).
- In **1737** Peshwa **Baji Rao I** invaded Delhi with **500 horsemen**.
- **Ahmad Shah (1748–54)** left affairs to **Udham Bai** (title **Qibla-i-Alam**), who ruled with eunuch **Javid Khan**.
- **Alamgir II** (grandson of Jahandar Shah): **Battle of Plassey (June 1757)** fought in his reign.
- **Shah Alam II (1759–1806)**: **Third Panipat (1761)** and **Battle of Buxar (1764)**; **Treaty of Allahabad (August 1765)** — Company protection, lived at **Allahabad**; farman granting **Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa** in perpetuity.
- Shah Alam II was taken to Delhi by the **Marathas in 1772**; accepted English protection again in **1803** after the defeat of **Daulat Rao Scindia**.
- **Akbar II (1806–37)** gave the title **Raja to Rammohan Rai**; coins bearing Mughal names stopped in **1835**.
- **Bahadur Shah II (Zafar)**, the **last Mughal emperor**, died at **Rangoon in 1862**; Mughal Empire legally ended on **1 November 1858**.
- Shortage of jagirs was called **bejagiri**; Mughal rule has been called **"the rule of the nobility"**.
- By **1740** Maratha influence spread over **Gujarat, Malwa and Bundelkhand**.
- Regional states: **Successor States** (Awadh, Bengal, Hyderabad); **Independent Kingdoms** (Mysore, Rajput states); **New States** (Maratha, Sikh, Jat).
- Summary dates: **Hyderabad 1724 (Nizam-ul-Mulk)**, **Bengal 1717 (Murshid Quli Khan)**, **Awadh 1722 (Saadat Khan Burhan-ul-Mulk)**.
- **Zulfikar Khan** first conceived an independent Deccan state (died **1713**); **Kilich Khan (Nizam-ul-Mulk)** killed **Mubariz Khan** at **Shakr-Kheda (1724)**; became viceroy in **1725** with the title **Asaf-Jah**.
- **Saadat Khan (Burhan-ul-Mulk)**, a **Shia**, founded Awadh; committed suicide under pressure from Nadir Shah; succeeded by **Safdar Jang**.
- Bengal: **Murshid Kuli Khan** → **Shuja-ud-din (1727)** → **Sarfaraz Khan**, killed in **1740** at **Gheria** by **Alivardi Khan, deputy governor of Bihar**.
- Bahadur Shah I marched against **Ajit Singh (1708)**, allied with **Jai Singh II** and **Durgadas Rathor**.
- Mysore, at the junction of the Eastern and Western Ghats, was ruled by the **Wodeyars**, then **Haider Ali**.
- **Martanda Varma**: Kerala state with capital **Travancore**; extended it from **Kanyakumari to Cochin**.
- Jat state of **Bharatpur** set up by **Churaman and Badan Singh**; zenith under **Suraj Mal** (died **1763**).
- Sikhs organised into **12 misls**; **Ranjit Singh**, son of **Mahan Singh** of the **Sukarchakiya misl**, took **Lahore (1799)** and **Amritsar (1802)**; **Tripartite Treaty (1838)** with **Shah Shuja** and the Company; died **1839**.
- **Ali Muhammad Khan** founded **Rohilakhand** (between **Kumaon** and the **Ganga**); **Mohammad Khan Bangash** set up a kingdom around **Farrukhabad**, east of Delhi.
- India's exports exceeded imports; trade balanced by gold and silver — India a **'sink of precious metals'**.
- Imports: **coffee** from Arabia; **tea, sugar, porcelain, silk** from China; **gold, musk, woollen cloth** from Tibet; **ivory** from Africa.
- **Patna** was an important textile centre; **Kashmir** — woollen manufactures.
- Zamorin of Calicut used the **Kunjali Maraikkars** for his navy.
- Elementary schools: **pathshalas** (Hindu), **maktabs** (Muslim); higher learning: **Chatuspathis or Tols** in **Bihar and Bengal**; **Madrasahs** for Persian and Arabic.
- Sanskrit centres: **Kasi, Tirhut (Mithila), Nadia, Utkala**; **Azimabad (Patna)** — famous centre of **Persian** education.
- **Sharif** Muslims looked down on **ajlaf** (lower class) Muslims.
- **Dowry** widespread in **Bengal and Rajputana**; **Sawai Jai Singh** and **Prashuram Bhau** tried to promote widow remarriage but failed.
- European companies bought slaves from markets of **Bengal, Assam and Bihar**; **Abyssinian slaves** sold at Surat, Madras and Calcutta.
- **Asaf-ud-Daula** built the **Bada Imambara**, Lucknow (**1784**).
- **Sawai Jai Singh**: pink city of **Jaipur**; observatories at **Delhi, Jaipur, Benares, Mathura, Ujjain**; time-tables **Jij Muhammad-shahi**.
- Urdu poets: **Mir, Sauda, Nazir, Mirza Ghalib**; **Tayumanavar (1706–44)** — **sittar** (Tamil) poetry; **Kanchan Nambiar** — Malayalam.
- **Heer Ranjha** — **Warris Shah** (Punjabi); **Risalo** — **Shah Abdul Latif** (Sindhi).
