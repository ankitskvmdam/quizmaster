## The legal framework

### The Constitution

1. **26 November 1949:** The provisions on citizenship and Article 324 came into force. Article 324 deals with the Election Commission.
2. **26 January 1950:** The rest of the Constitution came into force.

The electoral procedure is given in Part XV of the Constitution (Articles 324–329).

### The Election Commission

1. **25 January 1950:** The office of the Election Commission was set up.
2. **21 March 1950:** Sukumar Sen took office as the first Chief Election Commissioner. He was an officer of the Indian Civil Service (ICS).

- **Powers under Article 324:** The commission superintends, directs and controls elections. These are the elections to Parliament, to the state legislatures and to the offices of President and Vice-President.
- **Single-member body:** For long the commission was a single-member body.

⚠ May be outdated: the Election Commission as a single-member body. It has been a multi-member commission since the 1990s.

### The two laws

Two laws made the polls possible.

| Law | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Representation of the People Act, 1950 | Qualifications of voters, electoral rolls and allocation of seats. |
| Representation of the People Act, 1951 | Qualifications and disqualifications of members, conduct of elections, poll expenses and counting. |

## Seats

| House | Seats | Reserved for Scheduled Castes | Reserved for Scheduled Tribes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lok Sabha | 489 elective seats | 72 | 26 |
| State assemblies | 3,283 seats | 477 | 192 |

There were 401 constituencies.

- **Single-seat constituencies:** 314.
- **Two-seat constituencies:** 86.
- **Three-seat constituency:** 1.

## Voters and the electoral rolls

### Who could vote

- **Universal adult franchise:** Every adult could vote. The voting age was 21 years.
- **Voters enrolled:** About 173 million voters were enrolled. This figure leaves out Jammu and Kashmir.
- **Share of the population:** The enrolled voters were 49 per cent of the population.
- **Women voters:** About 45 per cent of the enrolled voters were women.
- **What critics said:** Critics called the election 'a leap in the dark' and 'an act of faith'.

⚠ May be outdated: the voting age of 21 years. It was lowered to 18 by a constitutional amendment (1988).

### Problems in preparing the rolls

Sukumar Sen's report notes these problems.

- **Women's names:** Of about 80 million women, 2–3 million could not be registered. They would not disclose their proper names. They had been enrolled as 'wife of' or 'mother of' someone.
- **Where this happened:** Most of these women were from Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Bharat, Rajasthan and Vindhya Pradesh.
- **Displaced persons:** Displaced persons were hard to register in West Bengal, Punjab and Delhi, and partly in Assam.
- **Parties:** The parties gave little help with the rolls.

## Parties and symbols

### Parties and candidates

About 178 parties sprang up.

| Party | Leader | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bharatiya Jana Sangh (BJS) | Shyama Prasad Mookerjee | Founded the party in October 1951. |
| Scheduled Castes Federation | Ambedkar | Revived the party. |
| Kisan Mazdoor Praja Party (KMPP) | J.B. Kripalani | Founded the party. |
| Socialist Party | Lohia and Jayaprakash Narayan (JP) | Led the party. |

- **Parties in the contest:** 53 parties contested the Lok Sabha election. Of these, 14 were national parties.
- **Candidates:** There were 1,874 candidates. Of these, 533 were independents.

### Symbols

- **Why symbols:** Over 70 per cent of the voters were illiterate. So party symbols were allotted.
- **Ballot boxes:** The symbols were painted on the ballot boxes. There was one box for each candidate.

⚠ May be outdated: symbols on ballot boxes / ballot papers. Polling now uses electronic voting machines.

## Polling arrangements

The first phase of polling began only on 15 October 1951.

- **Polling booths:** There were over 224,000 polling booths.
- **Ballot boxes:** Over 2 million steel ballot boxes were used.
- **Officials:** About a million officials worked on the election.
- **Indelible ink:** Indelible ink was introduced to stop impersonation (voting in another person's name).
- **Voting system:** Voting was by the first-past-the-post system. In it the candidate with the most votes wins.
- **Turnout:** The turnout was 45.7 per cent.
- **Invalid votes:** Only 3–4 per cent of the votes were invalid.
- **Women:** About 40 per cent of the eligible women voted.

## Results

### Lok Sabha

| Party | Seats won |
| --- | --- |
| Congress | 364 |
| Communist Party of India (CPI) | 16 |
| Socialist Party | 12 |
| KMPP | 9 |
| BJS | 3 |

- **Congress:** The Congress contested 472 seats. It got close to 45 per cent of the vote.
- **Other parties:** The CPI and the Socialist Party were the only other parties in double digits.
- **Independents:** The independents came second. They won the most seats after the Congress.

### Notable contests

- **Ambedkar:** He lost Bombay (North Central) to Narayan Sadoba Kajrolkar.
- **J.B. Kripalani:** He lost Faizabad.
- **Sucheta Kripalani:** She won in Delhi. She defeated Manmohini Sahgal.

### The first Lok Sabha

- **2 April 1952:** The first Lok Sabha was constituted. Ganesh Vasudev (G.V.) Mavalankar became the Speaker. Nehru became the Prime Minister.

### State assemblies

- **Seats:** The Congress won 2,248 seats in the states.
- **No majority of its own:** The Congress lacked a majority of its own only in Madras, Travancore-Cochin, Orissa and PEPSU.
