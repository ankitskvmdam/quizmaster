## Gist
*Book pages 628–634.*

The provisions on citizenship and **Article 324** (Election Commission) came into force on **November 26, 1949**, the rest of the Constitution on **January 26, 1950**. Electoral procedure is in **Part XV (Articles 324–329)**. The Election Commission office was set up on **January 25, 1950**; the **first Chief Election Commissioner, Sukumar Sen** (an ICS officer), took office on **March 21, 1950**. For long the commission was a **single-member** body. Under Article 324 it superintends, directs and controls elections to Parliament, state legislatures and the offices of President and Vice-President.

Two laws made the polls possible: the **Representation of the People Act, 1950** (voter qualifications, electoral rolls, allocation of seats) and the **Representation of the People Act, 1951** (qualifications/disqualifications of members, conduct of elections, poll expenses, counting). The first phase began only on **October 15, 1951**. Of **489** elective Lok Sabha seats, **72** were reserved for SCs and **26** for STs; state assemblies had **3,283** seats (477 SC, 192 ST).

Voting was by **universal adult franchise at 21 years**. About **173 million** voters were enrolled (excluding Jammu and Kashmir), about **45 per cent** of them women — **49 per cent** of the population. Critics called it **'a leap in the dark'** and **'an act of faith'**. Sen's report notes that **2–3 million of about 80 million women** could not be registered because they would not disclose their **proper names** (enrolled as 'wife of' or 'mother of'); most were from **Bihar**, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Bharat, Rajasthan and Vindhya Pradesh. **Displaced persons** in West Bengal, Punjab, Delhi and Assam were hard to register, and parties gave little help with the rolls.

With **over 70 per cent** of voters illiterate, **symbols** were allotted; they were painted on **ballot boxes**, one box for each candidate. About **178 parties** sprang up. **Shyama Prasad Mookerjee** founded the **Bharatiya Jana Sangh (October 1951)**; **Ambedkar** revived the **Scheduled Castes Federation**; **J.B. Kripalani** founded the **Kisan Mazdoor Praja Party**; **Lohia and JP** led the **Socialist Party**. **53 parties** (14 national) and **1,874 candidates** (533 independents) contested.

There were **401 constituencies** (314 single-seat, 86 two-seat, 1 three-seat), **over 224,000 polling booths**, **over 2 million steel ballot boxes**, about **a million officials**, and **indelible ink** against impersonation. Voting was **first-past-the-post**. Turnout was **45.7 per cent**; invalid votes only **3–4 per cent**; about **40 per cent** of eligible women voted.

The **Congress won 364 of 472 seats contested** with close to **45 per cent** of the vote; **CPI (16)** and the **Socialist Party (12)** were the only other parties in double digits; **independents** came second. **Ambedkar lost Bombay (North Central)** to **Narayan Sadoba Kajrolkar**; **Kripalani lost Faizabad**, but **Sucheta Kripalani** won in Delhi. The **first Lok Sabha was constituted on April 2, 1952**, with **G.V. Mavalankar** as Speaker and **Nehru** as Prime Minister. In the states the Congress won **2,248** seats, lacking a majority of its own only in **Madras, Travancore-Cochin, Orissa and PEPSU**.

## Key facts
- Citizenship provisions and **Article 324** came into force on **November 26, 1949**.
- Electoral provisions: **Part XV, Articles 324–329**.
- Election Commission office set up **January 25, 1950**; first CEC **Sukumar Sen** (ICS) took office **March 21, 1950**.
- **RPA 1950** — voter qualifications, electoral rolls, seat allocation; **RPA 1951** — conduct of elections, disqualifications, poll expenses, counting.
- First phase of polling began **October 15, 1951**.
- Lok Sabha: **489** elective seats; **72 SC**, **26 ST** reserved. State assemblies: **3,283** seats (**477 SC, 192 ST**).
- Voting age **21**; about **173 million** voters (excluding J&K); about **45 per cent** women; **49 per cent** of the population enrolled.
- 2–3 million women not registered for not giving **proper names** — mostly in **Bihar**, UP, Madhya Bharat, Rajasthan, Vindhya Pradesh.
- Displaced persons hard to register in **West Bengal, Punjab, Delhi** and partly **Assam**.
- **Over 70 per cent** of voters illiterate, hence **party symbols**, painted on **ballot boxes**.
- About **178** parties formed; **53** contested the Lok Sabha, including **14 national parties**; **1,874** candidates, **533** independents.
- **Bharatiya Jana Sangh** — **Shyama Prasad Mookerjee**, **October 1951**; **KMPP** — **J.B. Kripalani**; **Socialist Party** — **Lohia and JP**.
- **401** constituencies: **314** single-seat, **86** two-seat, **1** three-seat.
- Over **224,000** polling booths; over **2 million** steel ballot boxes; about **a million** officials; **indelible ink** introduced.
- **First-past-the-post** system; turnout **45.7 per cent**; invalid votes **3–4 per cent**.
- Congress contested **472**, won **364** seats, about **45 per cent** of votes; **CPI 16**, **Socialist Party 12**, **KMPP 9**, **BJS 3**.
- **Independents** won the most seats after the Congress.
- **Ambedkar** lost Bombay (North Central) to **Narayan Sadoba Kajrolkar**; **Sucheta Kripalani** defeated Manmohini Sahgal in Delhi.
- **First Lok Sabha** constituted **April 2, 1952**; Speaker **Ganesh Vasudev Mavalankar**.
- Congress lacked a majority on its own in **Madras, Travancore-Cochin, Orissa, PEPSU**; won **2,248** state seats.
- ⚠ May be outdated: voting age **21 years** — it was lowered to 18 by a constitutional amendment (1988).
- ⚠ May be outdated: Election Commission as a **single-member** body — it has been a multi-member commission since the 1990s.
- ⚠ May be outdated: symbols on ballot boxes / ballot papers — polling now uses electronic voting machines.
