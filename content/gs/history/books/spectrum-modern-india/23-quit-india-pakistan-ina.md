## Why a struggle was launched

The Cripps Mission had failed. After this the Congress moved towards a struggle for these reasons:

1. Britain's attitude was unchanged.
2. Prices were rising.
3. The British suffered reverses in South-East Asia.
4. The evacuation from South-East Asia was racist. There was a 'Black Road' for Indians and a 'White Road' for Europeans.
5. The masses had to be prepared for a Japanese invasion.

## Quit India Movement

### Launch

1. **14 July 1942:** The Congress Working Committee (CWC) met at Wardha. It accepted the idea of a struggle.
2. **8 August 1942:** The Quit India resolution was ratified at Gowalia Tank, Bombay. It was proposed by Nehru and seconded by Sardar Patel.
3. Gandhi was named the leader. He gave the mantra 'Do or Die'. Sumit Sarkar saw it as Gandhi's militant mood.
4. **9 August 1942:** All the top leaders were arrested early in the day. Congress bodies were declared unlawful under the Criminal Law Amendment Act, 1908. Meetings were banned under rule 56 of the Defence of India Rules.
5. On the same day Aruna Asaf Ali presided over the Congress session and hoisted the flag.

### Spread

- There was violence against the symbols of authority. This public violence was most intense in eastern UP and Bihar.
- The main storm centres were eastern UP, Bihar, Midnapore, Maharashtra and Karnataka.
- There was a complete absence of anti-zamindar violence.

### Underground leaders

- The underground leaders were Rammanohar Lohia, Jayaprakash Narayan, Aruna Asaf Ali, Usha Mehta, Biju Patnaik, Achyut Patwardhan and Sucheta Kripalani.
- Usha Mehta started an underground radio in Bombay.

### Parallel governments

| Place | Name and leaders | Period |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ballia | Under Chittu Pandey | August 1942, for a week |
| Tamluk, Midnapore | Jatiya Sarkar | December 1942 to September 1944 |
| Satara | Prati Sarkar, under Y.B. Chavan and Nana Patil | Mid-1943 to 1945 |

### Who stayed out

- The Communists stayed out.
- The Muslim League opposed the movement.
- The Hindu Mahasabha boycotted it.

### Repression

- About 10,000 people were killed in the repression.
- Martial law was not applied.

### Gandhi's fast

- **February 1943:** Gandhi went on a fast. It was directed against the violence of the State.
- Three members of the viceroy's executive council resigned.

## Bengal famine of 1943

The famine of 1943 killed 1.5 to 3 million people. It was largely man-made, for three reasons:

1. Food went to the army.
2. Rice imports from Burma stopped.
3. Supplies were mismanaged.

The worst areas included Tamluk-Contai-Diamond Harbour, Dacca, Faridpur, Tippera and Noakhali.

## Demand for Pakistan and the three proposals

Pakistan Day was observed on 23 March 1943.

### C. Rajagopalachari formula (1944)

- The formula proposed a plebiscite (a direct vote) after the war. The entire population of the Muslim-majority areas was to vote in this post-war plebiscite.
- Gandhi supported the formula.
- Jinnah wanted only Muslims to vote. He also rejected a common centre.
- Hindu leaders under Vir Savarkar condemned it.

### Desai–Liaqat Pact

- The pact is named after Bhulabhai Desai and Liaqat Ali Khan. It is also called the Desai–Liaqat proposal.
- It offered parity between Congress and League nominees. That is, the two would have equal nominees.
- It offered 20% seats for minorities.

### Wavell Plan

- **June 1945:** The plan was discussed at the Shimla Conference.
- It proposed a council in which all members were Indian, except the governor-general and the commander-in-chief.
- Caste Hindus and Muslims were to have equal representation.
- The League demanded to nominate all the Muslim members.
- Wavell wanted Khizr Hyat Khan as the Muslim representative from Western Punjab.
- Wavell announced that the talks had broken down. This gave the League a virtual veto.

## Subhash Bose and the INA

### Bose before 1941

1. Subhash Bose stood fourth in the ICS. He resigned in 1921.
2. His guru was Chittaranjan Das (C.R. Das).
3. **1923:** He became the mayor of Calcutta.
4. **March 1940:** He held an Anti-Compromise Conference at Ramgarh. It was a conference of the Forward Bloc and the Kisan Sabha. A struggle was to begin from 6 April, in the National Week.

### Escape and Germany

1. **26 January 1941:** Bose escaped and reached Peshawar as 'Ziauddin'. Bhagat Ram helped him.
2. He then met Hitler as 'Orlando Mazzotta'.
3. The Freedom Army (Mukti Sena) had its office at Dresden.
4. The slogan 'Jai Hind' came from the Free India Centre, Germany.

### The INA

1. The INA was originally the idea of Mohan Singh.
2. **1942:** Rashbehari Bose founded the Indian Independence League at Tokyo.
3. **September 1942:** The first division of the INA was formed with 16,300 men. The Japanese wanted only 2,000.
4. **July 1943:** Rashbehari Bose handed the League and the INA to Subhash.
5. **25 August:** Subhash became the Supreme Commander.
6. **21 October 1943:** The Provisional Government of Free India was formed at Singapore.
7. Bose gave the call "Give me blood, I will give you freedom" in Malaya.
8. **6 November 1943:** Andaman and Nicobar were renamed Shahid Dweep and Swaraj Dweep.
9. **January 1944:** The INA headquarters moved to Rangoon. The war cry was "Chalo Delhi!".
10. **18 March 1944:** The INA stood on Indian soil.
11. **14 April 1944:** Colonel Malik hoisted the INA flag at Moirang, Manipur.
12. Shah Nawaz's battalion fought in the Imphal campaign.
13. **6 July 1944:** Bose called Gandhi 'Father of Nation' on Azad Hind Radio.
14. **15 August 1945:** Japan surrendered.
15. **18 August 1945:** Bose reportedly died in an air crash at Taipei.
