## Gist
*Book pages 585–589.*

Modern industry entered India in the second half of the 19th century. The thousands employed in **railway construction** were the **harbingers of the modern Indian working class**; then came coal, cotton and jute. Workers faced low wages, long hours, hazardous conditions and child labour. Because of colonialism they faced **two antagonistic forces** — **imperialist political rule** and **exploitation by both foreign and native capitalists** — so their movement became **intertwined with the national struggle**.

**Early efforts:** the **Moderates** were indifferent to labour, distinguished between workers in Indian-owned and British-owned factories, feared labour laws would hurt Indian industry, and **did not support the Factory Acts of 1881 and 1891**. Early efforts were philanthropic: **Sasipada Banerjea** (1870, workingmen's club, **Bharat Shramjeevi**); **Sorabjee Shapoorji Bengalee** (1878, bill in the **Bombay Legislative Council**); **Narain Meghajee Lokhanday** (1880, **Deenbandhu**, **Bombay Mill and Millhands Association**). The **first strike of the Great Indian Peninsular Railways (1899)** was backed by **Tilak's Kesari and Maharatta**.

**Swadeshi upsurge:** strikes in the government press, railways and jute industry were organised by **Ashwini Coomar Banerjea**, Prabhat Kumar Roy Chaudhuri, Premtosh Bose and Apurba Kumar Ghosh; **Subramaniya Siva and Chidambaram Pillai** led strikes in **Tuticorin and Tirunelvelli**. The **biggest strike** of the period followed **Tilak's arrest and trial**.

**After the First World War:** high prices and low wages, Gandhi's mass movement, and the **Soviet republic, the Comintern and the ILO** gave the movement a new dimension. The **AITUC** was founded on **October 31, 1920**, with **Lala Lajpat Rai** (Congress president that year) as **first president** and **Dewan Chaman Lal** as **first general secretary**. Lajpat Rai first linked capitalism with imperialism. **C.R. Das** presided over the **third and fourth AITUC sessions**, and the **Gaya session of the Congress (1922)** welcomed the AITUC. Gandhi helped organise the **Ahmedabad Textile Labour Association (1918)**, winning a **27.5%** wage hike (later **35%** by arbitration).

The **Trade Union Act, 1926** recognised trade unions as **legal associations** with civil and criminal immunity for legitimate activities. Communist influence grew: a **six-month Bombay textile strike (1928)** was led by the **Girni Kamgar Union**. The government responded with the **Public Safety Ordinance (1929)** and the **Trade Disputes Act, 1929**, and the **Meerut Conspiracy Case (March 1929, 31 leaders arrested)** weakened the movement. In **1931** **N.M. Joshi's** corporatist group left to form the **All India Trade Union Federation**; communists rejoined the AITUC in **1935**.

The AITUC backed Congress candidates in **1937**. After **Russia joined the Allies (1941)** the communists called it a **'people's war'** and kept away from **Quit India**. In **1945** Bombay and Calcutta dock workers refused to load ships for troops in **Indonesia**; in **1946** workers struck in support of the **Naval Ratings**.

## Key facts
- Workers on **railway construction** — harbingers of the modern Indian working class.
- Indian workers faced **two antagonistic forces**: **imperialist political rule** and **economic exploitation by foreign and native capitalists**.
- The Moderates **did not support the Factory Acts of 1881 and 1891**.
- **1870** — **Sasipada Banerjea**: workingmen's club and newspaper **Bharat Shramjeevi**.
- **1878** — **Sorabjee Shapoorji Bengalee** tried to get a labour-welfare bill passed in the **Bombay Legislative Council**.
- **1880** — **Narain Meghajee Lokhanday**: newspaper **Deenbandhu** and the **Bombay Mill and Millhands Association**.
- **1899** — **first strike by the Great Indian Peninsular Railways**; **Tilak's Kesari and Maharatta** campaigned for it.
- **Bipin Chandra Pal** and **G. Subramanya Aiyar** demanded better conditions for workers.
- Swadeshi-era strikes (government press, railways, jute) organised by **Ashwini Coomar Banerjea, Prabhat Kumar Roy Chaudhuri, Premtosh Bose, Apurba Kumar Ghosh**.
- **Subramaniya Siva and Chidambaram Pillai** led strikes in **Tuticorin and Tirunelvelli**.
- The **biggest strike** of the Swadeshi period came after **Tilak's arrest and trial**.
- International influences: **socialist republic in the Soviet Union, the Comintern, the ILO**.
- **AITUC** founded **October 31, 1920**; first president **Lala Lajpat Rai**; first general secretary **Dewan Chaman Lal**.
- Lajpat Rai: **"imperialism and militarism are the twin children of capitalism"**.
- **C.R. Das** presided over the **third and fourth** AITUC sessions.
- **Gaya session of the Congress (1922)** welcomed the AITUC and formed a committee to assist it.
- Early AITUC was influenced by the **social democratic ideas of the British Labour Party**.
- **Ahmedabad Textile Labour Association (1918)** — Gandhi; **27.5%** wage hike, later **35%** by the arbitrator's award.
- **Trade Union Act, 1926**: trade unions recognised as **legal associations**; registration rules; **civil and criminal immunity** for legitimate activities; some curbs on political activity.
- **1928** — **six-month strike in Bombay textile mills** led by the **Girni Kamgar Union**.
- **Public Safety Ordinance (1929)** and **Trade Disputes Act, 1929**.
- TDA 1929: compulsory **Courts of Inquiry and Consultation Boards**; public-utility strikes illegal without **one month's advance notice** by each worker; banned coercive or purely political union activity and **sympathetic strikes**.
- **Meerut Conspiracy Case** — **March 1929**, **31 labour leaders** arrested; **three-and-a-half-year** trial; convicted included **Muzaffar Ahmed, S.A. Dange, Joglekar, Philip Spratt, Ben Bradley, Shaukat Usmani**.
- **1931** split — **N.M. Joshi's** corporatist trend formed the **All India Trade Union Federation**; communists rejoined the AITUC in **1935**.
- **1937** elections — AITUC supported **Congress** candidates.
- After **1941** (Russia joined the Allies) communists called it a **"people's war"** and **dissociated from the Quit India Movement**.
- **1945** — dock workers of **Bombay and Calcutta** refused to load ships for troops in **Indonesia**; **1946** — strike in support of the **Naval Ratings**.
