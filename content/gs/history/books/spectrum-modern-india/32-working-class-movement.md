## Background: the rise of the working class

1. Modern industry entered India in the second half of the 19th century.
2. Thousands were employed in railway construction. They were the harbingers of the modern Indian working class.
3. Coal, cotton and jute came next.
4. The workers faced low wages, long hours, hazardous conditions and child labour.
5. Because of colonialism the Indian workers faced two antagonistic forces. One was imperialist political rule. The other was economic exploitation by both foreign and native capitalists.
6. So the workers' movement became intertwined with the national struggle.

## Early efforts (1870–1899)

### Attitude of the Moderates

- The Moderates were indifferent to labour.
- They distinguished between workers in Indian-owned factories and workers in British-owned factories.
- They feared that labour laws would hurt Indian industry.
- They did not support the Factory Acts of 1881 and 1891.

### Philanthropic efforts

The early efforts were philanthropic.

- Bipin Chandra Pal and G. Subramanya Aiyar demanded better conditions for workers.

## Swadeshi upsurge

- Strikes took place in the government press, the railways and the jute industry. They were organised by Ashwini Coomar Banerjea, Prabhat Kumar Roy Chaudhuri, Premtosh Bose and Apurba Kumar Ghosh.
- Subramaniya Siva and Chidambaram Pillai led strikes in Tuticorin and Tirunelvelli.
- The biggest strike of the Swadeshi period came after Tilak's arrest and trial.

## After the First World War

### What gave the movement a new dimension

1. High prices and low wages.
2. Gandhi's mass movement.
3. International influences: the socialist republic in the Soviet Union, the Comintern and the International Labour Organisation (ILO).

### Ahmedabad Textile Labour Association (1918)

- Gandhi helped organise it.
- It won a wage hike of 27.5%.
- Later the arbitrator's award raised this to 35%.

### All India Trade Union Congress

1. **31 October 1920:** The AITUC was founded. Its first president was Lala Lajpat Rai, who was Congress president that year. Its first general secretary was Dewan Chaman Lal.
2. Lajpat Rai was the first to link capitalism with imperialism. He said: "imperialism and militarism are the twin children of capitalism".
3. C.R. Das presided over the third and fourth sessions of the AITUC.
4. **1922:** The Gaya session of the Congress welcomed the AITUC. It formed a committee to assist it.
5. The early AITUC was influenced by the social democratic ideas of the British Labour Party.

## Laws, strikes and repression (1926–1929)

### Trade Union Act, 1926

- It recognised trade unions as legal associations.
- It laid down rules for registration.
- It gave civil and criminal immunity for legitimate activities.
- It put some curbs on political activity.

### Bombay textile strike (1928)

- Communist influence in the movement grew.
- **1928:** A six-month strike took place in the Bombay textile mills. The Girni Kamgar Union led it.

### Government response (1929)

The government responded with the Public Safety Ordinance (1929) and the Trade Disputes Act, 1929. The Trade Disputes Act laid down four things:

- Courts of Inquiry and Consultation Boards were made compulsory.
- A strike in a public utility was illegal unless each worker gave one month's advance notice.
- Coercive or purely political union activity was banned.
- Sympathetic strikes were banned.

### Meerut Conspiracy Case (March 1929)

- **March 1929:** 31 labour leaders were arrested.
- The trial lasted three and a half years.
- Those convicted included Muzaffar Ahmed, S.A. Dange, Joglekar, Philip Spratt, Ben Bradley and Shaukat Usmani.
- The case weakened the movement.

## Split, elections and the War years (1931–1946)

1. **1931:** The AITUC split. The corporatist group of N.M. Joshi left and formed the All India Trade Union Federation.
2. **1935:** The communists rejoined the AITUC.
3. **1937:** The AITUC supported the Congress candidates in the elections.
4. **1941:** Russia joined the Allies. After this the communists called the War a 'people's war'. They dissociated themselves from the Quit India Movement.
5. **1945:** The dock workers of Bombay and Calcutta refused to load ships meant for troops in Indonesia.
6. **1946:** Workers struck in support of the Naval Ratings.
