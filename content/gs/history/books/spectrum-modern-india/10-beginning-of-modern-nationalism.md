## Background: how the book sees Indian nationalism

The book sees Indian nationalism as partly a result of colonial policies and partly a reaction to them. It names a mix of four factors:

1. The worldwide upsurge of nationalism and self-determination. The ideas of the French Revolution initiated it.
2. The Indian Renaissance.
3. An offshoot of British modernisation.
4. A strong reaction to British imperialist policies.

People also realised that colonial rule was the major cause of India's economic backwardness.

## Factors in the growth of nationalism

### Unification of the country

- The British created a larger state than that of the Mauryas or the great Mughals.
- The provinces were under direct rule. The princely states were under indirect rule.
- The British set up a professional civil service, a unified judiciary and codified laws.
- They built railways, roads, electricity and the telegraph. These were built for administrative convenience, defence and economic exploitation.

This unification had a two-fold effect:

1. The economic fate of the regions got linked. A crop failure in one region affected prices elsewhere.
2. The leaders of different regions were brought together.

### Western education and the middle class

- Western education spread the ideas of European writers. They were Milton, Shelley, John Stuart Mill (J.S. Mill), Rousseau, Paine, Spencer and Voltaire.
- English became a link language.
- The English-educated middle class intelligentsia (educated class) became the nucleus of political unrest.
- Percival Spear described the new middle class as "a well-integrated all-India class... a dynamic minority".
- This class led the Indian National Congress (INC) in all its stages.

### The press

- The press grew in the second half of the 19th century.
- **1877:** There were about 169 vernacular (Indian-language) newspapers. Their circulation was near 1,00,000.

### Rediscovery of India's past

- **European scholars:** Max Mueller, Monier Williams, Roth and Sassoon.
- **Indian scholars:** R.G. Bhandarkar and R.L. Mitra. Swami Vivekananda came later.

### Movements abroad

- New nations rose from the ruins of the Spanish and Portuguese empires in South America.
- The liberation movements of Greece and Italy inspired the nationalists.
- The movement of Ireland inspired them especially.

### Lytton's measures and the Ilbert Bill

Lytton's reactionary measures provoked opposition.

1. **1876:** The maximum age for the ICS (Indian Civil Service) was cut from 21 to 19.
2. **1877:** The Delhi Durbar was held during a famine.
3. **1878:** The Vernacular Press Act and the Arms Act were passed.

The Ilbert Bill came under Ripon's government.

1. The Bill sought to abolish "judicial disqualification based on race distinctions".
2. The European community opposed it. Because of this European opposition, the Bill was modified.
3. The European agitation taught the nationalists how to agitate for their rights.

## Political associations before the Congress

The associations changed with time.

- **Early 19th century:** Wealthy and aristocratic elements dominated the associations. They were local or regional. They used long petitions to the British Parliament.
- **Later:** The educated middle class dominated the associations.

### Bengal

1. **1836:** Associates of Raja Rammohan Roy formed the Bangabhasha Prakasika Sabha.
2. The Landholders' Society was also called the Zamindari Association. It marked the beginning of organised political activity and of constitutional agitation.
3. **1843:** The Bengal British India Society was formed.
4. **1851:** The Landholders' Society and the Bengal British India Society merged. The new body was the British Indian Association.
5. **1875:** Sisir Kumar Ghosh formed the Indian League. It aimed at "stimulating the sense of nationalism".
6. **1876:** Surendranath Banerjea and Ananda Mohan Bose formed the Indian Association of Calcutta. It was also called the Indian National Association. It superseded the Indian League. It was the most important pre-Congress association.
7. **28–30 December 1883:** The Indian Association held an all-India conference in Calcutta. More than 100 delegates came.
8. **1886:** The Indian Association merged with the INC.

The British Indian Association sent a petition with four demands:

1. A separate popular legislature.
2. Separation of executive and judicial functions.
3. Reduction in the salaries of higher officers.
4. Abolition of the salt duty, abkari (excise on liquor) and stamp duties.

The Charter Act of 1853 partly met these demands. It added six members to the governor-general's council for legislation.

The Indian Association worked in three ways:

- It demanded simultaneous ICS exams in England and India.
- It demanded Indianisation of higher posts.
- It kept its membership fee low to attract the poorer sections.

### London, Bombay and Madras

1. **1866:** Dadabhai Naoroji formed the East India Association in London.
2. **1867:** Mahadeo Govind Ranade (M.G. Ranade) and others formed the Poona Sarvajanik Sabha. It was a bridge between the government and the people. Book inconsistency: the text says 1867, but the chapter summary says 1870. Verify.
3. **1884:** The Madras Mahajan Sabha was formed. Its founders were M. Viraraghavachari, B. Subramaniya Aiyer and P. Anandacharlu.
4. **1885:** The Bombay Presidency Association was formed. Its founders were Badruddin Tyabji, Pherozshah Mehta and K.T. Telang.

## Campaigns before the Congress

The associations ran these campaigns:

- **1875:** for an import duty on cotton.
- **1878–79:** for Indianisation of the services.
- against Lytton's Afghan adventure.
- against the Arms Act.
- against the Vernacular Press Act.
- for the right to join the volunteer corps.
- against the Inland Emigration Act.
- in support of the Ilbert Bill.
- the Indian Civil Service agitation.
