## Gist
*Book pages 239–246.*

The book sees Indian nationalism as partly a result of, and partly a reaction to, colonial policies — a mix of four factors: the **worldwide upsurge of nationalism and self-determination initiated by the French Revolution**, the **Indian Renaissance**, an **offshoot of British modernisation**, and a **strong reaction to British imperialist policies**.

People realised that **colonial rule was the major cause of India's economic backwardness**. The British created a **larger state than the Mauryas or the great Mughals**; provinces were under **direct** rule and princely states under **indirect** rule. A professional civil service, unified judiciary and codified laws, plus railways, roads, electricity and telegraph (built for administrative convenience, defence and economic exploitation), had a two-fold effect: the **economic fate of regions got linked** and leaders from different regions were **brought together**.

Western education spread ideas of **Milton, Shelley, J.S. Mill, Rousseau, Paine, Spencer and Voltaire**; English became a link language, and the English-educated **middle class intelligentsia** became the nucleus of political unrest. The press grew in the **second half of the 19th century**: in **1877** about **169 vernacular newspapers** had a circulation near **1,00,000**. Research by **Max Mueller, Monier Williams, Roth, Sassoon, R.G. Bhandarkar, R.L. Mitra** and later **Swami Vivekananda** rediscovered India's past. **Percival Spear** called the new middle class "a dynamic minority"; it led the INC in all its stages. Liberation movements of **Greece, Italy and especially Ireland** inspired nationalists.

**Lytton's** reactionary measures — cutting the **ICS maximum age from 21 to 19 (1876)**, the **Delhi Durbar of 1877** during famine, the **Vernacular Press Act and Arms Act (1878)** — provoked opposition. Under **Ripon**, the **Ilbert Bill** sought to abolish "judicial disqualification based on race distinctions" but was modified under **European opposition**, which also **taught nationalists how to agitate**.

Early political associations were dominated by **wealthy, aristocratic elements** and were local; later ones by the **educated middle class**. In Bengal: **Bangabhasha Prakasika Sabha (1836)** by associates of **Raja Rammohan Roy**; the **Landholders' Society (Zamindari Association)**, the beginning of organised political activity; **Bengal British India Society (1843)**; and the **British Indian Association (1851)** formed by their merger, whose demands were partly met by the **Charter Act of 1853** (six members added to the governor-general's council for legislation). **Dadabhai Naoroji** formed the **East India Association (1866, London)**; **Sisir Kumar Ghosh** the **Indian League (1875)**. The **Indian Association of Calcutta (1876)** under **Surendranath Banerjea and Ananda Mohan Bose** was the **most important pre-Congress association**; it held an **all-India conference in Calcutta, 28–30 December 1883**, and **merged with the INC in 1886**. In Bombay: **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha** (**M.G. Ranade**) and **Bombay Presidency Association (1885)**; in Madras: **Madras Mahajan Sabha (1884)**.

## Key facts
- Book's four factors behind nationalism: **French Revolution's ideas of nationalism and self-determination**, **Indian Renaissance**, **British modernisation**, **reaction to imperialist policies**.
- British Indian state was **larger than that of the Mauryas or the great Mughals**; provinces under **direct** rule, princely states under **indirect** rule.
- Unification's two-fold effect: **economic fate of regions linked** (crop failure in one region affected prices elsewhere) and **leaders of different regions brought together**.
- European writers who inspired Indians: **Milton, Shelley, John Stuart Mill, Rousseau, Paine, Spencer, Voltaire**.
- In **1877** there were about **169 vernacular newspapers** with circulation near **1,00,000**.
- Rediscovery of India's past: European scholars **Max Mueller, Monier Williams, Roth, Sassoon**; Indian scholars **R.G. Bhandarkar, R.L. Mitra**, later **Swami Vivekananda**.
- **Percival Spear** described the new middle class as "a well-integrated all-India class... a **dynamic minority**".
- Foreign influences: nations from the ruins of **Spanish and Portuguese empires in South America**; liberation movements of **Greece, Italy**, and **Ireland** in particular.
- **Lytton**: ICS maximum age cut from **21 to 19 (1876)**; **Delhi Durbar 1877** amid famine; **Vernacular Press Act (1878)** and **Arms Act (1878)**.
- **Ilbert Bill** (**Ripon's** government): to abolish **"judicial disqualification based on race distinctions"**; modified due to **European community's opposition**.
- European agitation against the Ilbert Bill **taught nationalists how to agitate** for rights.
- Early-19th-century associations: dominated by **wealthy and aristocratic elements**, **local/regional**, used **long petitions to the British Parliament**.
- **Bangabhasha Prakasika Sabha (1836)** — formed by associates of **Raja Rammohan Roy**.
- **Landholders' Society** = **Zamindari Association**; marked the **beginning of organised political activity** and constitutional agitation.
- **Bengal British India Society** — **1843**.
- **British Indian Association (1851)** — merger of **Landholders' Society** and **Bengal British India Society**.
- British Indian Association petition demands: separate **popular legislature**, **separation of executive and judicial** functions, **reduction in salaries of higher officers**, **abolition of salt duty, abkari and stamp duties**; partly met by the **Charter Act of 1853** (**six members** added to the governor-general's council for legislation).
- **East India Association** — **Dadabhai Naoroji, 1866, London**.
- **Indian League** — **Sisir Kumar Ghosh, 1875**; aimed at "stimulating the sense of nationalism".
- **Indian Association of Calcutta / Indian National Association (1876)** — **Surendranath Banerjea and Ananda Mohan Bose**; superseded the Indian League; **most important pre-Congress association**.
- Indian Association demanded **simultaneous ICS exams in England and India** and **Indianisation of higher posts**; kept **membership fee low** to attract poorer sections.
- Indian Association's **all-India conference: Calcutta, 28–30 December 1883**, 100+ delegates; **merged with INC in 1886**.
- **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha** — **Mahadeo Govind Ranade** and others; a **bridge between government and people** (text says **1867**; the chapter summary says 1870).
- **Bombay Presidency Association (1885)** — **Badruddin Tyabji, Pherozshah Mehta, K.T. Telang**.
- **Madras Mahajan Sabha (1884)** — **M. Viraraghavachari, B. Subramaniya Aiyer, P. Anandacharlu**.
- Pre-Congress campaigns included: **for import duty on cotton (1875)**, **Indianisation of services (1878–79)**, against **Lytton's Afghan adventure**, **Arms Act**, **Vernacular Press Act**, for **right to join volunteer corps**, against **Inland Emigration Act**, in **support of the Ilbert Bill**, and the **Indian Civil Service agitation**.
