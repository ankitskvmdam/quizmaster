## Background: the awakening of the 19th century

In the 19th century a new, modern vision grew among enlightened Indians. This reawakening is sometimes called the 'Renaissance', though not with full justification. The colonial presence occasioned it but did not create it.

Society was weighed down by these ills:

- Superstition and priestly domination.
- Idolatry and polytheism.
- A degraded position of women: female infanticide, child marriage, polygamy, Kulinism in Bengal and sati.
- A rigid caste system, with untouchability at its base.

## The base of the reform movements

### Social base

- The social base was the newly emerging middle class and the educated intellectuals.
- Its roots lay not in trade or industry. They lay in government service and the professions: law, education, journalism and medicine.

### Ideological base

The ideological base was rationalism, religious universalism and humanism.

- **Akshay Kumar Dutt:** He declared that "rationalism is our only preceptor". He also used medical opinion against child marriage.
- **Brahmo Samaj:** It repudiated the infallibility of the Vedas.
- **Raja Rammohan Roy:** He defended the monotheism of the Vedas and the unitarianism of Christianity. He attacked Hindu polytheism and Christian trinitarianism.
- **Syed Ahmed Khan:** He held that religious tenets were not immutable. He said all prophets had the same 'din' (faith).

### Two streams

| Stream | Movements |
| --- | --- |
| Reformist | Brahmo Samaj, Prarthana Samaj, Aligarh Movement |
| Revivalist | Arya Samaj, Deoband |

### The agenda of social reform

Social reform was tied to religious reform. Social ills like untouchability and gender inequity derived legitimacy from religion.

Social reform had a two-point agenda:

1. The status of women.
2. The removal of untouchability.

## The status of women

### Sati

- Raja Rammohan Roy called sati a "murder according to every shastra".
- **1829:** Regulation XVII of the Bengal Code banned sati as culpable homicide.
- **1830:** The ban was extended to Madras and Bombay.

### Female infanticide

- **1795 and 1804:** The Bengal Regulations of these years declared female infanticide illegal.
- **1870:** An Act made the registration of all births compulsory.

### Widow remarriage

- **1850s:** Vishnu Shastri Pandit founded the Widow Remarriage Association.
- **1852:** Karsondas Mulji started Satya Prakash in Gujarati to advocate widow remarriage.
- **1856:** The Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act was passed. It was mainly due to Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (1820–91), the principal of Sanskrit College, Calcutta.
- **1893:** D.K. Karve married a widow. He opened a widows' home in Poona.

### Marriage laws

- **1872:** The Native Marriage Act was passed. It is also called the Civil Marriage Act. Its impact was limited, as it did not apply to Hindus, Muslims and other recognised faiths.
- **1891:** The Age of Consent Act came from the efforts of the Parsi reformer B.M. Malabari. It forbade the marriage of girls below 12.
- **Sarda Act:** It raised the marriage age to 18 for boys and 14 for girls. The book dates it 1930 on pp. 197 and 205, and 1929 on p. 199.
- **1978:** The Child Marriage Restraint (Amendment) Act raised the age for girls from 15 to 18. It raised the age for boys from 18 to 21.

⚠ May be outdated: marriage ages under the 1978 Act — the old child marriage law has since been replaced by the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006, and raising the girls' age further has been proposed.

### Women's education

- **1819:** Christian missionaries set up the Calcutta Female Juvenile Society. It was the first in women's education.
- **1849:** J.E.D. Bethune founded the Bethune School.
- **1854:** Wood's Despatch was another step for women's education.
- Vidyasagar was linked with no less than 35 girls' schools in Bengal.
- **1880s:** The Dufferin Hospitals came up.
- **1916:** Lady Hardinge Medical College opened in Delhi. In the same year Karve set up the Indian Women's University.

### Women's organisations

| Organisation | People | Key facts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ladies Social Conference (Bharat Mahila Parishad), 1904, Bombay | Ramabai Ranade | It was under the National Social Conference. |

Sarojini Naidu became president of the INC (Indian National Congress) in 1925. She was governor of the United Provinces (1947–49).

## The removal of untouchability

### What weakened caste

1. **British-era forces:** private property in land, equality before law, open services and secular education.
2. **Reform bodies:** They worked against caste rigidity.
3. **The national movement:** It also weakened caste rigidity.
4. **Gandhi:** He founded the All India Harijan Sangh in 1932.

The rulers of Travancore, Indore and Devas opened all state temples by proclamation.

### Leaders and movements

- **Jyotiba Phule:** He came from a Mali family. He led a movement against brahminical domination.
- **Sri Narayana Guru:** He gave the slogan "one religion, one caste, one God for mankind". His disciple Sahadaran Ayyapan said: "no religion, no caste, no God for mankind".
- **1920s:** E.V. Ramaswamy Naicker led the Self-Respect Movement.
- **1924:** Ambedkar founded the Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha. Its motto was 'Educate, Agitate and Organise'.
- **March 1927:** Ambedkar led the Mahad Satyagraha. About 2,500 untouchables went to the Chawdar tank.
- **December 1927:** The Manusmriti was burnt there.
- Ambedkar organised the All India Scheduled Castes Federation.
- **1935:** The Government of India Act gave special representation to the depressed classes.
