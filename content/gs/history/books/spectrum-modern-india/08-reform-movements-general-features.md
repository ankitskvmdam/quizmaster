## Gist
*Book pages 188–205.*

The 19th century saw a new, modern vision among enlightened Indians, a reawakening sometimes (not with full justification) called the **'Renaissance'**. The **colonial presence occasioned it but did not create it**. Society was weighed down by superstition, priestly domination, **idolatry and polytheism**, a degraded position of women (female infanticide, child marriage, polygamy, **Kulinism in Bengal**, sati) and a rigid caste system with untouchability at its base. **Raja Rammohan Roy** called sati a **"murder according to every shastra"**.

The **social base** was the new **middle class** and educated intellectuals, whose roots lay not in trade or industry but in **government service and the professions (law, education, journalism, medicine)**. The **ideological base** was **rationalism, religious universalism and humanism**. **Akshay Kumar Dutt** declared **"rationalism is our only preceptor"**; the Brahmo Samaj repudiated the infallibility of the Vedas; **Syed Ahmed Khan** held that religious tenets were not immutable and that all prophets had the same **'din'**. The movements fell into two streams: **reformist (Brahmo Samaj, Prarthana Samaj, Aligarh Movement)** and **revivalist (Arya Samaj, Deoband)**.

Social reform had a two-point agenda: the **status of women** and the **removal of untouchability**. Key measures: **Regulation XVII of 1829** (Bengal Code) banned **sati**, extended to Madras and Bombay in **1830**; **Bengal Regulations of 1795 and 1804** outlawed female infanticide, and an **1870 Act** made birth registration compulsory; **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**, principal of Sanskrit College, Calcutta, secured the **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856**. The **Native Marriage Act, 1872** had limited impact; **B.M. Malabari's** efforts produced the **Age of Consent Act (1891)** (no marriage of girls below **12**); the **Sarda Act** raised the age to **18 (boys) and 14 (girls)**. **D.K. Karve** married a widow in **1893** and set up the **Indian Women's University (1916)**.

For women's education: **Calcutta Female Juvenile Society (1819)** by missionaries, **Bethune School (1849)**, **Wood's Despatch (1854)**, **Lady Hardinge Medical College (1916)**. Organisations: **Bharat Stree Mahamandal (1910, Allahabad, Sarla Devi Chaudhurani)**, **Ladies Social Conference (1904, Ramabai Ranade)**, **Arya Mahila Samaj (Pandita Ramabai Saraswati)**, **National Council of Women in India (1925)** and the **All India Women's Conference (1927, Margaret Cousins)**. **Sarojini Naidu** became **INC president (1925)**.

Caste rigidity was weakened by British-era forces (private property in land, equality before law, open services, secular education), reform bodies, the national movement and Gandhi, who founded the **All India Harijan Sangh (1932)**. **Jyotiba Phule** fought brahminical domination; **E.V. Ramaswamy Naicker** led the **Self-Respect Movement**; **Sri Narayana Guru** gave the slogan **"one religion, one caste, one God for mankind"**. **Ambedkar** founded the **Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha (1924)** and led the **Mahad Satyagraha (March 1927)** at the **Chawdar tank**, burning the **Manusmriti** there in **December 1927**.

## Key facts
- Sati was called a **"murder according to every shastra"** by **Raja Rammohan Roy**.
- Social base of reform: the **newly emerging middle class**; its roots lay in **government service and the professions**, not trade or industry.
- Ideological base: **rationalism, religious universalism and humanism**.
- **"Rationalism is our only preceptor"** — **Akshay Kumar Dutt**, who also used **medical opinion against child marriage**.
- Rammohan Roy defended the **monotheism of the Vedas and unitarianism of Christianity**, and attacked Hindu **polytheism** and Christian **trinitarianism**.
- **Syed Ahmed Khan**: religious tenets were **not immutable**; all prophets had the **same 'din'**.
- **Reformist** movements: **Brahmo Samaj, Prarthana Samaj, Aligarh Movement**; **revivalist**: **Arya Samaj, Deoband**.
- Social reform was tied to religious reform because social ills like untouchability and gender inequity **derived legitimacy from religion**.
- Sati banned by **Regulation XVII of 1829 (Bengal Code)** as **culpable homicide**; extended to **Madras and Bombay in 1830**.
- Female infanticide declared illegal by **Bengal Regulations of 1795 and 1804**; an **1870 Act** made **registration of all births** compulsory.
- **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act, 1856** — mainly due to **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar** (1820–91), principal of **Sanskrit College, Calcutta**.
- **Vishnu Shastri Pandit** founded the **Widow Remarriage Association** in the **1850s**.
- **Karsondas Mulji** started **Satya Prakash** (Gujarati) in **1852** to advocate widow remarriage.
- **D.K. Karve** married a widow in **1893**, opened a widows' home in **Poona** and set up the **Indian Women's University (1916)**.
- **Native Marriage Act (Civil Marriage Act), 1872**: limited impact, as it did **not apply to Hindus, Muslims and other recognised faiths**.
- **Age of Consent Act (1891)** — efforts of Parsi reformer **B.M. Malabari**; forbade marriage of girls **below 12**.
- **Sarda Act** raised marriage age to **18 for boys and 14 for girls** (the book dates it **1930** on pp. 197 and 205, **1929** on p. 199).
- **Child Marriage Restraint (Amendment) Act, 1978**: girls **15 → 18**, boys **18 → 21**.
- ⚠ May be outdated: marriage ages under the 1978 Act — the old child marriage law has since been replaced by the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006, and raising the girls' age further has been proposed.
- **Calcutta Female Juvenile Society (1819)** — set up by **Christian missionaries**, the first in women's education.
- **Bethune School (1849)** — founded by **J.E.D. Bethune**; Vidyasagar was linked with **no less than 35 girls' schools** in Bengal.
- **Lady Hardinge Medical College, Delhi** opened in **1916**; **Dufferin Hospitals** in the **1880s**.
- **Sarojini Naidu** — **INC president (1925)**, governor of the **United Provinces (1947–49)**.
- **Bharat Stree Mahamandal (1910, Allahabad)** — **Sarla Devi Chaudhurani**; first major Indian women's organisation set up by a woman.
- **Ladies Social Conference (Bharat Mahila Parishad), 1904, Bombay** — **Ramabai Ranade**, under the National Social Conference.
- **Arya Mahila Samaj** — **Pandita Ramabai Saraswati**; her plea led to medical education for women at **Lady Dufferin College**.
- **National Council of Women in India (1925)** — branch of the **International Council of Women**; **Mehribai Tata**; **Cornelia Sarabji**, India's first lady barrister.
- **All India Women's Conference (1927)** — founded by **Margaret Cousins**; first conference at **Ferguson College, Pune**.
- Rulers of **Travancore, Indore and Devas** opened all state temples by proclamation.
- **All India Harijan Sangh** — founded by **Gandhi in 1932**.
- **Jyotiba Phule** — from a **Mali** family; led a movement against brahminical domination.
- **Ambedkar** organised the **All India Scheduled Castes Federation**; special representation for depressed classes came in the **Government of India Act, 1935**.
- **Self-Respect Movement (1920s)** — **E.V. Ramaswamy Naicker**.
- **Sri Narayana Guru**: **"one religion, one caste, one God for mankind"**; disciple **Sahadaran Ayyapan**: **"no religion, no caste, no God for mankind"**.
- **Mahad Satyagraha (March 1927)** — about **2,500** untouchables to the **Chawdar tank**; **Manusmriti burnt** there in **December 1927**.
- **Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha (1924)** — **Ambedkar**; motto **'Educate, Agitate and Organise'**.
