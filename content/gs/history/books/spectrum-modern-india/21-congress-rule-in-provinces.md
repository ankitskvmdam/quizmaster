## Background: the ministries take office

1. After the 1937 elections the Congress formed ministries in six provinces. These were Bombay, Madras, Central Provinces, Orissa, United Provinces and Bihar.
2. Later it formed ministries in the NWFP and Assam too.
3. Congress rule in the provinces lasted 28 months.

Gandhi gave the Congressmen four pieces of advice:

- They should hold the offices "lightly and not tightly".
- They should treat the offices as 'crowns of thorns'.
- They should use the offices in a way not expected or intended by the British.
- They should prove that the Congress could rule with the least assistance from the police and the Army.

## The work of the ministries

### Civil liberties

#### Steps taken

- Emergency laws were repealed.
- Bans on the Hindustan Seva Dal and the Youth Leagues were lifted. Bans on certain books were lifted too.
- Curbs on the press ended.
- Arms licences were restored.
- The CID stopped shadowing politicians. The CID was the detective branch of the police.
- Political prisoners were released.
- In Bombay, lands confiscated during the Civil Disobedience Movement were restored.
- Pensions were restored.

#### Limits

- The Madras government arrested two socialists, Yusuf Maherally and S.S. Batliwala. Batliwala got a sentence of six months.
- K.M. Munshi was the home minister of Bombay. He used the CID against communists and leftists.

### Agrarian reform

#### Steps taken

- Laws were passed on land reform, debt relief, forest grazing fees, rent arrears and tenures.

#### Limits

- The laws mainly helped the statutory and occupancy tenants.
- Sub-tenants gained little.
- Agricultural labourers did not benefit, because they were not mobilised.
- The ministries could not abolish zamindari (the landlord system).

#### Why zamindari could not be abolished

1. **Powers:** The ministries had inadequate powers.
2. **Finances:** Money was scarce. The lion's share went to the Government of India.
3. **Class adjustment:** There was a need for class adjustment.
4. **Time:** There was a lack of time.
5. **War:** War clouds gathered from around 1938.
6. **Second chamber:** UP, Bihar, Bombay, Madras and Assam had a reactionary second chamber, the Legislative Council. It was made up of landlords, moneylenders and capitalists.
7. **Agrarian structure:** The agrarian structure was complex.

### Labour

#### Steps taken

- The ministries sought industrial peace.
- They provided for compulsory arbitration prior to striking. Arbitration is settlement of a dispute by a third party.

#### Limits

- The ministries treated militant union protests as law-and-order problems.
- They frequently used Section 144.
- The approach was largely successful, except in Bombay. There it failed.
- Nehru was unhappy with the repressive measures. But he publicly supported and defended the ministries.
- Gandhi warned against frequent resort to colonial laws.

### Social welfare

#### Steps taken

- Prohibition (a ban on liquor) was brought in in certain areas.
- Harijan welfare was taken up, with temple entry and scholarships.
- Work was done in education and health.
- Khadi got subsidies.
- Prisons were reformed.
- Indigenous enterprise was supported.
- **1938:** The National Planning Committee was set up. It was set up under Subhash Bose, the Congress president.

### Extra-parliamentary work

The Congress also worked outside the legislatures:

- Mass literacy campaigns.
- Congress police stations and panchayats.
- Congress Grievance Committees.
- States' peoples' movements.

## Evaluation

### Gains

- The rule showed that the movement could use state power without being co-opted.
- The ministries raised the prestige of the Congress.
- They controlled communal riots.
- Bureaucratic morale came down.
- Landlords and other hostile elements were neutralised.
- The myth that Indians were unfit to rule was weakened.

### Problems

- The ministries could not change the basic imperialist character of the system.
- Labour militancy rose. Industrial unrest rose in Bombay, Gujarat, UP and Bengal.
- This brought an apparent anti-labour shift in the ministries.
- The Prajamandal movements in the princely states put the Congress in a dilemma.

### The Pirpur Committee

- **1938:** The All India Muslim League set up the Pirpur Committee.
- It accused the ministries of interfering in religious rites.
- It accused them of suppressing Urdu in favour of Hindi.
- It accused them of oppressing Muslims.

## The end of Congress rule

- **October 1939:** The Congress ministries resigned. This was after the outbreak of the Second World War.
