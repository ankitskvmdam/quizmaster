## Gist
*Book pages 411–415.*

After the 1937 elections, Congress ministries were formed in **Bombay, Madras, Central Provinces, Orissa, United Provinces and Bihar**, and **later in the NWFP and Assam**. **Gandhi** advised Congressmen to hold office **"lightly and not tightly"**, to treat offices as **'crowns of thorns'**, to use them in ways **not expected or intended by the British**, and to prove the Congress could rule with **least assistance from the police and the Army**. The ministries raised the Congress's prestige but could not change the **basic imperialist character** of the system.

In the **28 months** of Congress rule, **civil liberties** were eased: emergency laws were repealed; bans on the **Hindustan Seva Dal** and **Youth Leagues** and on certain books were lifted; press curbs ended; arms licences were restored; the **CID stopped shadowing politicians**; political prisoners were released; in **Bombay** lands confiscated during the CDM were restored; and pensions were restored. Blemishes: the **Madras** government arrested socialists **Yusuf Maherally** and **S.S. Batliwala** (six months' sentence), and Bombay home minister **K.M. Munshi** used the CID against communists and leftists.

**Zamindari could not be abolished** because of inadequate powers, scarce finances (the **lion's share** went to the Government of India), the need for class adjustment, lack of time, **war clouds from around 1938**, a **reactionary second chamber** (Legislative Council) in **UP, Bihar, Bombay, Madras and Assam**, and a complex agrarian structure. Laws on land reform, debt relief, forest grazing fees, rent arrears and tenures mostly helped **statutory and occupancy tenants**; sub-tenants gained little and **agricultural labourers**, not being mobilised, did not benefit.

On **labour**, ministries sought industrial peace through **compulsory arbitration prior to striking**, treated militant union protests as law-and-order problems and often used **Section 144**; this was largely successful **except in Bombay**. **Nehru** disliked the repression but defended the ministries in public; **Gandhi** warned against frequent resort to colonial laws.

**Social welfare** included prohibition in certain areas, Harijan welfare (temple entry, scholarships), education, health, **khadi subsidies**, prison reform, support for indigenous enterprise and the **National Planning Committee (1938)** under Congress president **Subhash Bose**. Extra-parliamentary work included **mass literacy campaigns**, **Congress police stations and panchayats**, **Congress Grievance Committees** and states' peoples' movements.

The rule showed that the movement could use state power without being co-opted, controlled **communal riots**, lowered **bureaucratic morale** and weakened the myth that Indians were unfit to rule. Rising labour militancy brought an apparent anti-labour shift, and the **Prajamandal** movements in princely states posed a dilemma. The **Muslim League's Pirpur Committee (1938)** accused the ministries of interfering in religious rites, suppressing **Urdu** in favour of **Hindi**, and oppressing Muslims. The ministries **resigned in October 1939** after the outbreak of the **Second World War**.

## Key facts
- Congress ministries: **Bombay, Madras, Central Provinces, Orissa, United Provinces, Bihar**; **later NWFP and Assam**.
- Gandhi: hold offices **"lightly and not tightly"**; offices are **'crowns of thorns'**; use them in a way **not expected or intended by the British**.
- Gandhi urged proving the Congress could rule with **least assistance from the police and the Army**.
- Congress rule in provinces lasted **28 months**.
- Bans lifted on the **Hindustan Seva Dal** and **Youth Leagues**; the **CID stopped shadowing politicians**.
- In **Bombay**, lands confiscated during the Civil Disobedience Movement were **restored**.
- **Yusuf Maherally** and **S.S. Batliwala** (socialists) were arrested by the **Madras** government; Batliwala got **six months**.
- **K.M. Munshi**, **Bombay home minister**, used the CID against communists and leftists.
- Ministries could not abolish **zamindari**; finances limited as the **lion's share** went to the Government of India; **war clouds** from around **1938**.
- Reactionary **second chamber** (Legislative Council) of landlords, moneylenders and capitalists in **UP, Bihar, Bombay, Madras and Assam**.
- Agrarian laws mainly benefited **statutory and occupancy tenants**; **agricultural labourers** did not benefit as they were **not mobilised**.
- Labour policy: **compulsory arbitration prior to striking**; frequent use of **Section 144**; approach failed in **Bombay**.
- **Nehru** was unhappy with repressive measures but **publicly supported** the ministries.
- **National Planning Committee** set up in **1938** under Congress president **Subhash Bose**.
- Extra-parliamentary activity: **mass literacy campaigns**, **Congress police stations and panchayats**, **Congress Grievance Committees**, states peoples' movements.
- Evaluation: ministries **controlled communal riots**; **bureaucratic morale came down**; landlords and other hostile elements neutralised.
- Industrial unrest rose in **Bombay, Gujarat, UP and Bengal**; Congress faced a dilemma over the **Prajamandal** movement in princely states.
- **Pirpur Committee (1938)** — set up by the **All India Muslim League**; charged suppression of **Urdu in favour of Hindi**.
- Congress ministries **resigned in October 1939** after the outbreak of the **Second World War**.
