## At a glance: three kinds of challenges

| Kind | Challenges |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate | Princely states, riots, refugees, communist insurgency |
| Medium term | Constitution, elections, abolition of the feudal set-up in agriculture |
| Long term | National integration, economic development, poverty alleviation |

## The first day of independence

1. **11 p.m., 14 August 1947:** The Constituent Assembly met. Rajendra Prasad presided over it.
2. **Midnight of 14–15 August 1947:** Nehru gave his 'tryst with destiny' speech.
3. **15 August 1947:** Nehru hoisted the flag above the Lahori Gate of the Red Fort.

## The first government

### Heads of state

1. Mountbatten was the first Governor-General.
2. C. Rajagopalachari followed him. He held the post till 26 January 1950.
3. **26 January 1950:** Rajendra Prasad became the first President.

## Partition

### The Boundary Commission

- **Chairman:** Sir Cyril Radcliffe.
- **Members:** two Muslim and two non-Muslim judges.
- **Deadline:** The commission had to draw the line in six weeks.
- **Basis:** It had to use out-of-date maps and the faulty 1941 census.
- **Report:** The report was ready by 12 August. Mountbatten published it after 15 August.

### Violence

1. **October 1946:** In Bihar, Hindu peasants killed Muslims. They were allegedly instigated by landlords. Violence at Garhmukteswar in the United Provinces (UP) followed.
2. After partition, Gandhi's efforts prevented massacres there.
3. About 180,000 people were killed in the partition violence.
4. A boundary force of 50,000 was set up. It split on communal lines.
5. Bengal saw less violence than Punjab. This was because of Gandhi's fasts.

### Dividing the services, the forces and the money

- **Civil services:** A partition council divided the civil services. Its steering committee had H.M. Patel and Mohammad Ali. About 1,60,000 employees opted to transfer.
- **Armed forces:** A joint defence council divided the forces. Auchinleck was its Supreme Commander. This post was later abolished.
- **British troops:** They left between 17 August 1947 and February 1948.
- **Sterling balance:** Pakistan's share of the sterling balance was Rs. 550 million. India withheld it over Kashmir. Gandhi fasted, and his fast forced its release.

## Gandhi's assassination and the ban on the RSS

1. **30 January 1948:** Nathuram Godse shot Gandhi at Birla mansion, New Delhi.
2. **4 February 1948:** The Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) was banned.
3. **July 1949:** The ban was lifted after the RSS accepted the government's conditions.

The conditions were three:

1. It would do cultural work only.
2. It would be loyal to the Constitution and the flag.
3. It would be organised democratically.

## Refugees

### Relief and resettlement

- Nearly 60 lakh refugees had to be rehabilitated.
- India set up a Ministry of Relief and Rehabilitation.
- **Ulhasnagar:** This town in Maharashtra was built for refugees from Sindh.
- **Delhi colonies:** They included Lajpat Nagar, Rajinder Nagar, Punjabi Bagh, Nizamuddin East and Kingsway Camp.

### Bengal

1. **December 1949–January 1950:** Violence in Khulna led to fresh migration.
2. **After February 1950:** Riots took place. About one million Muslims left West Bengal after them.
3. **By 1951:** About 15 lakh Hindu refugees had come.

### The Delhi Pact

The Delhi Pact on Minorities was signed on 8 April 1950. It is also called the Liaquat-Nehru Pact. Mookherjee resigned against it.

The pact provided four things:

- minority ministers;
- minority commissions;
- commissions of inquiry;
- an agency to recover abducted women.

## The communist insurgency

1. **September 1947:** Russia's Zhdanov thesis came. It was the answer to the Marshall Plan.
2. **December 1947:** The Communist Party of India (CPI) called independence fake. Its slogan was 'ye azadi jhooti hai'.
3. **February–March 1948:** The CPI held its Second Congress at Calcutta. It adopted the B.T. Ranadive line there.
4. **March 1948:** The CPI was banned in West Bengal.
5. **9 May 1949:** A proposed railway strike failed.
6. **1950:** Ajoy Ghosh, S.A. Dange and S.V. Ghate criticised the line.
7. **October 1951:** The Third Party Congress was held at Calcutta. It withdrew the Telangana struggle.
8. **1951–52:** The CPI took part in the general election.
