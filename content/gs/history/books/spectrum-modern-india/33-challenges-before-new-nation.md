## Gist
*Book pages 590–602.*

The **Constituent Assembly** met at **11 p.m. on August 14, 1947**, presided over by **Rajendra Prasad**; at midnight **Nehru** gave his **'tryst with destiny'** speech. On **August 15, 1947** Nehru hoisted the flag above the **Lahori Gate of the Red Fort**. **Sardar Patel** was **deputy prime minister** (and Home and States) till his death in **December 1950**; **Mountbatten** and then **C. Rajagopalachari** were Governor-General till **January 26, 1950**, when **Rajendra Prasad** became the first President. In the first ministry, **Jagjivan Ram** held **Labour**, **R.K. Shanmukham Chetty** Finance and **B.R. Ambedkar** Law; **Shyama Prasad Mookherjee** (Hindu Mahasabha) was the **first to resign (April 1950)**.

Challenges were **immediate** (princely states, riots, about **60 lakh refugees**, communist insurgency), **medium term** (Constitution, elections, **abolition of the feudal set-up**) and **long term** (national integration, development, poverty).

The **Boundary Commission** under **Sir Cyril Radcliffe** (two Muslim and two non-Muslim judges) had to draw the line in **six weeks** using out-of-date maps and the faulty **1941 census**. Its report was ready by **August 12** but **Mountbatten** published it **after August 15**. About **180,000** people were killed. **Bengal** saw less violence than Punjab because of **Gandhi's fasts**. In **Bihar (October 1946)** Hindu peasants, allegedly instigated by landlords, killed Muslims, followed by violence at **Garhmukteswar (UP)**; after partition, Gandhi's efforts prevented massacres there. A **boundary force of 50,000** split on communal lines.

A **partition council** (with a steering committee of **H.M. Patel and Mohammad Ali**) divided the civil services; about **1,60,000** employees opted to transfer. A **joint defence council** under **Auchinleck** divided the forces; British troops left between **August 17, 1947** and **February 1948**. India withheld Pakistan's share of the **sterling balance (Rs. 550 million)** over **Kashmir**; **Gandhi fasted** to get it released.

**Gandhi** was shot by **Nathuram Godse** at **Birla mansion** on **January 30, 1948**. The **RSS** was banned on **February 4, 1948** and the ban lifted in **July 1949** on conditions (cultural work only, loyalty to Constitution and flag, democratic organisation).

For refugees, India set up a **Ministry of Relief and Rehabilitation**. In Bengal, violence in **Khulna** (December 1949–January 1950) led to fresh migration; by **1951** about **15 lakh** Hindu refugees had come. The **Delhi Pact / Liaquat-Nehru Pact (April 8, 1950)** provided minority ministers, minority commissions and an agency for abducted women; **Mookherjee** resigned against it. **Ulhasnagar** was built for Sindhi refugees.

The **CPI** called independence fake (**'ye azadi jhooti hai'**, December 1947) and adopted the **B.T. Ranadive line** at its **Second Congress, Calcutta (February–March 1948)**. After criticism from **Ajoy Ghosh, S.A. Dange and S.V. Ghate (1950)**, the **Third Party Congress (October 1951)** withdrew the **Telangana** struggle and the party joined the **1951–52 elections**.

## Key facts
- Constituent Assembly met at **11 p.m., August 14, 1947**; presided over by **Rajendra Prasad**.
- Nehru's **'tryst with destiny'** speech — midnight of **August 14–15, 1947**; flag hoisted above the **Lahori Gate of the Red Fort** on August 15.
- **Sardar Patel** — Deputy PM; Home Affairs and States; Information and Broadcasting; served till his death in **December 1950**.
- Governors-General: **Mountbatten**, then **C. Rajagopalachari**, till **January 26, 1950**; **Rajendra Prasad** became the first President.
- First ministry: **Maulana Azad** — Education; **John Mathai** — Railways and Transport; **Baldev Singh** — Defence; **Jagjivan Ram** — Labour; **Amrit Kaur** — Health; **Rafi Ahmad Kidwai** — Communications; **R.K. Shanmukham Chetty** — Finance; **K.C. Neogy** — Relief and Rehabilitation; **C.H. Bhabha** — Commerce.
- **B.R. Ambedkar** — Law; belonged to the **Scheduled Castes Federation**; resigned in **1951**.
- **Shyama Prasad Mookherjee** — Industries and Supplies (Hindu Mahasabha); **first to resign from the cabinet, April 1950**.
- **N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar** — minister without portfolio, link with the **East Punjab** cabinet.
- Immediate challenge included rehabilitating nearly **60 lakh refugees**; medium-term: Constitution, elections, **abolition of the feudal set-up in agriculture**; long-term: national integration, economic development, poverty alleviation.
- West Punjab got **62,000 sq miles**; East Punjab **37,000 sq miles**; West Bengal **28,000 sq miles (21.2 million people)**; East Bengal **49,400 sq miles (39.10 million)**.
- Boundary Commission — chairman **Sir Cyril Radcliffe**; **two Muslim and two non-Muslim judges**; deadline **six weeks**; based on the **1941 census**.
- Report ready by **August 12**, published **after August 15** by Mountbatten.
- About **180,000** killed in partition violence; boundary force of **50,000** split on communal lines.
- **Bihar, October 1946** — Hindu peasants, allegedly instigated by landlords, killed Muslims; followed by **Garhmukteswar** (UP).
- Partition council steering committee — **H.M. Patel and Mohammad Ali**; about **1,60,000** employees opted to transfer.
- Joint defence council — **Auchinleck** as Supreme Commander (post later abolished); British troops left **August 17, 1947 – February 1948**.
- Pakistan's share of the **sterling balance — Rs. 550 million** — withheld over **Kashmir**; Gandhi's fast forced its release.
- **January 30, 1948** — Gandhi shot by **Nathuram Godse** at **Birla mansion, New Delhi**.
- **RSS** banned **February 4, 1948**; ban lifted **July 1949** after it accepted government conditions.
- Bengal: violence in **Khulna** (Dec 1949–Jan 1950); about **one million Muslims** left West Bengal after February 1950 riots; about **15 lakh** Hindu refugees by **1951**.
- **Delhi Pact on Minorities / Liaquat-Nehru Pact** — **April 8, 1950**; minority ministers, **minority commissions**, commissions of inquiry, agency to recover **abducted women**.
- **Ulhasnagar** (Maharashtra) — built for refugees from **Sindh**; Delhi colonies included **Lajpat Nagar, Rajinder Nagar, Punjabi Bagh, Nizamuddin East, Kingsway Camp**.
- CPI slogan **'ye azadi jhooti hai'** (December 1947); **B.T. Ranadive line** at the **Second Congress, Calcutta (February–March 1948)**.
- Russia's **Zhdanov thesis (September 1947)** was the answer to the **Marshall Plan**.
- CPI banned in West Bengal in **March 1948**; proposed railway strike failed on **May 9, 1949**.
- **Third Party Congress, Calcutta (October 1951)** — Telangana movement withdrawn; CPI took part in the **1951–52 general election**.
