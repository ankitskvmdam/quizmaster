## Why revolutionary activity began

1. Revolutionary activity grew as a by-product of militant nationalism.
2. Its first phase was a fallout of the Swadeshi and Boycott Movement. This phase continued till 1917.
3. The second phase followed the Non-Cooperation Movement.
4. The leaders, even the Extremists, failed to find new forms of struggle. Young nationalists were disillusioned with them.
5. The young nationalists also faced repression.
6. They concluded that the British must be expelled by force.

## The methods of the revolutionaries

The revolutionaries rejected two paths as impractical: a mass revolution, and subverting the Army. They followed the methods of the Russian nihilists or the Irish nationalists. This meant individual heroic actions, not mass struggle.

- **Assassinations:** They killed unpopular officials and traitors.
- **Swadeshi dacoities:** These were robberies to raise funds.
- **Military conspiracies:** In the First World War they planned these with help from the enemies of Britain.

## Bengal

### Groups and papers

- **1902:** The first revolutionary groups were organised. One was in Midnapore, under Jnanendranath Basu. The other was in Calcutta: the Anushilan Samiti, founded by Promotha Mitter.
- **April 1906:** An inner circle started the weekly Yugantar. Barindra Kumar Ghosh and Bhupendranath Dutta were in it.
- **Dacca Anushilan:** It worked under Pulin Das.
- **Jugantar:** Bagha Jatin (Jatindranath Mukherjee) led it. He linked Calcutta with Bengal, Bihar and Orissa.
- **Revolutionary papers:** Sandhya and Yugantar.

### Actions and trials

1. **1907:** There was an abortive attempt on Sir Fuller. He was the first Lt. Governor of Eastern Bengal and Assam.
2. **1908:** The Dacca Anushilan carried out the Barrah dacoity.
3. **1908:** Prafulla Chaki and Khudiram Bose threw a bomb at a carriage in Muzaffarpur, in Bihar. The carriage was supposed to carry the judge Kingsford. He was not in it, and two British ladies were killed. Chaki shot himself. Khudiram was hanged.
4. The Alipore conspiracy case followed. Its details are given below.
5. **December 1912:** Rashbehari Bose and Sachin Sanyal threw a bomb at Viceroy Hardinge at Chandni Chowk in Delhi. Hardinge was injured.
6. The Delhi Conspiracy trial followed. Basant Kumar Biswas, Amir Chand and Avadh Behari were executed. Rashbehari Bose evaded arrest.
7. Bagha Jatin planned the 'German Plot', also called the 'Zimmerman Plan'. His call was: "We shall die to awaken the nation".
8. **September 1915:** Bagha Jatin died at Balasore.

The Alipore conspiracy case:

- It is also called the Manicktolla bomb conspiracy and the Muraripukur conspiracy.
- Chittaranjan (C.R.) Das defended Aurobindo. Aurobindo was acquitted.
- Barindra Ghosh was the head of the group. Ullaskar Dutt was the bomb maker. Both got death sentences, which were commuted to life.
- Narendra Gosain became an approver (an accused who turns government witness). Satyendranath Bose and Kanailal Dutta shot him in jail.

## Maharashtra

### Groups and papers

- **1879:** Vasudev Balwant Phadke organised the Ramosi Peasant Force.
- **1899:** Savarkar started the Mitra Mela.
- **1904:** The Mitra Mela merged with Abhinav Bharat. It was modelled on Mazzini's 'Young Italy'.
- **Revolutionary paper:** Kal.

### Actions and trials

1. **1897:** The Chapekar brothers, Damodar and Balkrishna, killed Rand and Lt. Ayerst. Rand was the Plague Commissioner of Poona.
2. **1909:** Anant Lakshman Kanhere killed A.M.T. Jackson, the Collector of Nasik.
3. **1910:** Savarkar was extradited (handed over for trial). In the Nasik conspiracy case he got transportation for life, that is, exile for life to a prison overseas.

## Punjab

### Groups and papers

- **Lajpat Rai:** He brought out the Punjabee.
- **Ajit Singh:** He was the uncle of Bhagat Singh. He organised the Anjuman-i-Mohisban-i-Watan at Lahore. Its journal was Bharat Mata.

### Actions and trials

1. **May 1907:** The government banned political meetings. It deported Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh.

## Abroad

### Europe

Groups and papers:

- **India House, London (1905):** Shyamji Krishnavarma founded it. It was also called the Indian Home Rule Society. Its journal was The Indian Sociologist.
- **Madam Bhikaji Cama:** She worked from Paris and Geneva. She brought out the Bande Mataram.
- **Virendranath Chattopadhyaya:** He worked from Berlin.
- **Berlin Committee for Indian Independence (1915):** Virendranath Chattopadhyaya, Bhupendranath Dutta and Lala Hardayal set it up under the 'Zimmerman Plan'.

Actions and trials:

1. **1909:** Madanlal Dhingra assassinated Curzon-Wyllie.

### North America: the Ghadr Party

Groups and papers:

- **Earlier bodies:** the Swadesh Sevak Home at Vancouver and the United India House at Seattle.
- **Ghadr Party (1913):** It was established in 1913. Its headquarters was at San Francisco.
- **Leaders:** Lala Hardayal, Kartar Singh Saraba, Barkatullah and others.

Actions and trials:

1. **September 1914:** The ship Komagata Maru anchored at Calcutta. It had carried 370 passengers from Singapore to Vancouver and had come back. At Budge Budge 22 people died.
2. **January–February 1915:** Political dacoities took place in Punjab. They had a new social content: the raiders targeted moneylenders and debt records.
3. **21 February 1915:** This was the date fixed for the Ghadr revolt at Ferozepur, Lahore and Rawalpindi. It was foiled by treachery. In the end 45 were hanged.
4. After the failed revolt Rashbehari Bose escaped to Japan in 1915. Later he was important in founding the INA. Sachin Sanyal was transported for life.
5. **March 1915:** The Defence of India Act was passed. Its main purpose was to smash the Ghadr movement.

### Other attempts during the war

1. **15 February 1915:** The Singapore mutiny broke out. The 5th Light Infantry and the 36th Sikh battalion took part. It was crushed: 37 were executed and 41 were transported for life.
2. **Kabul mission:** Raja Mahendra Pratap Singh, Barkatullah and Obaidullah Sindhi went to Kabul. They set up a 'provisional Indian government' with the crown prince Amanullah.

## Why the activity declined

Revolutionary activity declined after the war. There were three reasons.

1. **Release of prisoners:** The prisoners were released.
2. **Montagu's statement of August 1917:** It created an atmosphere of conciliation.
3. **Gandhi:** He offered non-violent non-cooperation as a new way.
