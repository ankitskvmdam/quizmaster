## Gist
*Book pages 619–627.*

India entered independence with diplomatic experience: it was a member of **51 international organisations** and a signatory to **about 600 treaties**. It signed the **Versailles Treaty**, largely because it had contributed **more than a million soldiers** to the First World War, was a **founding member of the League of Nations and the ILO** in the 1920s, took part in the **Washington Conference on Naval Armaments (1921–22)**, and had a **high commissioner in London from 1920**.

**1880 to the First World War — anti-imperialism and pan-Asian feeling.** Nationalists opposed British expeditions: the **Second Afghan War (1878–80)**, troops sent in **1882** against **Col. Arabi in Egypt**, the **annexation of Burma (1885)**, **Curzon's invasion of Tibet (1903)** and north-west annexations in the 1890s. **C. Sankaran Nair**, Congress president in **1897**, said, "Our true policy is a peaceful policy." Japan inspired them as a model of industrial development, and its **defeat of Czarist Russia** exploded the myth of European superiority.

**World War I and after.** Nationalists backed Britain, then demanded a place at the Peace Conference; in **1920** the Congress urged people not to join the army to fight in the West, and in **1925** it condemned sending Indian troops against **Sun Yat-sen's** nationalist army.

**1920s–30s — identifying with socialists.** **Dadabhai Naoroji** attended the Hague session of the International Socialist Congress and was a friend of **H.M. Hyndman**; **Lajpat Rai** met American socialists (USA, **1914–18**); **Gandhi** had close relations with **Tolstoy** and **Romain Rolland**. In **1927** **Nehru** attended the **Congress of Oppressed Nationalities at Brussels** on behalf of the INC, was an honorary president with **Einstein**, was nominated to the executive council of the **League Against Imperialism**, and visited the **Soviet Union**.

**After 1936 — anti-fascism.** Support for struggles in Ethiopia, Spain, China and Czechoslovakia; at the **Tripuri session (1939)** the Congress dissociated itself from British policy; a **medical mission under Dr Atal** went to China. On **Palestine** it backed the Palestinians, sympathised with the Jews, and **opposed partition**.

**After independence.** **Nehru**, architect of the foreign policy, laid its foundations in his address to the Constituent Assembly on **December 4, 1947**. **Panchsheel** was first formally enunciated on **April 29, 1954** in the India–China agreement on trade with the **Tibet region**; **Nehru and Zhou Enlai** elaborated it in a joint statement on **June 18, 1954**. A boxed article credits **Zhou** with first stating the principles (**December 31, 1953**) and **T.N. Kaul** with putting them into India's draft. Panchsheel entered the **Bandung Conference (April 1955, 29 Afro-Asian countries)** declaration, a **UN General Assembly resolution (December 11, 1957)** moved by **India, Yugoslavia and Sweden**, and was accepted at the **Belgrade NAM conference (1961)**.

**Non-alignment** — the term gained currency after **Bandung (1955)** — means refusing to join either bloc. It is **not neutrality**: neutrality is passive and mainly for wartime, while non-alignment takes positive positions in war and peace. NAM's five pioneers: **Tito (Yugoslavia), Nasser (Egypt), Nkrumah (Ghana), Sukarno (Indonesia), Nehru (India)**.

## Key facts
- At independence India was a member of **51 international organisations** and signatory to **about 600 treaties**.
- India signed the **Versailles Treaty**, having contributed **more than a million soldiers** to the First World War.
- India was a **founding member** of the **League of Nations** and the **ILO** in the 1920s.
- India took part in the **Washington Conference on Naval Armaments, 1921–22**; an Indian **high commissioner in London from 1920**.
- **C. Sankaran Nair** (Congress president, **1897**): "Our true policy is a peaceful policy."
- British expeditions opposed: **Second Afghan War 1878–80**, **Egypt (Col. Arabi) 1882**, **Burma annexed 1885**, **Tibet invaded under Curzon 1903**.
- **1925** — Congress condemned sending the Indian Army against the Chinese nationalist army of **Sun Yat-sen**.
- **Dadabhai Naoroji** — Hague session of the International Socialist Congress; friend of **H.M. Hyndman**.
- **Lajpat Rai** — contacts with American socialists during his US visit, **1914–1918**.
- **1927** — Nehru at the **Congress of Oppressed Nationalities, Brussels**; nominated to the **League Against Imperialism** executive council; visited the **Soviet Union**.
- **Tripuri session (1939)** — Congress dissociated itself from British policy supporting fascism in Europe.
- Congress **medical mission to China** under **Dr Atal**; the 1939 Japanese attack on China was condemned.
- On Palestine, the Congress supported the Palestinians and **opposed the partition of Palestine**.
- Nehru laid the foundations of foreign policy in the Constituent Assembly on **December 4, 1947**.
- **Panchsheel** first formally enunciated **April 29, 1954** — India–China agreement on trade with the **Tibet region**.
- **Nehru–Zhou Enlai joint statement** on Panchsheel, Delhi, **June 18, 1954**.
- Boxed view: **Zhou** first stated the five principles (**December 31, 1953**); **T.N. Kaul** put them in India's draft; 'sheel' classically means **'character'**.
- **Bandung Conference (April 1955)** — **29** Afro-Asian countries; Panchsheel in its Ten Principles.
- UNGA resolution on peaceful co-existence (**December 11, 1957**) — moved by **India, Yugoslavia and Sweden**.
- **First NAM summit — Belgrade, 1961**; accepted Panchsheel as NAM's basic principles.
- Term **'non-alignment'** gained currency after **Bandung (1955)**.
- Non-alignment ≠ **neutrality**: neutrality is passive and mainly in war; non-alignment matters in **war and peace**.
- NAM pioneers: **Tito (Yugoslavia)**, **Nasser (Egypt)**, **Nkrumah (Ghana)**, **Sukarno (Indonesia)**, **Nehru (India)**.
