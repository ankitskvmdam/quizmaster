## Background: diplomatic experience before independence

India entered independence with diplomatic experience.

- At independence India was a member of 51 international organisations. It was a signatory to about 600 treaties.
- India signed the Versailles Treaty. This was largely because it had contributed more than a million soldiers to the First World War.
- **From 1920:** There was an Indian high commissioner in London.
- **1920s:** India was a founding member of the League of Nations and the International Labour Organisation (ILO).
- **1921–22:** India took part in the Washington Conference on Naval Armaments.

## Before independence: phase by phase

### 1880 to the First World War: anti-imperialism and pan-Asian feeling

The nationalists opposed British expeditions and annexations:

1. **1878–80:** The Second Afghan War.
2. **1882:** Troops were sent against Col. Arabi in Egypt.
3. **1885:** Burma was annexed.
4. **1890s:** Annexations were made in the north-west.
5. **1903:** Tibet was invaded under Curzon.

Two more points belong to this phase:

- **1897:** C. Sankaran Nair was the Congress president. He said, "Our true policy is a peaceful policy."
- **Japan:** It inspired the nationalists as a model of industrial development. Its defeat of Czarist Russia exploded the myth of European superiority.

### World War I and after

1. The nationalists backed Britain in the war. Then they demanded a place at the Peace Conference.
2. **1920:** The Congress urged people not to join the army to fight in the West.
3. **1925:** The Congress condemned the sending of the Indian Army against the Chinese nationalist army of Sun Yat-sen.

### 1920s–30s: identifying with socialists

- **Dadabhai Naoroji:** He attended the Hague session of the International Socialist Congress. He was a friend of H.M. Hyndman.
- **Lajpat Rai:** He made contacts with American socialists during his visit to the USA, 1914–1918.
- **Gandhi:** He had close relations with Tolstoy and Romain Rolland.
- **Nehru:** His work in 1927 is listed below.

Nehru in 1927:

1. He attended the Congress of Oppressed Nationalities at Brussels on behalf of the Indian National Congress (INC).
2. He was an honorary president of it, along with Einstein.
3. He was nominated to the executive council of the League Against Imperialism.
4. He visited the Soviet Union.

### After 1936: anti-fascism

- The Congress supported the struggles in Ethiopia, Spain, China and Czechoslovakia.
- **1939, Tripuri session:** The Congress dissociated itself from the British policy that supported fascism in Europe.
- **1939:** The Japanese attack on China was condemned.
- **China:** A Congress medical mission went to China under Dr Atal.
- **Palestine:** The Congress supported the Palestinians and sympathised with the Jews. It opposed the partition of Palestine.

## After independence

### Foundations

- Nehru was the architect of the foreign policy.
- **4 December 1947:** He laid its foundations in his address to the Constituent Assembly.

### Panchsheel

Panchsheel means the five principles.

1. **31 December 1953:** A boxed article in the book says Zhou first stated the five principles on this date. It says T.N. Kaul put them into India's draft.
2. **29 April 1954:** Panchsheel was first formally enunciated. This was in the India–China agreement on trade with the Tibet region.
3. **18 June 1954:** Nehru and Zhou Enlai elaborated it in a joint statement at Delhi.
4. **April 1955:** The Bandung Conference of 29 Afro-Asian countries was held. Panchsheel entered the Ten Principles of its declaration.
5. **11 December 1957:** The UN General Assembly (UNGA) passed a resolution on peaceful co-existence. India, Yugoslavia and Sweden moved it.
6. **1961:** The first summit of the Non-Aligned Movement (NAM) was held at Belgrade. It accepted Panchsheel as the basic principles of NAM.

The boxed article adds that 'sheel' classically means 'character'.

### Non-alignment

- Non-alignment means refusing to join either bloc.
- The term 'non-alignment' gained currency after Bandung (1955).
- Non-alignment is not neutrality.

| Point | Neutrality | Non-alignment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nature | Passive | Takes positive positions |
| When it applies | Mainly in wartime | In war and peace |
