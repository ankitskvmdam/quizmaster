## Gist
*Book pages 557–562.*

**James Augustus Hickey** started **The Bengal Gazette or Calcutta General Advertiser (1780)**, the first newspaper in India; it was seized for its outspoken criticism of the Government. Others followed — The Bengal Journal, The Calcutta Chronicle, The Madras Courier, The Bombay Herald — and the Company's officers, fearing exposure in London, sought curbs.

**Early regulations:** **Lord Wellesley's Censorship of Press Act, 1799**, anticipating a **French invasion**, imposed near-wartime restrictions including **pre-censorship**; **Lord Hastings** dispensed with pre-censorship in **1818**. The **Licensing Regulations, 1823** of acting governor-general **John Adams** made running a press without licence a penal offence, chiefly hitting Indian-language papers; **Rammohan Roy's Mirat-ul-Akbar** had to stop. **Metcalfe** repealed them through the **Press Act of 1835**, earning the title **'liberator of the Indian press'**. The **Licensing Act, 1857** added licensing after the revolt; the **Registration Act, 1867** replaced Metcalfe's Act and was **regulatory, not restrictive**.

**Nationalists and the press:** as early as **1824 Rammohan Roy** protested against press curbs. In the early phase (**c. 1870–1918**) the **INC relied solely on the press** to spread its resolutions. Papers were run as national service, not for profit, and fed a **library movement** in villages. Journalists evaded **Section 124A IPC** (sedition: transportation, or up to **three years** in prison) by prefacing attacks with loyalty or quoting socialists and Irish nationalists.

**Vernacular Press Act, 1878:** criticism of **Lytton** over the **1876–77 famine** and the lavish **Delhi Durbar** led to the VPA, the **'gagging Act'**. A **district magistrate** could demand a bond and security, his action was **final with no appeal**, and papers could escape by submitting proofs to a censor. Its worst features were **discrimination between English and vernacular press** and **no right of appeal**. Cases were brought against **Som Prakash, Bharat Mihir, Dacca Prakash and Samachar**; **Amrita Bazar Patrika** turned into an English paper overnight. **Ripon repealed it in 1882**.

**Surendranath Banerjea** became the **first Indian journalist to be imprisoned (1883)**. **Tilak** (Kesari, Maharatta) started **Ganapati (1893)** and **Shivaji (1896)** festivals; after the **Chapekar brothers** killed **Rand**, he was jailed for **18 months** over the poem **'Shivaji's Utterances'** and became **'Lokmanya'**. In **1898 Section 124A** was amended and **153A** added.

**Later laws:** **Newspaper (Incitement to Offences) Act, 1908** (Tilak then transported to **Mandalay for six years**; Lenin hailed the Bombay workers' strike); **Indian Press Act, 1910** revived the VPA's worst features; a committee under **Tej Bahadur Sapru** led to repeal of the 1908 and 1910 Acts in **1921**; the **Indian Press (Emergency Powers) Act, 1931** targeted the **Civil Disobedience Movement**. In the Second World War, pre-censorship came under the **Defence of India Rules**.

## Key facts
- First newspaper in India: **The Bengal Gazette or Calcutta General Advertiser (1780)** — **James Augustus Hickey**.
- **Censorship of Press Act, 1799** — **Lord Wellesley**, anticipating **French invasion**; imposed **pre-censorship**.
- Pre-censorship dispensed with in **1818** under **Lord Hastings**.
- **Licensing Regulations, 1823** — acting governor-general **John Adams**; **Rammohan Roy's Mirat-ul-Akbar** had to stop publication.
- **Press Act of 1835 (Metcalfe Act)** — **Metcalfe** (governor-general 1835–36), **'liberator of the Indian press'**.
- **Licensing Act, 1857** — licensing added because of the **1857 revolt**, on top of Metcalfe's registration procedure.
- **Registration Act, 1867** — replaced Metcalfe's Act; **regulatory, not restrictive**; a copy of a book to be given to local government **within one month**.
- **Rammohan Roy** protested against a resolution restricting press freedom as early as **1824**.
- **The Hindu** and **Swadesamitran** — **G. Subramaniya Aiyar**; **Amrita Bazar Patrika** — **Sisir Kumar Ghosh and Motilal Ghosh**; **Indian Mirror** — **N.N. Sen**.
- **Kesari** (Marathi) and **Maharatta** (English) — **Tilak**; **Hindustan** and **Advocate** — **G.P. Verma**; **Voice of India** — **Dadabhai Naoroji**.
- **Tribune** and **Akbhar-i-am** — **Punjab**; **Indu Prakash, Dhyan Prakash, Kal, Gujarati** — **Bombay**; **Som Prakash, Banganivasi, Sadharani** — **Bengal**.
- **Section 124A IPC**: disaffection punishable by **transportation for life** or imprisonment **up to three years**.
- **Vernacular Press Act, 1878** — under **Lytton**; nicknamed **'the gagging Act'**.
- VPA worst features: **discrimination between English and vernacular press** and **no right of appeal**.
- VPA cases: **Som Prakash, Bharat Mihir, Dacca Prakash, Samachar**; **Amrita Bazar Patrika** became English overnight to escape it.
- VPA repealed by **Ripon in 1882**.
- **Surendranath Banerjea** — **first Indian journalist imprisoned (1883)**, for an editorial in **The Bengalee** criticising a Calcutta High Court judge.
- Tilak: **Ganapati festival 1893**, **Shivaji festival 1896**; **no-tax campaign 1896–97**.
- **Rand**, chairman of the Plague Committee in Poona, murdered by the **Chapekar brothers** amid unrest over harsh plague measures (plague hit Poona in **1897**).
- Tilak sentenced to **18 months' rigorous imprisonment** over the poem **'Shivaji's Utterances'** in Kesari; got the title **'Lokmanya'**.
- **1898**: Section 124A amended and **Section 153A** added.
- **Newspaper (Incitement to Offences) Act, 1908** — magistrates could **confiscate press property**.
- Tilak transported to **Mandalay (Burma) for six years**; **Lenin** hailed the Bombay workers' strike.
- **Indian Press Act, 1910** — revived the VPA's worst features; printer to submit **two copies** of each issue free.
- **1921**: Press Acts of **1908 and 1910 repealed** on the recommendation of a committee chaired by **Tej Bahadur Sapru**.
- **Indian Press (Emergency Powers) Act, 1931** — against **Civil Disobedience** propaganda; amplified in **1932**.
