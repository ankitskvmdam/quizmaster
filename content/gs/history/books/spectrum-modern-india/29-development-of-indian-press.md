## The first newspapers

1. **1780:** James Augustus Hickey started The Bengal Gazette or Calcutta General Advertiser. It was the first newspaper in India.
2. The paper was seized for its outspoken criticism of the Government.
3. Other papers followed: The Bengal Journal, The Calcutta Chronicle, The Madras Courier and The Bombay Herald.
4. The Company's officers feared that these papers would expose them in London. So they sought curbs on the press.

## Early regulations (1799–1867)

1. **Censorship of Press Act, 1799:** Lord Wellesley brought it in, anticipating a French invasion. It imposed near-wartime restrictions. These included pre-censorship (checking a paper before it is printed).
2. **1818:** Lord Hastings dispensed with pre-censorship.
3. **Licensing Regulations, 1823:** The acting governor-general John Adams issued them. Running a press without a licence became a penal offence. The regulations chiefly hit Indian-language papers. Rammohan Roy's Mirat-ul-Akbar had to stop publication.
4. **Press Act of 1835 (Metcalfe Act):** Metcalfe was governor-general in 1835–36. He repealed the 1823 regulations through this Act. For this he earned the title 'liberator of the Indian press'.
5. **Licensing Act, 1857:** Licensing was added because of the 1857 revolt. It came on top of Metcalfe's registration procedure.
6. **Registration Act, 1867:** It replaced Metcalfe's Act. It was regulatory, not restrictive. A copy of a book had to be given to the local government within one month.

## The nationalists and the press

### How the nationalists used the press

1. **1824:** Rammohan Roy protested against a resolution restricting press freedom. This was an early protest against press curbs.
2. **c. 1870–1918:** In this early phase the Indian National Congress (INC) relied solely on the press to spread its resolutions.
3. Papers were run as national service, not for profit.
4. The papers fed a library movement in the villages.

### Section 124A and how journalists evaded it

Section 124A of the Indian Penal Code (IPC) dealt with sedition. It made disaffection punishable with transportation for life or with imprisonment up to three years. Transportation meant being sent away to a distant prison.

Journalists evaded this section in two ways:

- They prefaced their attacks with words of loyalty.
- They quoted socialists and Irish nationalists.

## Vernacular Press Act, 1878

The Vernacular Press Act (VPA) was passed under Lytton. It was nicknamed 'the gagging Act'.

### Why it came

- The press criticised Lytton over the famine of 1876–77.
- It also criticised him over the lavish Delhi Durbar.

### Provisions

- A district magistrate could demand a bond and security from a paper.
- His action was final, with no appeal.
- A paper could escape the Act by submitting its proofs to a censor.

### Worst features

1. It discriminated between the English press and the vernacular press.
2. It gave no right of appeal.

### Cases under the Act

- Cases were brought against Som Prakash, Bharat Mihir, Dacca Prakash and Samachar.
- Amrita Bazar Patrika turned into an English paper overnight to escape the Act.

### Repeal

- **1882:** Ripon repealed the Act.

## Journalists on trial

### Surendranath Banerjea

- **1883:** He became the first Indian journalist to be imprisoned. The reason was an editorial in The Bengalee. It criticised a judge of the Calcutta High Court.

### Tilak

1. **1893:** He started the Ganapati festival.
2. **1896:** He started the Shivaji festival.
3. **1896–97:** He ran a no-tax campaign.
4. **1897:** Plague hit Poona. There was unrest over the harsh plague measures. Amid this unrest the Chapekar brothers murdered Rand, the chairman of the Plague Committee in Poona.
5. After the murder Tilak was sentenced to 18 months' rigorous imprisonment. The sentence was over the poem 'Shivaji's Utterances', published in Kesari. He got the title 'Lokmanya'.
6. **1898:** Section 124A was amended and Section 153A was added.
7. After the Newspaper (Incitement to Offences) Act, 1908, Tilak was transported to Mandalay (Burma) for six years. Lenin hailed the Bombay workers' strike.

## Later laws (1908–1931)

1. **Newspaper (Incitement to Offences) Act, 1908:** Magistrates could confiscate press property.
2. **Indian Press Act, 1910:** It revived the worst features of the VPA. A printer had to submit two copies of each issue free.
3. **1921:** The Press Acts of 1908 and 1910 were repealed. A committee chaired by Tej Bahadur Sapru had recommended this.
4. **Indian Press (Emergency Powers) Act, 1931:** It was aimed at the propaganda of the Civil Disobedience Movement.
5. **1932:** The 1931 Act was amplified.
6. During the Second World War pre-censorship was imposed under the Defence of India Rules.
