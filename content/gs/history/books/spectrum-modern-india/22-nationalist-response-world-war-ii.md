## Gist
*Book pages 416–446.*

After civil disobedience, Gandhi felt the Congress had to set its house in order before any new struggle. **Subhash Chandra Bose**, president of the **Bengal Provincial Congress Committee**, wanted action. At **Haripura (Gujarat, February 1938)** he was **unanimously elected president**, spoke of planning, and later set up a **National Planning Committee**. In **January 1939** he stood again and defeated Gandhi's candidate **Pattabhi Sitaramayya** by **1580 votes to 1377**; Gandhi said **"Pattabhi's defeat is my defeat."**

At **Tripuri (March 1939, Central Provinces, near Jabalpur)** Bose proposed a **six-month ultimatum** to Britain. **Govind Ballabh Pant's resolution** asked him to nominate the working committee "in accordance with the wishes of Gandhiji". Bose **resigned in April 1939**; **Rajendra Prasad** became president. In **May 1939** Bose formed the **Forward Bloc (Makur, Unnao)**; in **August 1939** he was removed as Bengal PCC president and **debarred from elective office for three years**.

The book contrasts Gandhi and Bose: non-violence vs force, means vs ends, Gandhi's **Ramrajya** and village republics (**Hind Swaraj, 1909**; **trusteeship**; **Nai Talim, 1937**) vs Bose's **'samyavada'** (Socialism plus Fascist discipline), state-led industrialisation and an initially authoritarian state. Bose formed the **Rani of Jhansi Regiment (1943)** under **Captain Lakshmi Swaminathan** and called Gandhi **"The Father of Our Nation"** (Rangoon radio, **1944**); Gandhi called Bose the **"Prince among the Patriots"** (1942).

When war broke out (**Germany attacked Poland, 1 September 1939**), the Government of India declared India's support without consulting Indians. The Congress offered conditional support (a **constituent assembly after the war** and a **responsible government at the Centre now**); **Viceroy Linlithgow** rejected it. At the **Wardha CWC**, Gandhi wanted unconditional support, Bose and the socialists (**Narendra Dev, Jayaprakash Narayan**) wanted a struggle, and **Nehru's** middle position was adopted. Linlithgow's **17 October 1939** statement was negative, and on **23 October 1939** the CWC asked the **Congress ministries to resign**. The **Ramgarh session (March 1940, Maulana Azad)** demanded nothing short of complete independence; the Muslim League passed the **Lahore (Pakistan) Resolution, March 1940**.

The **August Offer (1940)** offered dominion status, an Indian-majority executive council and a post-war constituent assembly ("mainly" Indians), with **minorities' consent** for any constitution. The Congress rejected it (Nehru: **"dead as a doornail"**). **Individual satyagraha** followed (**October 1940**): **Vinoba Bhave** first, **Nehru** second, **25,000** convicted by May 1941. In **July 1941** Indians got **8 of 12** council seats. Around this time Gandhi named **Nehru his successor**.

The **Cripps Mission (March 1942)** promised dominion status with the right to leave the Commonwealth, a post-war constituent assembly and a **provincial right to secede**; defence stayed British. Everyone objected. Gandhi called it **"a post-dated cheque"**, and talks broke down over the **viceroy's veto**.

## Key facts
- **Haripura session (February 1938, Gujarat)** — **Subhash Bose** unanimously elected president.
- **1939 presidential election** — Bose **1580** votes beat **Pattabhi Sitaramayya** (Gandhi's candidate) **1377**.
- Gandhi: **"Pattabhi's defeat is my defeat."**
- **Tripuri session (March 1939)** — Central Provinces, **near Jabalpur**; Bose proposed a **six-month ultimatum** to Britain.
- Tripuri resolution reaffirming faith in Gandhian policies was moved by **Govind Ballabh Pant**.
- Bose resigned in **April 1939**; **Rajendra Prasad** became Congress president.
- **Forward Bloc** formed in **May 1939** at **Makur, Unnao**.
- **August 1939** — Bose removed as Bengal PCC president and **debarred from elective Congress office for 3 years**.
- Tripuri also approved a **Congress Medical Mission to China**.
- Bose called Gandhi **"The Father of Our Nation"** in a **radio broadcast from Rangoon, 1944**; Gandhi called Bose **"Prince among the Patriots"** in **1942**.
- Bose's synthesis of Socialism and Fascism: **'samyavada'** (stated as early as **1930**).
- **Provisional Government of Azad Hind** proclaimed **21 October 1943**.
- Gandhi's political ideas: **Hind Swaraj (1909)**; village **panchayat of five persons**; more emphasis on **duties than rights**.
- Gandhi's **theory of trusteeship** — capitalists as trustees of wealth.
- **Nai Talim** (basic education) conceived by Gandhi in **1937**; Gandhi wanted free, compulsory education for ages **7 to 14**.
- The **separate planning commission for women**, insisted on by Bose as Congress president (1938), was chaired by **Rani Lakshmi Bhai Rajawade**.
- **Rani of Jhansi Regiment (1943)** — commanded by **Captain Lakshmi Swaminathan (Sahgal)**.
- Congress conditions (1939): **constituent assembly after the war** + **responsible government at the Centre immediately**; rejected by **Linlithgow**.
- **Wardha CWC**: Gandhi wanted **unconditional support**; Bose, **Acharya Narendra Dev, Jayaprakash Narayan** wanted struggle; **Nehru's** position adopted.
- **Linlithgow's statement — 17 October 1939**; CWC asked **Congress ministries to resign — 23 October 1939**.
- Secretary of State **Zetland** branded the Congress a purely Hindu organisation.
- Linlithgow, **January 1940**: "Dominion status of Westminster variety, after the war".
- **Ramgarh session (March 1940)** — president **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad**.
- **Pakistan Resolution** — Muslim League, **Lahore, March 1940**.
- **August Offer (August 1940)** — first recognition of Indians' **inherent right to frame their constitution**; Nehru: **"Dominion status concept is dead as a doornail."**
- **July 1941** — Viceroy's executive council: Indians a majority of **8 out of 12** for the first time; a **National Defence Council** set up (advisory).
- **Individual satyagraha (October 1940)** — first **Vinoba Bhave**, second **Nehru**; **25,000** convicted by May 1941; also called the **'Delhi Chalo Movement'**.
- Gandhi chose **Nehru as successor** — explained in Rajmohan Gandhi's **The Good Boatman**.
- **Cripps Mission (March 1942)** — **Stafford Cripps**, left-wing Labourite and member of the War Cabinet.
- Cripps: secession by **60 per cent** legislature vote, otherwise a **plebiscite of adult males**.
- Congress negotiators with Cripps: **Nehru and Maulana Azad**.
- Cripps undermined by **Churchill, Amery, Linlithgow and Ward**; talks broke down on the **viceroy's veto**.
- Gandhi called the Cripps scheme **"a post-dated cheque"**.
