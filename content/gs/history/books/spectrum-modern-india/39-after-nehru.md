## Gulzarilal Nanda and the Syndicate

- **1963:** The Syndicate was formed. It was a group in the Congress, and it included the party president K. Kamaraj. It guided both successions that followed.
- **May 1964:** Nehru died. Gulzarilal Nanda became interim prime minister.
- **January 1966:** Nanda became interim prime minister a second time, after Shastri's death.

## Lal Bahadur Shastri (1964–66)

Shastri faced three crises. They were the anti-Hindi agitation in Madras (1965), food shortages and the second war with Pakistan.

### Coming to power

1. **2 October 1904:** Lal Bahadur Shastri was born.
2. **1928:** Kashi Vidyapeeth, Varanasi, gave him the title 'Shastri'.
3. As a minister in Uttar Pradesh (UP) he appointed women bus conductors. He also ordered the use of water jets instead of lathis.
4. **1956:** He resigned as railways minister over a rail accident.
5. **1961:** As home minister he formed the Committee on Prevention of Corruption. K. Santhanam headed it. Its recommendations led to the Central Vigilance Commission.
6. **9 June 1964:** He was sworn in as the second prime minister.
7. He was the first prime minister to have a secretary to the prime minister. The secretary was L.K. Jha. This was the foundation of the Prime Minister's Office (PMO).

### Economy and welfare

- **Green Revolution:** Its base was laid under Shastri, with C. Subramaniam. It had a threefold thrust: technological, economic and organisational.
- **Dr B.P. Pal:** He was the first scientist to head the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR).
- **M.S. Swaminathan:** He was at the Indian Agricultural Research Institute (IARI). He flagged Norman Borlaug's wheat.
- **White Revolution:** Shastri also set it in motion. It was a national campaign to raise milk production and supply.
- **October 1964:** Shastri opened Amul's cattle feed factory at Kanjari.
- **January 1965:** The Agricultural Prices Commission (APC) and the Food Corporation of India (FCI) were set up.
- **1965:** The National Dairy Development Board (NDDB) was set up at Anand, with Verghese Kurien.
- **1965:** India imported 250 tonnes of wheat seed.
- **November 1965:** The Subramaniam-Freeman accord was reached at Rome. Under it India was to end food grain imports by 1971.
- **PL-480:** US President Lyndon B. Johnson threatened to stop wheat under PL-480. Shastri urged people to skip a meal a week.

### Foreign policy and security

1. **1964:** The Bandaranaike-Shastri accord was made. It provided for the repatriation of 6,00,000 Indian Tamils to India. It also provided citizenship for 3,75,000 Tamils in Ceylon. Both were to be done by 1981. The accord was later considered lapsed.
2. **April 1965:** Pakistan probed the Rann of Kachchh, and there were clashes. A ceasefire came through Britain.
3. **August 1965:** Pakistan launched Operation Grandslam in Kashmir. The defence minister was Y.B. Chavan.
4. During the 1965 war Shastri gave the slogan 'Jai Jawan Jai Kisan'.
5. **23 September 1965:** A United Nations (UN) ceasefire ended the fighting.
6. **December 1965:** Shastri visited Rangoon and met General Ne Win.
7. **10 January 1966:** Ayub Khan and Shastri signed the Tashkent Declaration. The Soviet mediator was Alexei Kosygin. Both sides were to withdraw by 25 February 1966 to the positions held before 5 August 1965. The declaration had no no-war pact. Shastri died the next morning.

### Science and technology

- **1965:** Shastri inaugurated the plutonium reprocessing plant at Trombay.
- **SNEPP:** It was set up at Homi Bhabha's initiative.

## Indira Gandhi: first phase (1966–77)

### Coming to power

1. **19 November 1917:** Indira Gandhi was born at Allahabad.
2. At twelve she led a children's group, the Vanar Sena.
3. Her sons were Rajiv and Sanjay.
4. **19 January 1966:** She became the third prime minister. She was the first woman prime minister.
5. P.N. Haksar was the first principal secretary to a prime minister.

### Politics and elections

1. **1967:** The Congress won the polls with a thin majority. SVD (Samyukta Vidhayak Dal) governments were formed in states. In Madras the DMK formed the first non-Congress government under C.N. Annadurai. Morarji Desai became deputy prime minister and finance minister.
2. **August 1969:** A presidential election was held after Zakir Hussain's death. Indira backed V.V. Giri against the official candidate, N. Sanjiva Reddy. Reddy lost to Giri.
3. **November 1969:** S. Nijalingappa expelled Indira, and the party split. She led the Congress (R), the 'Requisitionists'. The other side was the Congress (O), the 'Organisation', headed by Ram Subhag Singh.
4. **1971:** Indira won the election on the slogan 'Garibi Hatao'. The Grand Alliance of the Swatantra, the Congress (O) and the SSP answered with 'Indira Hatao'. For the first time the national polls were delinked from the state polls.

### Economy and welfare

1. **June 1966:** The rupee was devalued by 36.5%.
2. **1966-69:** These were years of annual plans. The Fourth Plan ran from 1969-74. The Fifth Plan was for 1974-79 but ended in 1978. ⚠ May be outdated: five-year plans and the Planning Commission. The Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog (2015), and five-year plans ended in 2017.
3. **July 1969:** 14 banks were nationalised by ordinance. 6 more were nationalised in 1980.
4. **1969:** The MRTP Act was passed. It came after the abolition of the managing agency system. ⚠ May be outdated: the MRTP Act (1969) and FERA. MRTP was repealed and replaced by the Competition Act, and FERA by FEMA.
5. **1973:** The oil companies were nationalised. They had refused fuel to Indian forces in the 1971 war.

### Laws and the Constitution

1. **1967:** The Official Languages Act was amended. This brought de facto bilingualism, that is, two languages in practice.
2. **1969:** A bill to end the privy purses failed in the Rajya Sabha by one vote. Privy purses were payments to the former princes, under Article 291.
3. **1971:** The 24th Amendment said that Parliament may amend any provision of the Constitution. It is linked with the Golaknath case.
4. **1971:** The 26th Amendment abolished the privy purses. It inserted Article 363-A.
5. **1972:** The 25th Amendment dealt with the directive principles.
6. **1972 to 1974:** Three steps for the environment came in these years. They were the Wildlife Protection Act 1972, Project Tiger 1973 and the Water Act 1974.
7. **1973:** A.N. Ray was made Chief Justice, superseding three senior judges.
8. **1975:** The 38th Amendment made the satisfaction behind an emergency non-justiciable, that is, not open to challenge in court. The 39th Amendment sent disputes about the prime minister's election to a special court.
9. **1976:** The 42nd Amendment was called a 'mini constitution'. Its main points are listed below.

Main points of the 42nd Amendment:

- It added the words 'socialist secular' and 'integrity' to the Preamble.
- It added the fundamental duties.
- It made the term of the Lok Sabha six years.
- It froze seats at the 1971 census, till the first census after 2000. ⚠ May be outdated: the freeze was later extended (to the first census after 2026).
- It allowed President's rule without approval for one year.

### States and regional movements

1. **1966:** Punjab was reorganised and Haryana was formed. Chandigarh became a shared union territory. Darshan Singh Pherumal fasted unto death.
2. **1966:** The Mizo National Front (MNF) under Laldenga revolted. The Indian Air Force (IAF) struck Aizawl. Mizoram became a union territory in January 1972 and a state in 1987.
3. **1966:** Bal Thackeray founded the Shiv Sena.
4. **1967:** The Naxalbari rising took place under Kanu Sanyal.
5. **1969:** The CPI(ML) was formed. Charu Mazumdar led it.
6. **1972:** Meghalaya, Manipur and Tripura got statehood. NEFA became the union territory of Arunachal Pradesh.
7. **1975:** Indira Gandhi declared Jammu and Kashmir (J&K) a constituent of India. ⚠ May be outdated: this, and the National Conference and state set-up. J&K's special status (Article 370) was ended and the state was reorganised into union territories in 2019.
8. **May 1975:** Sikkim became the 22nd state by the 36th Amendment. It had been a protectorate since 1950.
9. **November 1975:** The Shillong Accord was signed. Nagaland had become a state in 1963.

### Foreign policy and security

1. **1968:** RAW (Research and Analysis Wing) was set up. Its head was R.N. Kao.
2. **August 1971:** The Indo-Soviet Treaty of Peace, Friendship and Cooperation was signed. Swaran Singh and Gromyko signed it.
3. **3 December 1971:** Pakistan attacked, and the 1971 war began. Sam Manekshaw is a name linked with this war.
4. In Operation Trident, Vice Admiral S.N. Kohli of the Western Naval Command hit Karachi. Vice Admiral Nilakanta Krishnan of the Eastern command set up a blockade. INS Vikrant took part.
5. **6 December 1971:** India recognised Bangladesh. The US sent Task Force 74, with USS Enterprise.
6. **16 December 1971:** A.A.K. Niazi surrendered to Jagjit Singh Aurora at Ramna Race Course, Dacca.
7. **2 July 1972:** The Simla Agreement was signed, after a summit in June 1972. It had 'renunciation of force' instead of a no-war pact. The Line of Control (LoC) came from the ceasefire of 17 December 1971. Troops were to withdraw within 30 days.
8. **1974:** Katchatheevu was ceded to Sri Lanka.

### Science and technology

- **18 May 1974:** Pokhran I, called 'Smiling Buddha', was carried out.
- **People:** Raja Ramanna was director of the Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC). Homi Sethna was chairman of the Atomic Energy Commission (AEC).
- **Plutonium:** It came from Canada's CIRUS reactor.
- **Result abroad:** The test led to the Nuclear Suppliers Group.

### The road to the Emergency

1. **December 1973-March 1974:** The Nav Nirman movement took place in Gujarat. Chief Minister Chimanbhai Patel resigned.
2. After it, student protests began in Bihar. Jayaprakash Narayan (JP) took them up, and this became the JP movement.
3. JP called for 'Total Revolution'. He demanded that the Bihar Congress government resign and the assembly be dissolved. The non-left opposition backed him.
4. **May 1974:** An all-India railway strike took place.
5. **12 June 1975:** Justice Sinha of the Allahabad High Court voided Indira's 1971 election. He did so on Raj Narain's petition.
6. **24 June 1975:** V.R. Krishna Iyer gave a conditional stay.

### The Emergency (1975–77)

1. **25-26 June 1975:** President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed proclaimed the Emergency under Article 352(1).
2. Leaders were arrested under MISA (Maintenance of Internal Security Act).
3. Four bodies were banned: the Anand Marg, the RSS, the Jamaat-i-Islami and the CPI(ML).
4. The CPI supported the Emergency. The CPM opposed it.
5. Sanjay Gandhi drove sterilisation and demolitions.
6. **1976:** Dev Kant Baruah said, "India is Indira, and Indira is India".
7. **1976:** The Guwahati session of the AICC (All India Congress Committee) was Sanjay Gandhi's debut.
8. **21 March 1977:** The Emergency ended.

## Janata government (1977-80): Morarji Desai and Charan Singh

### Coming to power

1. The Janata Party was made up of the Jan Sangh, the Congress (O), the BLD (Bharatiya Lok Dal) of Charan Singh and the Socialist Party.
2. The CFD (Congress for Democracy) allied with it and later merged. Its leaders were Jagjivan Ram, Nandini Satpathy and H.N. Bahuguna.
3. **February 1977:** President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed died.
4. **March 1977:** The Janata Party won 330 of 542 seats. Its symbol was the 'chakra-haldhar'.
5. **23 March 1977:** Morarji Desai was sworn in. He was the first non-Congress prime minister. Kripalani and JP chose him.
6. **June 1977:** N. Sanjiva Reddy was elected President.

### Politics and elections

1. The Shah Commission, under Justice J.C. Shah, probed the excesses of the Emergency.
2. **May 1977:** Harijans were burnt at Belchi in Bihar. Indira reached the village, riding the last stretch on an elephant. The visit revived her.
3. **January 1978:** The Congress (I) was formed. Indira won from Chikmagalur.
4. **January 1979:** The Mandal Commission was set up. It was the Second Backward Classes Commission. Its chairman was Bindeshwari Prasad Mandal (B.P. Mandal), a former chief minister of Bihar.

### Economy and welfare

- **April 1977:** The Food for Work programme began.
- **Sixth Plan (1978-83):** The Janata plan was a 'rolling plan'.
- **George Fernandes:** He expelled Coca-Cola and IBM.
- **Madhu Dandavate:** He cushioned the second-class sleepers.

### Laws and the Constitution

The 43rd Amendment (1977) and the 44th Amendment (1978) undid the changes of the Emergency. The law minister was Shanti Bhushan. Their main points were:

- 'Armed rebellion' replaced 'internal disturbances' as a ground for emergency.
- The written advice of the cabinet was made necessary.
- Articles 20 and 21 can never be suspended.
- The right to property (Article 31) was removed from the Fundamental Rights.

### Foreign policy and security

- **Jimmy Carter:** He was the first US president to visit India after Eisenhower.
- **1979:** Vajpayee visited Beijing.

### How the term ended

- **July 1979:** The Desai government resigned.
- **Charan Singh:** He never faced Parliament, because Indira withdrew support.

## Indira Gandhi: second phase (1980-84)

### Coming to power

- **1980:** Indira won from both Rae Bareilly and Medak. She kept Medak.
- **June 1980:** Sanjay died. Rajiv was elected from Amethi.

### Economy and welfare

- **New Sixth Plan:** It ran from 1980-85.
- **2 October 1980:** The IRDP (Integrated Rural Development Programme) was launched.
- **October 1980:** The NREP (National Rural Employment Programme) was launched.
- **1982:** NABARD (National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development) was set up. It is linked with the Shivaraman Committee.
- **1982:** Operation Forward was launched.

### Laws and the Constitution

- **1980:** The Forest (Conservation) Act was passed.
- **1981:** The Air Act was passed.

### States and regional movements

- **Jharkhand:** A movement for statehood went on there. It was then part of Bihar.
- **NSCN:** Muivah set it up.
- **1982:** N.T. Rama Rao's Telugu Desam was formed.

The Punjab crisis, in date order:

1. **1973:** The Anandpur Resolution was passed.
2. Bhindranwale was head of the Damdami Taksal.
3. **June 1980:** Khalistan was declared. Its president was Jagjit Singh Chauhan, in London.
4. **Republic Day 1983:** The Akali MLAs resigned.
5. **June 1984:** Operation Blue Star was carried out. Army Chief A.S. Vaidya recommended it. The troops were under Maj. Gen. K.S. Brar and were directed by Gen. K. Sundarji. Bhindranwale and Shubeg Singh were killed.

### Foreign policy and security

- **1983:** The summit of the Non-Aligned Movement (NAM) was held at Delhi. Indira was its chairperson.
- **April 1984:** India launched Operation Meghdoot in Siachen. The line there was undemarcated beyond the point NJ9842. India secured Sia La and Bilfond La. Pakistan kept Gyong La.

### Science and technology

- **2 April 1984:** Rakesh Sharma went into space in Soyuz T-11, to Salyut 7. He is remembered for the words "Sare jahan se achcha".

### How the term ended

- **31 October 1984:** Beant Singh and Satwant Singh assassinated Indira. President Zail Singh swore in Rajiv Gandhi.

## Rajiv Gandhi (October 1984 to December 1989)

### Coming to power

1. Rajiv Gandhi's term began with the anti-Sikh riots and the Bhopal gas tragedy.
2. **3 December 1984:** Methyl isocyanate (MIC) gas leaked from the Union Carbide plant at Bhopal. Union Carbide (UCC) paid USD 470 million as a full and final settlement. ⚠ May be outdated: the Bhopal settlement described as "full and final". The government later sought more compensation from UCC (curative petition, dismissed in 2023).
3. **December 1984:** The Congress (I) won more than 400 seats in the election. Rajiv Gandhi was the youngest prime minister.
4. **March 1985:** The Bhopal Gas Leak Disaster Act made the government the sole representative of the victims.

### Politics and elections

1. **1 February 1986:** The Faizabad district judge ordered the locks of the Babri Mosque opened.
2. **March 1986:** The Bofors deal was made. It was for 400 155mm howitzers, at USD 285 million (about Rs 1500 crore).
3. **April 1987:** A Swedish radio report alleged bribes in the deal. Chitra Subramaniam of The Hindu investigated it. Ottavio Quattrocchi of Snamprogetti was named.

Two farmers' bodies grew in these years:

- **Shetkari Sanghatana:** Sharad Joshi led it in Maharashtra.
- **Bharatiya Kisan Union:** Mahendra Singh Tikait led it in Punjab and Haryana.

### Economy and welfare

- **NPE 1986:** This National Policy on Education (NPE) brought Operation Blackboard, IGNOU and a Navodaya Vidyalaya in every district. The first NPE was in 1968.
- **April 1989:** The NREP and the RLEGP were merged into the Jawahar Rozgar Yojana.

### Laws and the Constitution

1. **January 1985:** The anti-defection law was passed.
2. **1985:** In the Shah Bano case the Supreme Court (SC) applied Section 125 CrPC. ⚠ May be outdated: Shah Bano and Section 125 CrPC. The CrPC was replaced by the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (in force from July 2024).
3. **1986:** The Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986 reversed the Shah Bano judgment. It limited maintenance to the iddat period. Arif Mohammed Khan resigned in protest.
4. **1986:** The Environment Protection Act was passed.
5. **1991:** The Public Liability Insurance Act was passed.

### States and regional movements

1. **July 1985:** The Rajiv–Longowal accord was signed on Punjab. Longowal was shot dead in August.
2. **15 August 1985:** The Assam Accord was signed with the AASU (All Assam Students' Union). The AASU became the Asom Gana Parishad and won the December elections.
3. **February 1987:** Mizoram became the 23rd state. Laldenga of the Mizo National Front became chief minister.
4. **1987:** The J&K elections were rigged against the Muslim United Front. This marked the beginning of militancy in Kashmir.
5. **May 1988:** Operation Black Thunder was carried out. The police were under J.F. Ribeiro and K.P.S. Gill.
6. **August 1988:** The TNV of Tripura signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU).
7. In Darjeeling the GNLF (Gorkha National Liberation Front) under Subhash Ghisingh accepted an autonomous district council.

### Foreign policy and security

| Operation | Date | Place |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operation Flowers are Blooming | 1986 | Seychelles |
| Operation Rajiv | 1987 | Quaid Post, Siachen |
| Operation Poomalai (Eagle Mission 4) | 4 June 1987 | Air-drop over Jaffna |
| Operation Cactus | 1988 | Maldives |

- **29 July 1987:** Rajiv Gandhi and J.R. Jayawardene signed the Indo-Sri Lanka Accord at Colombo. It sent the IPKF (Indian Peace Keeping Force).
- **December 1988:** Rajiv Gandhi visited China. He was the first prime minister to do so since Nehru (1954). He met Deng Xiaoping. A Joint Working Group was set up on the boundary.

### Science and technology

- **Six technology missions:** They covered drinking water, milk, edible oil, immunisation, literacy and telecom.
- **Sam Pitroda:** He chaired the Telecom Commission.
- **MTNL:** It was set up in this period.

### How the term ended

- **21 May 1991:** An LTTE suicide bomber assassinated Rajiv Gandhi at Sriperumbudur in Tamil Nadu.

## V.P. Singh and the National Front (December 1989 to November 1990)

### Coming to power

1. The Janata Dal was formed from the Jan Morcha of V.P. Singh, the Janata Party, the Lok Dal and the Congress (S).
2. Its coalition with regional parties was the National Front.
3. **1989:** This was the first election in which no single party won a majority.
4. **2 December 1989:** V.P. Singh was sworn in. The BJP and the Left supported him from outside.
5. He recalled the IPKF.

### Politics and elections

1. **December 1980:** The Mandal Commission reported. Officially it was the Backward Classes Commission.
2. **7 August 1990:** V.P. Singh accepted the Mandal report. He announced 27% reservation for OBCs.
3. The rise of Lalu Yadav in Bihar and Mulayam Singh Yadav in UP as OBC leaders was part of his Mandal calculation.
4. **September 1990:** L.K. Advani's Ram Rath Yatra began at Somnath.
5. Bihar Chief Minister Lalu Prasad Yadav stopped the yatra and had Advani arrested in Bihar. Mulayam Singh's UP government arrested the kar sevaks.
6. **September 1991:** An order of the Rao government gave preference to poorer OBCs within the 27%.
7. **November 1992:** The Supreme Court upheld the Mandal order 7–3. Seven judges upheld it and three dissented. The court set a 50% ceiling. It allowed no caste criterion in promotions.

### States and regional movements

- **December 1989:** The JKLF kidnapped the daughter of Home Minister Mufti Mohammad Sayeed.
- **Jagmohan:** He became governor of J&K.
- **Kashmiri Pandits:** They fled the valley.

### How the term ended

- **November 1990:** The BJP withdrew support after Advani's arrest, and the government fell.

## Chandra Shekhar (November 1990 to June 1991)

- Chandra Shekhar and Devi Lal formed the Samajwadi Janata Party.
- **10 November 1990:** Chandra Shekhar was sworn in. He had only 64 MPs.
- **March 1991:** Gold was pledged to meet the balance-of-payments crisis. The finance minister was Yashwant Sinha.

## P.V. Narasimha Rao (1991–96)

### Coming to power

- **1991 election:** The Congress won 244 seats.
- **Nandyal:** Narasimha Rao entered the Lok Sabha through a by-election from Nandyal in Andhra Pradesh.
- **Manmohan Singh:** He was Chairman of the UGC (University Grants Commission) when Rao made him finance minister.
- **Industries:** Rao himself held the industries portfolio.

### Politics and elections

1. **6 December 1992:** The Babri Masjid was demolished. Kalyan Singh was chief minister of UP.
2. The Liberhan Commission was appointed ten days later. It reported in June 2009 and named 68 people culpable.
3. **July 1993:** In a no-confidence vote, members of the JMM and the Janata Dal (Ajit Singh) voted against the motion. This became the JMM bribery case. Rao and Buta Singh were convicted, then cleared in 2002.
4. Kanshi Ram founded BAMCEF and then the BSP.
5. **June 1995:** Mayawati became the first Dalit woman chief minister of UP. She held the post again in 2007–2012.

### Economy and welfare

Rao and Manmohan Singh brought the New Economic Policy 1991. The reforms of 1991 were:

- The rupee was devalued.
- The Industrial Policy 1991 was announced.
- The MRTP Act was modified.
- The Controller of Capital Issues was abolished.
- The SEBI Act 1992 was passed.
- The NSE was started.

### Laws and the Constitution

- **73rd Amendment:** It added Part IX and the Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects).
- **74th Amendment:** It added Part IX-A, on municipalities.

### Foreign policy and security

- **Look East policy:** Rao launched it, towards ASEAN.
- **1993:** Rao visited China.
