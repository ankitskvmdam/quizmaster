## Two strands of the last two years

The last two years of British rule had two strands.

1. **Negotiations:** The government, the Congress and the League held tortuous negotiations. These ended in freedom and partition amid communal violence.
2. **Mass action:** There was sporadic, militant mass action. It included the INA release movement, the RIN revolt, Tebhaga, Worli, the Punjab Kisan Morchas, Punnapra-Vayalar and Telangana.

## The new government and its promises (1945)

1. **June 1945:** The Congress leaders were released.
2. **July 1945:** The Labour Party formed the government in Britain. Clement Attlee became PM. Pethick Lawrence became Secretary of State for India.
3. **August 1945:** Elections were announced.
4. **September 1945:** A constituent assembly was promised.

### Reasons

1. The USA and the USSR favoured Indian freedom.
2. Britain was war-weary.
3. There was fear of a 1942-type revolt.
4. The last elections had been held long before: in 1934 for the Centre and in 1937 for the provinces.

## INA trials

1. **September 1945:** The first post-War Congress session was held. This Bombay session passed a resolution supporting the INA cause.
2. **5–11 November:** INA week was observed.
3. **November 1945:** The first INA trial was held at the Red Fort, Delhi.
4. **12 November 1945:** INA Day was observed.

The first trial put three men in the dock together:

- **A Hindu:** Prem Kumar Sehgal.
- **A Muslim:** Shah Nawaz Khan.
- **A Sikh:** Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon.

Five men defended them: Bhulabhai Desai, Tej Bahadur Sapru, Kailash Nath Katju (K.N. Katju), Jawaharlal Nehru and Asaf Ali.

### RIN revolt

RIN stands for the Royal Indian Navy. Ratings are naval sailors.

1. **18 February 1946:** About 1100 RIN ratings of HMIS Talwar struck in Bombay.
2. The rebels hoisted the tricolour, crescent and hammer-and-sickle flags.
3. The Royal Indian Air Force held sympathy strikes at Bombay, Poona, Calcutta, Jessore and Ambala.
4. **23 February 1946:** Patel and Jinnah persuaded the ratings to surrender. A Maratha battalion rounded them up.

### The Congress view

- Gandhi said that if the ratings mutinied for freedom they were "doubly wrong".
- The Congress did not back the upsurges. Its reasons were their tactics and timing.

## Elections of 1945–46

- The 1945-46 election campaign glorified the martyrs of 1942.
- The franchise (the right to vote) was under 10% for the provinces. It was under 1% for the Central Assembly.

| Point | Congress | League |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Votes | 91% of non-Muslim votes | 86.6% of Muslim votes |
| Central Assembly | 57 of 102 seats | All 30 reserved seats |
| Provinces | No majority in Bengal, Sindh and Punjab | Majority in Bengal and Sindh |

In Punjab a Unionist-Congress-Akali coalition was formed under Khizr Hayat Khan.

## Cabinet Mission (1946)

The Cabinet Mission reached Delhi on 24 March 1946. It had three members.

| Member | Post |
| --- | --- |
| Pethick Lawrence | Chairman of the mission |
| Stafford Cripps | President, Board of Trade |
| A.V. Alexander | First Lord of Admiralty |

### The plan (May 1946)

- The mission rejected full Pakistan. In it, non-Muslims would be 38% in the North-West and 48% in the North-East.
- The provinces were grouped into three sections.
- The constituent assembly was to have 389 members: 292 from the provinces, 4 from the chief commissioner's provinces and 93 from the princely states.
- A province could leave a group after the first general elections. It could seek reconsideration after 10 years.

| Section | Provinces |
| --- | --- |
| Section A | Madras, Bombay, Central Provinces (CP), United Provinces (UP), Bihar, Orissa |
| Section B | Punjab, North-West Frontier Province (NWFP), Sindh |
| Section C | Bengal, Assam |

### Wavell's 'Breakdown Plan' (May 1946)

- The plan was for a British withdrawal to the Muslim provinces of the North-West and North-East.

### Response

1. **6 June 1946:** The League accepted the plan.
2. **24 June 1946:** The Congress accepted the plan.
3. **10 July:** Nehru made a statement.
4. **29 July 1946:** After this statement the League withdrew its acceptance. It called for "direct action" from 16 August.
5. Riots followed. The worst-hit areas were Calcutta, Bombay, Noakhali, Bihar and Garhmukteshwar (UP).

## Interim Government and Constituent Assembly

1. **2 September 1946:** The Interim Government under Nehru was sworn in.
2. **26 October 1946:** The League joined the Interim Government. It then obstructed it.
3. **9 December 1946:** The Constituent Assembly first met. It passed the Objectives Resolution, which Nehru had drafted.

## Growth of communalism

The chapter traces communalism and the two-nation theory.

### Three stages

1. Communal nationalism.
2. Liberal communalism.
3. Extreme communalism.

### Milestones

1. **1887:** Syed Ahmed Khan and Raja Shiv Prasad of Bhinga were propped up as an anti-Congress front.
2. **1906:** The Shimla deputation met Lord Minto. The Agha Khan led it.
3. **1906:** The Muslim League was founded. Its founders were the Agha Khan, Nawab Salimullah of Dacca, Mohsin-ul-Mulk and Waqar-ul-Mulk.
4. **1909:** Separate electorates came.
5. **1909:** The Punjab Hindu Sabha was formed. U.N. Mukherjee and Lal Chand were its leaders.
6. **April 1915:** The Hindu Mahasabha held its first session. The Maharaja of Kasim Bazar presided.
7. **1920s:** The Arya Samaj ran Shuddhi and Sangathan. Muslims ran Tabligh and Tanzeem.
8. **1925:** The RSS was formed.
9. **Early 1930s:** Rahmat Ali gave the idea of a separate Muslim nation. Iqbal developed it.
10. **1932:** The Communal Award came.
11. **24 March 1940:** The Pakistan Resolution was passed at the Lahore session.
