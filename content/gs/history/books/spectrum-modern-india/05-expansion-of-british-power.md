## Gist
*Book pages 81–135.*

Historians debate whether the conquest was accidental — **John Seeley**: made "in a **fit of absent-mindedness**" — or intentional; the book finds trade-protection motives first and a conscious imperial plan later (**1798–1818**, Wellesley and Hastings). The British period is dated variously from **1740**, **1757** or **1761**. British success rested on **superior arms**, **discipline and regular salary**, merit-based selection, **leadership and second-line leaders**, **strong finances** and **national pride**.

**Bengal.** Bengal was the richest Mughal province and its Nawab also controlled **Bihar and Odisha**; nearly **60 per cent** of British imports from Asia came from Bengal, and the English had factories at **Balasore, Hooghly, Kasimbazar, Patna and Dacca** (1630s). **Alivardi Khan**, deputy governor of Bihar, seized power and ruled till **1756**; his grandson **Siraj-ud-daula** faced **Shaukat Jang (Nawab of Purnea)**, **Ghasiti Begum** and **Mir Jafar**. Clive conspired with **Mir Jafar, Rai Durlabh, Jagat Seth and Omichand**, and won **Plassey (23 June 1757)**; Siraj was killed on **Miran's** orders. Mir Jafar gave the **24 Parganas**. The Dutch were beaten at **Bedara (1759)**. By the **treaty of 1760**, **Mir Kasim** ceded **Burdwan, Midnapur and Chittagong**. He moved his capital to **Munger in Bihar**, clashed with **Ram Narayan, deputy governor of Bihar** (backed by the English at **Patna**), and abolished all duties because of the **misuse of the dastak**. War broke out in **1763**; Mir Kasim, **Shuja-ud-daulah** and **Shah Alam II** were defeated by **Major Hector Munro** at **Buxar (22 October 1764)**. By the **Treaties of Allahabad (August 1765)** Shuja gave up **Allahabad and Kara** and paid **Rs 50 lakh**; Shah Alam II granted the **diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa** for **Rs 26 lakh** a year. Clive's **Dual Government (1765–72)** used two deputy diwans — **Mohammad Reza Khan (Bengal)** and **Raja Sitab Roy (Bihar)** — until **Warren Hastings** ended it in **1772**.

**Mysore.** **Haidar Ali** became de facto ruler in **1761** and set up a French-aided arms factory at **Dindigul**. Wars: **First (1767–69)** — **Treaty of Madras**; **Second (1780–84)** — Haidar lost at **Porto Novo (1781)**, died **7 December 1782**, **Treaty of Mangalore (1784)**; **Third** — **Cornwallis**, **Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)**, half of Mysore lost; **Fourth (1799)** — Tipu died at Seringapatam under **Wellesley**. **Tipu** set up a **Board of Admiralty (1796)**, a **Jacobin Club (1797)** and pioneered **rockets** and **sericulture**.

**Marathas.** The confederacy of **Gaekwad (Baroda), Bhonsle (Nagpur), Holkar (Indore), Sindhia (Gwalior) and the Peshwa (Poona)** weakened after Panipat and the death of **Madhavrao I (1772)**. **First war (1775–82)**: **Treaty of Surat (1775)** with Raghunathrao, **Purandhar (1776)**, **Wadgaon (1779)**, **Salbai (1782)** with **Mahadji Sindhia** as guarantor. **Second (1803–05)**: **Bajirao II** signed the **Treaty of Bassein (31 December 1802)** — "the key to India". **Third (1817–19)**: defeats at **Khirki, Sitabuldi and Mahidpur**; the peshwaship was abolished (1818) and **Bajirao II** was sent to **Bithur**.

**Sindh** (Talpura Amirs): Treaty of **'Eternal Friendship'** under **Minto**, treaty of **1832 (Pottinger)**, protectorate **1838**, subsidiary alliance **1839**, annexed **1843** under **Ellenborough** with **Charles Napier** as first governor. **Punjab**: **Ranjit Singh** signed the **Treaty of Amritsar (1809)** fixing the **Sutlej** boundary. **First Anglo-Sikh War (1845–46)** — **Treaty of Lahore** and **Treaty of Bhairowal**; Kashmir sold to **Gulab Singh**. **Second (1848–49)** — Multan revolt under **Mulraj**, battles of **Ramnagar, Chillhanwala and Gujarat**; Punjab annexed by **Dalhousie**.

**Administrative expansion:** Warren Hastings' **ring-fence**, Wellesley's **subsidiary alliance** (first trap: **Awadh, 1765**; first Wellesley ally: **Hyderabad, 1798**) and Dalhousie's **doctrine of lapse** (**Satara 1848**, **Jhansi**, **Nagpur 1854**); **Awadh** was annexed for **misgovernment (1856)**.

**Neighbours:** **Nepal — Treaty of Sagauli (1816)**; **Burma** — **Yandabo (1826)**, Pegu (1852), Upper Burma (1885); **Tibet — Younghusband**, **Treaty of Lhasa (1904)**; **Afghanistan** — Auckland's **forward policy**, **First Afghan War (1839–42)**, **Lawrence's masterly inactivity**, **Lytton's proud reserve**, **Treaty of Gandamak (1879)**; **Durand Line (1893)**; **Curzon** created the **NWFP**.

## Key facts
- **John Seeley**: conquest of India made blindly, "in a **fit of absent-mindedness**".
- The British 'second empire' began around **1783 (Peace of Paris)**.
- B.L. Grover: from **1798 to 1818** British motives were **consciously imperialistic**.
- Beginning of British period variously dated **1740** (Anglo-French struggle), **1757** (Plassey) or **1761** (Third Panipat).
- Nawab of Bengal had authority over **Bihar and Odisha**; nearly **60 per cent** of British imports from Asia came from **Bengal**.
- 1630s English factories in Bengal: **Balasore, Hooghly, Kasimbazar, Patna and Dacca**; the Company paid **Rs 3,000** a year to trade freely in Bengal.
- Calcutta's population rose from **15,000 (1706)** to **100,000 (1750)**.
- **Murshid Quli Khan** became Dewan of Bengal in **1700**, ruled till **1727**; **Alivardi Khan** (Deputy Governor of Bihar) ruled till **1756**.
- **Siraj-ud-daula** (grandson of Alivardi): rivals **Shaukat Jang (Nawab of Purnea)**, aunt **Ghasiti Begum**, commander **Mir Jafar** (replaced by **Mir Madan**); **Mohan Lal**, a Kashmiri, acted almost as prime minister.
- English gave asylum to **Krishna Das**, son of Raj Ballabh, who fled with treasure.
- 'Black Hole Tragedy': **146** English said to be imprisoned, **123** died — historians doubt it.
- Clive's conspirators: **Mir Jafar, Rai Durlabh, Jagat Seth, Omichand**.
- **Battle of Plassey — June 23, 1757**; Siraj's **50,000**-strong army defeated; Siraj murdered on the order of **Miran** (Mir Jafar's son).
- Mir Jafar gave the English the **zamindari of 24 Parganas**; Plassey is regarded as the **starting point of British rule**.
- Mir Jafar's Dutch conspiracy (Chinsura) ended with Dutch defeat at **Bedara, November 1759**.
- **Treaty of 1760 (Vansittart–Mir Kasim)**: Mir Kasim ceded **Burdwan, Midnapur and Chittagong**; half share of the **chunam trade of Sylhet**; **Rs 5 lakh** for the Company's southern wars; Mir Jafar got a pension of **Rs 1,500** a year.
- **Mir Kasim** (ablest successor of Alivardi) shifted the capital from **Murshidabad to Munger in Bihar**.
- **Ram Narayan, deputy governor of Bihar**, refused Mir Kasim revenue accounts, backed by English officials of **Patna**.
- **Dastak** — trade permit exempting goods from duties; its misuse led **Mir Kasim to abolish all duties**.
- English victories of 1763: **Katwah, Murshidabad, Giria, Sooty, Munger**.
- **Battle of Buxar — October 22, 1764**: **Major Hector Munro** defeated **Mir Kasim, Shuja-ud-daulah (Awadh) and Shah Alam II**; the Mughal Emperor too was defeated.
- After Buxar, Mir Jafar ceded **Midnapore, Burdwan and Chittagong** for the army; duty-free trade except **2% duty on salt**; then minor **Najim-ud-daula** became nawab.
- **Robert Clive**: Governor of Bengal **1757–60** and **1765–67**; allegedly committed suicide in **1774**.
- **Treaty of Allahabad (August 1765)** — **Shuja-ud-Daula**: surrender **Allahabad and Kara**, pay **Rs 50 lakh**, give **Balwant Singh (Banaras)** his estate; Awadh became a **buffer state**.
- **Treaty of Allahabad** — **Shah Alam II**: reside at Allahabad; **diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa** for **Rs 26 lakh** a year; **Rs 53 lakh** for nizamat functions.
- Mir Kasim died a homeless wanderer in **June 1777**.
- **Dual Government (1765–72)**: diwani from the **emperor**, nizamat from the **subahdar**; deputy diwans **Mohammad Reza Khan (Bengal)** and **Raja Sitab Roy (Bihar)**; ended by **Warren Hastings in 1772**.
- Wodeyar kingdom of Mysore emerged in **1612**; **Haidar Ali** (born **1721**) became de facto ruler in **1761**; arms factory at **Dindigul** with French help.
- Marathas under **Madhavrao** defeated Haidar in **1764, 1766 and 1771**.
- **First Anglo-Mysore War (1767–69)**: **Treaty of Madras, April 4, 1769**.
- **Second Anglo-Mysore War**: dispute over **Mahe**; Haidar defeated **Colonel Baillie (1781)**, lost at **Porto Novo (November 1781)**; died of cancer **December 7, 1782**; **Treaty of Mangalore (March 1784)**.
- **Third Anglo-Mysore War**: began over **Travancore**; **Cornwallis** took **Bangalore (March 1791)**; **Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)** — **half of Mysore**, **Baramahal, Dindigul, Malabar** to the English, **Rs 3 crore** indemnity, Tipu's **two sons** as hostages.
- **Fourth Anglo-Mysore War: April 17 – May 4, 1799**; Tipu defeated by **Stuart** and **Harris**; **Wellesley** Governor-General; Tipu's family interned at **Vellore**.
- After Tipu: Marathas **refused Soonda and Harponelly**; Nizam got **Gooty and Gurramkonda**; **Krishnaraja III** accepted subsidiary alliance; **Bentinck** took over Mysore in **1831**; **Ripon** restored it in **1881**.
- **Tipu Sultan** ('Tiger of Mysore'): **Board of Admiralty (1796)**; dockyards at **Mangalore, Wajedabad, Molidabad**; 'pioneer of rocket technology'; introduced **sericulture**; **Jacobin Club (1797)**, 'Citizen Tipu', **Tree of Liberty**; funded **Sringeri** temple repair.
- **Bajirao I (1720–40)**, greatest Peshwa, started the Maratha confederacy: **Gaekwad (Baroda), Bhonsle (Nagpur), Holkar (Indore), Sindhia (Gwalior), Peshwa (Poona)**.
- **Narayanrao** (fifth peshwa) killed by uncle **Raghunathrao**; **Barabhai** led by **Nana Phadnavis** backed infant **Sawai Madhavrao**.
- **Treaty of Surat (1775)**: Raghunathrao ceded **Salsette and Bassein**; Calcutta Council sent **Colonel Upton** → **Treaty of Purandhar (1776)**.
- **Mahadji Sindhia** trapped the English; **Treaty of Wadgaon (January 1779)**; **Goddard** took Ahmedabad and Bassein; **Popham** took Gwalior (1780); **Camac** defeated Sindhia at **Sipri (1781)**.
- **Treaty of Salbai (May 1782)**: peace for **20 years**; **Salsette** stays English; **Bassein** restored; **Mahadji Sindhia** mutual guarantor.
- **Jaswantrao Holkar** defeated Peshwa and Sindhia at **Hadapsar (October 25, 1802)**.
- **Treaty of Bassein (December 31, 1802)**: **Bajirao II** accepted at least **6,000** troops, ceded territory yielding **Rs 26 lakh**, surrendered **Surat**; said to give the English "the key to India".
- Subsidiary treaties: **Bhonsle — Devgaon (Dec 17, 1803)**; **Sindhia — Surajianjangaon (Dec 30, 1803)**; **Holkar — Rajpurghat (1806)**.
- Third Anglo-Maratha War: Peshwa beaten at **Khirki**, Bhonsle at **Sitabuldi**, Holkar at **Mahidpur**; treaties of **Poona (June 1817)**, **Gwalior (Nov 1817)**, **Mandasor (Jan 1818)**.
- **June 1818**: peshwaship abolished; **Bajirao II** became a British retainer at **Bithur near Kanpur**; **Pratap Singh** made ruler of **Satara**.
- Pindari leaders **Amir Khan** and **Karim Khan** surrendered; **Chitu Khan** fled to the jungles.
- Sindh: English factory at **Thatta (1758)**; **Talpuras** under **Mir Fath Ali Khan** took Sindh in **1783**; his brothers were the **'Char Yar'**.
- **Lord Minto's** missions: **Metcalfe — Lahore**, **Elphinstone — Kabul**, **Malcolm — Teheran**; **Nicholas Smith** concluded the Amirs' **first treaty** ('Eternal Friendship').
- **Treaty of 1832**: **Bentinck** sent **Colonel Pottinger**; Sindh became a British **protectorate in 1838**; subsidiary force at **Shikarpur and Bukkar (1839)**, **Rs 3 lakh** a year.
- **Sindh merged in 1843** under **Ellenborough**; **Charles Napier** first governor; Elphinstone's "bully" remark.
- **Banda Bahadur** defeated by Farrukhsiyar **1715**, executed **1716**; **Kapur Singh Faizullapuria** organised the **Dal Khalsa** (**Budha Dal** and **Taruna Dal**); **misl** means 'equal' (Arabic).
- **Ranjit Singh** born **November 2, 1780**; made governor of Lahore by **Zaman Shah (1799)**; **Treaty of Amritsar (April 25, 1809)** — **Sutlej** as boundary; took **Multan (1818), Kashmir (1819), Peshawar (1834)**; died **June 1839**.
- **First Anglo-Sikh War (1845–46)**: Sikh army crossed the Sutlej **December 11, 1845**; battles **Mudki, Ferozeshah, Buddelwal, Aliwal, Sobraon**; treachery of **Lal Singh and Teja Singh**.
- **Treaty of Lahore (March 8, 1846)**: **Jalandhar Doab** annexed; resident **Henry Lawrence**; Kashmir sold to **Gulab Singh** for **Rs 75 lakh** (treaty of **March 16, 1846**).
- **Treaty of Bhairowal (December 1846)**: Rani Jindan removed; council of **8 Sikh sardars** under **Henry Lawrence**.
- **Second Anglo-Sikh War (1848–49)**: revolt of **Mulraj** at **Multan**; battles **Ramnagar, Chillhanwala, Gujarat (Feb 21, 1849)**; Gujarat is on the **Jhelum**; Dalhousie made **Marquess**.
- Punjab board: **Henry and John Lawrence, Charles Mansel**; replaced in **1853** — **John Lawrence first chief commissioner**.
- **Ring-fence** — **Warren Hastings**; buffer zones, e.g. defence of **Awadh** at the Nawab's expense.
- **Subsidiary alliance** — **Wellesley (1798–1805)**; **Dupleix** probably first hired out European troops; first trap: **Awadh (1765)**; no foreign relations first insisted on with **Nawab of Carnatic (1787, Cornwallis)**.
- Subsidiary allies: **Hyderabad (1798, 1800)**, Mysore (1799), Tanjore (1799), **Awadh (Nov 1801)**, Bhonsle (1803), Sindhia (1804), Rajput states and Bharatpur (1818); **Holkars last (1818)**.
- **Doctrine of lapse** — attributed to **Dalhousie (1848–56)** but he was not its originator; **Satara (1848)**, **Jhansi and Nagpur (1854)**, Jaitpur, Sambhalpur, Baghat.
- **Awadh** annexed **1856** for **misgovernment**; Residents **Sleeman** and **Outram**; **Wajid Ali Shah** exiled to **Calcutta**; Nawab made king in **1819**.
- **Bhutan**: passes surrendered in **1865** for an annual subsidy.
- **Treaty of Sagauli (1816)**: Nepal accepted a resident, ceded **Garhwal and Kumaon**, gave up **Terai** claims, withdrew from **Sikkim**; gave hill stations **Shimla, Mussoorie, Nainital**.
- **Treaty of Yandabo (1826)**: Rs 1 crore; ceded **Arakan and Tenasserim**; gave up Assam, Cachar, Jaintia; **Manipur** independent; resident at **Ava**.
- Second Burma War (**1852**) — **Pegu**; Third (**1885**) — **Thibaw**, annexed by **Dufferin**; Burma separated from India in **1935**; independent **January 4, 1948**.
- **Younghusband** entered **Lhasa (August 1904)**; Treaty of Lhasa: indemnity **Rs 75 lakh** (cut to **25 lakh**), **Chumbi Valley**, marts at **Yatung, Gyantse, Gartok**.
- **Auckland (1836)** — **forward policy**; **Shah Shuja** (deposed **1809**, pensioner at **Ludhiana**); **First Afghan War (1839–42)** cost **Rs 1.5 crore** and about **20,000** men.
- **John Lawrence (1864–69)** — **masterly inactivity**; **Lytton (1876)** — **proud reserve**; **Treaty of Gandamak (May 1879)** with **Yakub Khan**; **Ripon** kept Afghanistan a **buffer state**.
- **Durand Line / Agreement (1893)** with Amir **Abdur Rahman**; **Curzon (1899–1905)** — policy of withdrawal and concentration; created **NWFP**.
- ⚠ May be outdated: the book says the NWFP "belongs to Pakistan" — the province was renamed **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa** in 2010.
