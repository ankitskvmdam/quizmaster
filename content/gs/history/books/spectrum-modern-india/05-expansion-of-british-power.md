## At a glance: how each power fell

| Power | Wars and battles | How it ended |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bengal | Plassey (1757), Buxar (1764) | The Company got the diwani in 1765 |
| Mysore | Four wars, 1767–1799 | Tipu died in 1799 |
| Marathas | Three wars, 1775–1819 | The peshwaship was abolished in 1818 |
| Sindh | — | Merged in 1843 |
| Punjab | Two wars, 1845–1849 | Annexed by Dalhousie |

## Background: was the conquest planned?

- **John Seeley:** He said the conquest of India was made blindly, "in a fit of absent-mindedness". On this view the conquest was accidental.
- **The other view:** The conquest was intentional.
- **The book's view:** Motives of protecting trade came first. A conscious imperial plan came later, in 1798–1818, under Wellesley and Hastings.
- **B.L. Grover:** From 1798 to 1818 British motives were consciously imperialistic.
- **Second empire:** The British 'second empire' began around 1783, with the Peace of Paris.

The beginning of the British period is dated in three ways:

- **1740:** the Anglo-French struggle.
- **1757:** the Battle of Plassey.
- **1761:** the Third Battle of Panipat.

## The Indian powers: one by one

### Bengal

#### Background

- Bengal was the richest Mughal province. Its Nawab had authority over Bihar and Orissa too.
- **1630s:** The English had factories (trading posts) at Balasore, Hooghly, Kasimbazar, Patna and Dacca.
- The Company paid Rs 3,000 a year to trade freely in Bengal.
- Nearly 60 per cent of British imports from Asia came from Bengal.
- **1700:** Murshid Quli Khan became Dewan of Bengal. He ruled till 1727.
- **1706 to 1750:** The population of Calcutta rose from 15,000 to 100,000.
- Alivardi Khan, the Deputy Governor of Bihar, seized power. He ruled till 1756.

#### Key people

- **Siraj-ud-daula:** He was the grandson of Alivardi Khan. His rivals were Shaukat Jang (Nawab of Purnea), his aunt Ghasiti Begum and his commander Mir Jafar. Mir Jafar was replaced by Mir Madan. Mohan Lal, a Kashmiri, acted almost as prime minister.
- **Mir Kasim:** He was the ablest successor of Alivardi Khan. He died a homeless wanderer in June 1777.
- **Robert Clive:** He was Governor of Bengal in 1757–60 and 1765–67. He allegedly committed suicide in 1774.

#### Wars and treaties

Siraj-ud-daula and Plassey:

1. The English gave asylum to Krishna Das, son of Raj Ballabh. Krishna Das had fled with treasure.
2. **'Black Hole Tragedy':** It is said that 146 English were imprisoned and 123 of them died. Historians doubt it.
3. Clive conspired with Mir Jafar, Rai Durlabh, Jagat Seth and Omichand.
4. **23 June 1757:** Clive won the Battle of Plassey. Siraj's army of 50,000 was defeated. Plassey is regarded as the starting point of British rule.
5. Siraj was murdered on the order of Miran, the son of Mir Jafar.
6. Mir Jafar gave the English the zamindari of 24 Parganas.
7. **November 1759:** Mir Jafar's conspiracy with the Dutch (Chinsura) ended with the Dutch defeat at Bedara.

Mir Kasim and Buxar:

1. **1760:** A treaty was signed between Vansittart and Mir Kasim.
2. Mir Kasim shifted the capital from Murshidabad to Munger in Bihar.
3. Ram Narayan, the deputy governor of Bihar, refused to give Mir Kasim the revenue accounts. The English officials of Patna backed him.
4. **Dastak:** A dastak was a trade permit that exempted goods from duties. Its misuse led Mir Kasim to abolish all duties.
5. **1763:** War broke out. The English won at Katwah, Murshidabad, Giria, Sooty and Munger.
6. **22 October 1764:** Major Hector Munro won the Battle of Buxar. He defeated Mir Kasim, Shuja-ud-daulah (Awadh) and Shah Alam II. So the Mughal Emperor too was defeated.
7. After Buxar, Mir Jafar ceded Midnapur, Burdwan and Chittagong for the army. Trade became duty-free except for a 2 per cent duty on salt.
8. Then the minor Najim-ud-daula became nawab.
9. **August 1765:** The Treaties of Allahabad were signed.

| Treaty | With | Terms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treaty of 1760 | Mir Kasim | He ceded Burdwan, Midnapur and Chittagong. He gave a half share in the chunam (lime) trade of Sylhet. He gave Rs 5 lakh for the Company's southern wars. Mir Jafar got a pension of Rs 1,500 a year. |
| Treaty of Allahabad (August 1765) | Shuja-ud-daulah | He surrendered Allahabad and Kara. He paid Rs 50 lakh. He gave Balwant Singh (Banaras) his estate. Awadh became a buffer state. |
| Treaty of Allahabad (August 1765) | Shah Alam II | He was to reside at Allahabad. He granted the diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa for Rs 26 lakh a year. Rs 53 lakh was fixed for the nizamat functions. |

#### After the wars

- **1765–72:** Clive ran the Dual Government. The diwani (the right to collect revenue) came from the emperor. The nizamat came from the subahdar.
- There were two deputy diwans. Mohammad Reza Khan was for Bengal. Raja Sitab Roy was for Bihar.
- **1772:** Warren Hastings ended the Dual Government.

### Mysore

#### Background

1. **1612:** The Wodeyar kingdom of Mysore emerged.
2. **1721:** Haidar Ali was born.
3. **1761:** Haidar Ali became the de facto (real) ruler. He set up an arms factory at Dindigul with French help.
4. **1764, 1766 and 1771:** The Marathas under Madhavrao defeated Haidar.

#### Key people

Tipu Sultan was called the 'Tiger of Mysore'.

- **1796:** He set up a Board of Admiralty. He had dockyards at Mangalore, Wajedabad and Molidabad.
- **1797:** He set up a Jacobin Club. The names 'Citizen Tipu' and the Tree of Liberty go with it.
- He is called the 'pioneer of rocket technology'.
- He introduced sericulture (silk farming).
- He funded the repair of the Sringeri temple.

#### Wars and treaties

| Anglo-Mysore War | Main events | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First (1767–69) | — | Treaty of Madras, 4 April 1769. |
| Second (1780–84) | There was a dispute over Mahe. In 1781 Haidar defeated Colonel Baillie. In November 1781 he lost at Porto Novo. On 7 December 1782 he died of cancer. | Treaty of Mangalore, March 1784. |
| Third | It began over Travancore. In March 1791 Cornwallis took Bangalore. | Treaty of Seringapatam (1792). Tipu lost half of Mysore. Baramahal, Dindigul and Malabar went to the English. He paid an indemnity of Rs 3 crore. His two sons were taken as hostages. |
| Fourth (17 April – 4 May 1799) | Wellesley was Governor-General. Stuart and Harris defeated Tipu. Tipu died at Seringapatam. | Tipu's family was interned at Vellore. |

#### After the wars

- The Marathas refused Soonda and Harponelly.
- The Nizam got Gooty and Gurramkonda.
- Krishnaraja III accepted the subsidiary alliance.
- **1831:** Bentinck took over Mysore.
- **1881:** Ripon restored it.

### Marathas

#### Background

1. **1720–40:** Bajirao I was the greatest Peshwa. He started the Maratha confederacy.
2. The confederacy weakened after Panipat and the death of Madhavrao I (1772).
3. Narayanrao, the fifth peshwa, was killed by his uncle Raghunathrao.
4. The Barabhai (a council of twelve), led by Nana Phadnavis, backed the infant Sawai Madhavrao.

The confederacy had five members.

| Family | Seat |
| --- | --- |
| Gaekwad | Baroda |
| Bhonsle | Nagpur |
| Holkar | Indore |
| Sindhia | Gwalior |
| Peshwa | Poona |

#### Wars and treaties

First Anglo-Maratha War (1775–82):

1. **1775:** The Treaty of Surat was signed with Raghunathrao. He ceded Salsette and Bassein.
2. **1776:** The Calcutta Council sent Colonel Upton. The result was the Treaty of Purandhar.
3. **January 1779:** Mahadji Sindhia trapped the English. The Treaty of Wadgaon followed.
4. Goddard took Ahmedabad and Bassein.
5. **1780:** Popham took Gwalior.
6. **1781:** Camac defeated Sindhia at Sipri.
7. **May 1782:** The Treaty of Salbai was signed. It gave peace for 20 years. Salsette stayed with the English and Bassein was restored. Mahadji Sindhia was the mutual guarantor.

Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803–05):

1. **25 October 1802:** Jaswantrao Holkar defeated the Peshwa and Sindhia at Hadapsar.
2. **31 December 1802:** Bajirao II signed the Treaty of Bassein. He accepted at least 6,000 troops. He ceded territory yielding Rs 26 lakh and surrendered Surat. The treaty is said to have given the English "the key to India".
3. **17 December 1803:** Bhonsle signed the subsidiary Treaty of Devgaon.
4. **30 December 1803:** Sindhia signed the subsidiary Treaty of Surajianjangaon.
5. **1806:** Holkar signed the subsidiary Treaty of Rajpurghat.

Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817–19):

1. The Peshwa was beaten at Khirki. Bhonsle was beaten at Sitabuldi. Holkar was beaten at Mahidpur.
2. **June 1817:** The Treaty of Poona was signed.
3. **November 1817:** The Treaty of Gwalior was signed.
4. **January 1818:** The Treaty of Mandasor was signed.
5. The Pindari leaders Amir Khan and Karim Khan surrendered. Chitu Khan fled to the jungles.

#### After the wars

- **June 1818:** The peshwaship was abolished. Bajirao II became a British retainer at Bithur near Kanpur.
- Pratap Singh was made ruler of Satara.

### Sindh

#### Background

1. **1758:** An English factory opened at Thatta.
2. **1783:** The Talpuras under Mir Fath Ali Khan took Sindh. His brothers were called the 'Char Yar'. The rulers of Sindh were the Talpura Amirs.

#### Wars and treaties

1. Lord Minto sent three missions. Metcalfe went to Lahore, Elphinstone to Kabul and Malcolm to Teheran.
2. Under Minto, Nicholas Smith concluded the first treaty with the Amirs. It was a treaty of 'Eternal Friendship'.
3. **1832:** Bentinck sent Colonel Pottinger. The Treaty of 1832 was signed.
4. **1838:** Sindh became a British protectorate.
5. **1839:** A subsidiary alliance was made. A subsidiary force was placed at Shikarpur and Bukkar. Sindh was to pay Rs 3 lakh a year.
6. **1843:** Sindh was merged under Ellenborough.

#### After the wars

- Charles Napier became the first governor of Sindh.
- Elphinstone's "bully" remark is about this merger.

### Punjab

#### Background

1. **1715:** Farrukhsiyar defeated Banda Bahadur.
2. **1716:** Banda Bahadur was executed.
3. Kapur Singh Faizullapuria organised the Dal Khalsa. It had two parts, the Budha Dal and the Taruna Dal.
4. The word misl means 'equal' in Arabic.

#### Key people

Ranjit Singh:

1. **2 November 1780:** He was born.
2. **1799:** Zaman Shah made him governor of Lahore.
3. **25 April 1809:** He signed the Treaty of Amritsar. It fixed the Sutlej as the boundary.
4. **1818:** He took Multan.
5. **1819:** He took Kashmir.
6. **1834:** He took Peshawar.
7. **June 1839:** He died.

#### Wars and treaties

First Anglo-Sikh War (1845–46):

1. **11 December 1845:** The Sikh army crossed the Sutlej.
2. Battles were fought at Mudki, Ferozeshah, Buddelwal, Aliwal and Sobraon.
3. Lal Singh and Teja Singh committed treachery.
4. **8 March 1846:** The Treaty of Lahore was signed. The Jalandhar Doab was annexed. Henry Lawrence became the resident.
5. **16 March 1846:** By a separate treaty, Kashmir was sold to Gulab Singh for Rs 75 lakh.
6. **December 1846:** The Treaty of Bhairowal was signed. Rani Jindan was removed. A council of 8 Sikh sardars was set up under Henry Lawrence.

Second Anglo-Sikh War (1848–49):

1. Mulraj revolted at Multan.
2. Battles were fought at Ramnagar and Chillhanwala.
3. **21 February 1849:** The battle of Gujarat was fought. The book places Gujarat on the Jhelum.
4. Dalhousie annexed Punjab. He was made a Marquess.

#### After the wars

- A board was set up for Punjab. Its members were Henry Lawrence, John Lawrence and Charles Mansel.
- **1853:** The board was replaced. John Lawrence became the first chief commissioner.

## Policies of expansion

### Ring-fence

- The policy of ring-fence was that of Warren Hastings.
- It meant buffer zones. One example was the defence of Awadh at the expense of its Nawab.

### Subsidiary alliance

- The subsidiary alliance was the policy of Wellesley (1798–1805).
- Dupleix was probably the first to hire out European troops.
- **1765:** Awadh was the first state caught in this trap.
- **1787:** The condition of no foreign relations was first insisted on with the Nawab of Carnatic. This was under Cornwallis.

| --- | --- |
| Hyderabad | 1798 and 1800; Wellesley's first ally |
| Mysore | 1799 |
| Tanjore | 1799 |
| Awadh | November 1801 |
| Bhonsle | 1803 |
| Sindhia | 1804 |
| Rajput states and Bharatpur | 1818 |
| Holkars | 1818; the last |

### Doctrine of lapse

- The doctrine of lapse is attributed to Dalhousie (1848–56). He was not its originator.
- **1848:** Satara.
- **1854:** Jhansi and Nagpur.
- Jaitpur, Sambhalpur and Baghat were the other states.

### Annexation of Awadh

1. **1819:** The Nawab of Awadh was made king.
2. Sleeman and Outram were Residents at Awadh.
3. **1856:** Awadh was annexed for misgovernment.
4. Wajid Ali Shah was exiled to Calcutta.

## Relations with neighbours

### Nepal

The Treaty of Sagauli was signed in 1816. It had five results:

1. Nepal accepted a resident.
2. Nepal ceded Garhwal and Kumaon.
3. Nepal gave up its claims to the Terai.
4. Nepal withdrew from Sikkim.
5. The treaty gave the English the hill stations of Shimla, Mussoorie and Nainital.

### Burma

| Event | Year | Details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treaty of Yandabo | 1826 | Rs 1 crore was to be paid. Burma ceded Arakan and Tenasserim. It gave up Assam, Cachar and Jaintia. Manipur became independent. A resident was placed at Ava. |
| Second Burma War | 1852 | Pegu |
| Third Burma War | 1885 | Thibaw; Upper Burma was annexed by Dufferin |

- **1935:** Burma was separated from India.
- **4 January 1948:** Burma became independent.

### Afghanistan

1. **Shah Shuja:** He was deposed in 1809. He was a pensioner at Ludhiana.
2. **Auckland (1836):** He followed the forward policy.
3. **1839–42:** The First Afghan War was fought. It cost Rs 1.5 crore and about 20,000 men.
4. **John Lawrence (1864–69):** He followed the policy of masterly inactivity.
5. **Lytton (1876):** He followed the policy of proud reserve.
6. **May 1879:** The Treaty of Gandamak was signed with Yakub Khan.
7. **Ripon:** He kept Afghanistan as a buffer state.

### Bhutan

- **1865:** Bhutan surrendered the passes in return for an annual subsidy.

### North-West Frontier

1. **1893:** The Durand Line was drawn by the Durand Agreement with Amir Abdur Rahman.
2. **Curzon (1899–1905):** He followed a policy of withdrawal and concentration. He created the North-West Frontier Province (NWFP).

⚠ May be outdated: the book says the NWFP "belongs to Pakistan". The province was renamed Khyber Pakhtunkhwa in 2010.

### Tibet

1. **August 1904:** Younghusband entered Lhasa.
2. **1904:** The Treaty of Lhasa was signed. The indemnity (war compensation) was Rs 75 lakh, later cut to Rs 25 lakh. The treaty also covered the Chumbi Valley. Trade marts were set up at Yatung, Gyantse and Gartok.

## Why the British succeeded

1. **Superior arms:** The British had superior arms.
2. **Discipline and regular salary:** Their soldiers had discipline and a regular salary.
3. **Merit:** Selection was based on merit.
4. **Leadership:** They had leadership and second-line leaders.
5. **Strong finances:** Their finances were strong.
6. **National pride:** They had national pride.
