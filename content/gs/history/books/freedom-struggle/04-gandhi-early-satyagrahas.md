## Gist
**Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi** was born at **Porbandar** in Gujarat on **2 October 1869**. He studied for a short time at **Samaldas College, Bhavnagar**, then went to London and became a barrister. In **1893** he went to **South Africa** to work on a law case. There he saw how Indians were insulted and denied rights, and he stayed for about twenty years to fight for them.

In South Africa Gandhi worked out his method, **satyagraha**: to resist an unjust law openly, without violence, and to accept the punishment. He founded the **Natal Indian Congress**, started the paper **Indian Opinion**, and set up the **Phoenix Settlement** and **Tolstoy Farm**. He first used satyagraha in **1906** against a law that forced Indians in the Transvaal to carry registration certificates.

Gandhi came back to India in **January 1915**. On the advice of **Gopal Krishna Gokhale**, his political guru, he spent a year travelling and watching. He set up his ashram at **Ahmedabad**, and gave his first major public speech at the opening of **Banaras Hindu University** in **1916**. Then came three local struggles: **Champaran (1917)** for the indigo peasants of Bihar, the **Ahmedabad mill strike (1918)**, in which he fasted for the first time, and **Kheda (1918)**, where peasants refused to pay land revenue after their crops failed.

In **1919** the government passed the **Rowlatt Act**, which let it jail people without trial. Gandhi called for a countrywide **hartal** on **6 April 1919**. This **Rowlatt Satyagraha** was his first all-India struggle. In Punjab the repression was fierce. On **13 April 1919**, the day of Baisakhi, **General Dyer** ordered his troops to fire on a peaceful crowd in **Jallianwala Bagh** at **Amritsar**. Hundreds were killed. Violence had also broken out in some towns, and Gandhi called off the satyagraha.

At the same time Indian Muslims were angry at the harsh treatment of the Sultan of Turkey, their **Khalifa**, after the First World War. The **Ali brothers** started the **Khilafat Movement**. Gandhi saw in it a chance to unite Hindus and Muslims, and he presided over the **All India Khilafat Conference** at Delhi in **November 1919**. The Punjab wrongs and the Khilafat wrong together led to the Non-Cooperation Movement of 1920.

## Key facts
- Gandhi was born on **2 October 1869** at **Porbandar** (Gujarat); his father was **Karamchand Gandhi**, his mother **Putlibai** and his wife **Kasturba**.
- Gandhi studied at **Samaldas College, Bhavnagar**, in **1888**, before he left for London to study law.
- He went to **South Africa** in **1893** to work on a law case for the merchant **Dada Abdullah**.
- He was thrown out of a first-class railway compartment at **Pietermaritzburg** station in **1893**.
- He founded the **Natal Indian Congress** in **1894** and started the paper **Indian Opinion** in **1903**.
- **Phoenix Settlement (1904)**, near **Durban**: inspired by **John Ruskin's** book **Unto This Last**.
- **Tolstoy Farm (1910)**, near **Johannesburg**: set up with the help of **Hermann Kallenbach**.
- Gandhi used **satyagraha** for the first time in **1906** in South Africa, against the law that made **registration certificates** compulsory for Indians in the Transvaal.
- He wrote **Hind Swaraj** in **1909**.
- Gandhi returned to India from South Africa on **9 January 1915**; **Pravasi Bharatiya Divas** is observed on 9 January.
- The government gave him the **Kaiser-i-Hind** medal in **1915**; he returned it in **1920**.
- **Gopal Krishna Gokhale** was Gandhi's **political guru**.
- He founded the **Satyagraha Ashram** at **Kochrab, Ahmedabad**, in **1915**; it moved to the bank of the **Sabarmati** in **1917**.
- Gandhi gave his **first major public speech** in India at the opening of **Banaras Hindu University** in **February 1916**.
- **Champaran Satyagraha (1917)**, Bihar: Gandhi's first satyagraha and first civil disobedience in India (see the section on Champaran).
- **Ahmedabad mill strike (1918)**: a dispute between mill owners and workers over the withdrawal of the **plague bonus**.
- At Ahmedabad Gandhi asked the workers to demand a **35 per cent** rise in wages; the tribunal awarded it.
- In the Ahmedabad strike Gandhi used the **hunger strike** as a weapon **for the first time**.
- **Anasuya Sarabhai** supported the workers at Ahmedabad; her brother **Ambalal Sarabhai** was a leading mill owner.
- **Kheda Satyagraha (1918)**, Gujarat: peasants refused to pay **land revenue** after crop failure; it was Gandhi's **first non-cooperation**.
- Gandhi's co-workers in Kheda: **Vallabhbhai Patel, Indulal Yagnik, Mohanlal Pandya and Shankarlal Banker**.
- Order of the three early struggles: **Champaran (1917)** → **Ahmedabad (March 1918)** → **Kheda (1918)**.
- The **Rowlatt Act** was passed in **March 1919**; it was based on the report of the Sedition Committee headed by Justice **Sidney Rowlatt**.
- The Rowlatt Act allowed **detention without trial for up to two years**; it was called the **Black Act**, summed up as **"no dalil, no vakil, no appeal"**.
- **Mohammad Ali Jinnah, Madan Mohan Malaviya and Mazharul Haque** (of Bihar) resigned from the Imperial Legislative Council in protest against the Rowlatt Act.
- Gandhi founded the **Satyagraha Sabha** in **February 1919**; the all-India **hartal** was held on **6 April 1919**.
- The **Rowlatt Satyagraha (1919)** was Gandhi's **first all-India movement**.
- The Punjab leaders **Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr Satyapal** were arrested on **10 April 1919**.
- **Jallianwala Bagh massacre**: **13 April 1919**, the day of **Baisakhi**, at **Amritsar**.
- **Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer** ordered the firing; **Michael O'Dwyer** was the Lieutenant-Governor of Punjab and **Lord Chelmsford** the Viceroy.
- **General Dyer** issued the **"crawling order"**: Indians had to crawl on all fours through the lane in Amritsar where an Englishwoman, **Marcella Sherwood**, had been attacked.
- **Rabindranath Tagore** gave up his **knighthood** in protest against the massacre.
- **Sir Sankaran Nair** resigned from the **Viceroy's Executive Council** in protest.
- The government appointed the **Hunter Committee** (Disorders Inquiry Committee) in **October 1919**; its chairman was **Lord William Hunter**.
- Gandhi called his launching of the Rowlatt Satyagraha a **"Himalayan blunder"** and withdrew it on **18 April 1919**.
- The **Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (Government of India Act, 1919)** brought **dyarchy** to the provinces.
- The **Khilafat Movement** was led by the **Ali brothers (Mohammad Ali and Shaukat Ali)**, with **Maulana Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan and Hasrat Mohani**.
- The **All India Khilafat Committee** was formed at **Bombay** in **1919**.
- **Gandhi presided** over the **All India Khilafat Conference** at **Delhi** in **November 1919**.
- The **Treaty of Sèvres (1920)** broke up the Turkish empire; the Khilafat issue ended when **Mustafa Kemal** abolished the Caliphate in **1924**.
- Gandhi's papers: **Indian Opinion** (South Africa), **Young India** and **Navajivan** (from 1919), **Harijan** (1933).
- Gandhi's autobiography is **The Story of My Experiments with Truth**, written in **Gujarati**.
- **Rabindranath Tagore** called Gandhi **"Mahatma"**; **Subhas Chandra Bose** called him **"Father of the Nation"**.
- Gandhi was arrested in **March 1922**, not 1921, and sentenced to **six years** in prison.
- Gandhi founded the **All India Harijan Sangh (Harijan Sevak Sangh)** in **1932**; **G.D. Birla** was its first president and **Thakkar Bapa** its secretary.

## What the exams ask
- Life facts: where Gandhi studied (Samaldas College), when he returned (1915), where he gave his first major public speech (BHU).
- "First" questions: first satyagraha in India (Champaran), first hunger strike (Ahmedabad), first non-cooperation (Kheda), first all-India movement (Rowlatt Satyagraha).
- Jallianwala Bagh: the date, the officer and the "crawling order" (General Dyer).
- Which year is correctly paired with an event; which statement is not correct (Gandhi was arrested in 1922, not 1921).
- Bodies Gandhi founded: Natal Indian Congress, Satyagraha Sabha, All India Harijan Sangh (1932).
