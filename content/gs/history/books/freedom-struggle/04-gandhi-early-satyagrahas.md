## Early life (1869–1893)

1. **2 October 1869:** Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi was born at Porbandar in Gujarat. His father was Karamchand Gandhi and his mother was Putlibai. His wife was Kasturba.
2. **1888:** Gandhi studied for a short time at Samaldas College, Bhavnagar. Then he went to London to study law, and became a barrister.

## South Africa (1893–1915)

In South Africa Gandhi saw how Indians were insulted and denied rights. He stayed for about twenty years to fight for them. There he worked out his method, satyagraha: to resist an unjust law openly, without violence, and to accept the punishment.

1. **1893:** Gandhi went to South Africa to work on a law case for the merchant Dada Abdullah. In the same year he was thrown out of a first-class railway compartment at Pietermaritzburg station.
2. **1894:** He founded the Natal Indian Congress.
3. **1903:** He started the paper Indian Opinion.
4. **1904:** He set up the Phoenix Settlement near Durban. It was inspired by John Ruskin's book Unto This Last.
5. **1906:** Gandhi used satyagraha for the first time. It was against the law that made registration certificates compulsory for Indians in the Transvaal.
6. **1909:** He wrote Hind Swaraj.
7. **1910:** He set up Tolstoy Farm near Johannesburg, with the help of Hermann Kallenbach.

## Return to India and three local struggles (1915–1918)

1. **9 January 1915:** Gandhi returned to India from South Africa. Pravasi Bharatiya Divas is observed on 9 January. On the advice of Gopal Krishna Gokhale, his political guru, he spent a year travelling and watching.
2. **1915:** The government gave him the Kaiser-i-Hind medal. He returned it in 1920.
3. **1915:** He founded the Satyagraha Ashram at Kochrab, Ahmedabad. It moved to the bank of the Sabarmati in 1917.
4. **February 1916:** Gandhi gave his first major public speech in India, at the opening of Banaras Hindu University.
5. **1917:** The Champaran Satyagraha in Bihar was Gandhi's first satyagraha and first civil disobedience in India. It is told in the section on Champaran.
6. **March 1918:** At Ahmedabad, mill owners and workers fell out over the withdrawal of the plague bonus. Gandhi asked the workers to demand a 35 per cent rise in wages, and the tribunal awarded it. Here Gandhi used the hunger strike as a weapon for the first time.
7. **1918:** In the Kheda Satyagraha in Gujarat, peasants refused to pay land revenue after their crops failed. It was Gandhi's first non-cooperation.

- **Ahmedabad:** Anasuya Sarabhai supported the workers. Her brother Ambalal Sarabhai was a leading mill owner.
- **Kheda:** Gandhi's co-workers were Vallabhbhai Patel, Indulal Yagnik, Mohanlal Pandya and Shankarlal Banker.

## The Rowlatt Satyagraha and Jallianwala Bagh (1919)

1. **February 1919:** Gandhi founded the Satyagraha Sabha.
2. **March 1919:** The Rowlatt Act was passed. It was based on the report of the Sedition Committee headed by Justice Sidney Rowlatt. It allowed detention without trial for up to two years. It was called the Black Act, summed up as "no dalil, no vakil, no appeal".
3. **6 April 1919:** Gandhi called a countrywide hartal (a general strike, with shops shut). This Rowlatt Satyagraha was his first all-India movement.
4. **10 April 1919:** The Punjab leaders Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr Satyapal were arrested. In Punjab the repression was fierce.
5. **13 April 1919:** On the day of Baisakhi, Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer ordered his troops to fire on a peaceful crowd in Jallianwala Bagh at Amritsar. Hundreds were killed. Michael O'Dwyer was the Lieutenant-Governor of Punjab, and Lord Chelmsford was the Viceroy.
6. **18 April 1919:** Violence had broken out in some towns. Gandhi withdrew the satyagraha and called his launching of it a "Himalayan blunder".
7. **October 1919:** The government appointed the Hunter Committee (Disorders Inquiry Committee). Its chairman was Lord William Hunter.
8. **1919:** The Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (Government of India Act, 1919) brought dyarchy to the provinces.

Protests against the Rowlatt Act and the massacre:

- **Resignations over the Act:** Mohammad Ali Jinnah, Madan Mohan Malaviya and Mazharul Haque (of Bihar) resigned from the Imperial Legislative Council.
- **"Crawling order":** General Dyer made Indians crawl on all fours through the lane in Amritsar where an Englishwoman, Marcella Sherwood, had been attacked.
- **Tagore:** Rabindranath Tagore gave up his knighthood in protest against the massacre.
- **Sankaran Nair:** Sir Sankaran Nair resigned from the Viceroy's Executive Council in protest.

## The Khilafat Movement (1919–1924)

After the First World War, Indian Muslims were angry at the harsh treatment of the Sultan of Turkey, their Khalifa. The Ali brothers, Mohammad Ali and Shaukat Ali, started and led the Khilafat Movement. Maulana Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan and Hasrat Mohani worked with them.

1. **1919:** The All India Khilafat Committee was formed at Bombay.
2. **November 1919:** Gandhi presided over the All India Khilafat Conference at Delhi. He saw in the movement a chance to unite Hindus and Muslims.
3. **1920:** The Treaty of Sèvres broke up the Turkish empire.
4. **1924:** Mustafa Kemal abolished the Caliphate (the office of the Khalifa). This ended the Khilafat issue.

The Punjab wrongs and the Khilafat wrong together led to the Non-Cooperation Movement of 1920.

## Gandhi: papers, titles and later facts

- **Papers:** Indian Opinion (South Africa), Young India and Navajivan (from 1919), and Harijan (1933).
- **Autobiography:** The Story of My Experiments with Truth. He wrote it in Gujarati.
- **Titles:** Rabindranath Tagore called Gandhi "Mahatma". Subhas Chandra Bose called him "Father of the Nation".
- **March 1922:** Gandhi was arrested, not in 1921. He was sentenced to six years in prison.
- **1932:** Gandhi founded the All India Harijan Sangh (Harijan Sevak Sangh). G.D. Birla was its first president, and Thakkar Bapa its secretary.

## What the exams ask

- Life facts: where Gandhi studied (Samaldas College), when he returned (1915), where he gave his first major public speech (Banaras Hindu University).
- "First" questions: the first satyagraha in India (Champaran), the first hunger strike (Ahmedabad), the first non-cooperation (Kheda), the first all-India movement (Rowlatt Satyagraha).
- The order of the three early struggles: Champaran (1917), Ahmedabad (March 1918), Kheda (1918).
- Jallianwala Bagh: the date, the officer and the "crawling order" (General Dyer).
- Which year is correctly paired with an event, and which statement is not correct (Gandhi was arrested in 1922, not 1921).
- Bodies Gandhi founded: Natal Indian Congress, Satyagraha Sabha, All India Harijan Sangh (1932).
