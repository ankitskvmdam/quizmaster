## Gist
This section cuts across all the others. Exams often ask not about an event but about a person: what title he was known by, which paper he edited, which book he wrote, which party he founded, which slogan he gave. These questions mostly come as "match the following" or "which pair is correct", so the facts have to be learnt as pairs.

Leaders were given popular titles by the people. **C.R. Das** was **Deshbandhu**, **Tilak** was **Lokmanya**, **Lala Lajpat Rai** was **Punjab Kesari**, and **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** was the **Frontier Gandhi**. Bihar had its own: **Rajendra Prasad** was **Deshratna**, **Shri Krishna Sinha** was **Bihar Kesari**, **Anugrah Narayan Sinha** was **Bihar Vibhuti**, and **Jayaprakash Narayan** was **Loknayak**.

The press was the main weapon of the national movement, and almost every leader ran a paper. **Tilak** had **Kesari** and **Mahratta**, **Gandhi** had **Indian Opinion, Young India, Navajivan and Harijan**, **Abul Kalam Azad** had **Al-Hilal**, **Mohammad Ali** had **The Comrade**, and **Ambedkar** had **Mooknayak** and **Bahishkrit Bharat**. Leaders also wrote books that shaped opinion, such as **Dadabhai Naoroji's Poverty and Un-British Rule in India**, **Lala Lajpat Rai's Unhappy India** and **Nehru's The Discovery of India**.

Besides the Congress there were many parties and groups, each tied to a founder: the **Indian National Liberal Federation** of the Moderates, **Ambedkar's Independent Labour Party**, **Mashriqi's Khaksars** and **Bose's Forward Bloc**. Titles, papers, books and founders already given in the earlier sections are not repeated here in detail.

## Key facts
- **Chittaranjan (C.R.) Das** was known as **"Deshbandhu"** (friend of the nation).
- **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** was called **"Lokmanya"**; the British journalist **Valentine Chirol** called him the **"Father of Indian Unrest"**.
- **Tilak** gave the slogan **"Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it"**.
- **Lala Lajpat Rai** was known as **"Punjab Kesari"** and **"Sher-e-Punjab"** (Lion of Punjab).
- **Dadabhai Naoroji** was called the **"Grand Old Man of India"**.
- **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** was known as the **"Frontier Gandhi"** and **"Badshah Khan"**.
- **Madan Mohan Malaviya** was called **"Mahamana"**; he founded the **Banaras Hindu University** in **1916**.
- **C.F. Andrews** was called **"Deenbandhu"** (friend of the poor).
- **Sarojini Naidu** was called the **"Nightingale of India"**; **C. Rajagopalachari** was popularly called **"Rajaji"**.
- **Tanguturi (T.) Prakasam** was known as **"Andhra Kesari"** (Lion of Andhra).
- **Matangini Hazra** of **Tamluk** (Bengal) was fondly called **"Gandhi Buri"** (Old Lady Gandhi).
- **Ambabai**, a woman freedom fighter of **Karnataka**, picketed foreign cloth and liquor shops at **Udipi**.
- **Bihar titles**: **Rajendra Prasad** was **"Deshratna"**, **Shri Krishna Sinha** was **"Bihar Kesari"** and **Anugrah Narayan Sinha** was **"Bihar Vibhuti"**.
- **Jayaprakash Narayan** was called **"Loknayak"**; he was born at **Sitab Diara** in **1902** and wrote **Why Socialism?** (1936).
- **Mazharul Haque** of Bihar was called **"Desh Bhushan"**.
- **Tilak** brought out **Kesari** (in Marathi) and **Mahratta** (in English).
- **Gandhi** edited **Indian Opinion** (South Africa), **Young India**, **Navajivan** and **Harijan**.
- **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad** brought out the Urdu journals **Al-Hilal** (**1912**) and **Al-Balagh**.
- **Maulana Mohammad Ali** brought out **The Comrade** (in English) and **Hamdard** (in Urdu).
- The paper **Zamindar** was edited by **Zafar Ali Khan** from Lahore.
- **Surendranath Banerjea** edited **The Bengalee**; his autobiography is **A Nation in Making**.
- **Dadabhai Naoroji** started **Rast Goftar** and **Voice of India**, and wrote **Poverty and Un-British Rule in India**.
- **Taraknath Das** brought out **Free Hindustan** from North America.
- **Annie Besant** brought out **New India** and **Commonweal**.
- **B.R. Ambedkar** started **Mooknayak** (**1920**) and **Bahishkrit Bharat** (**1927**), and wrote **Annihilation of Caste**.
- **Bande Mataram** (English daily, 1906) was started by **Bipin Chandra Pal** and edited by **Aurobindo Ghosh**; Aurobindo also brought out **Karmayogin**.
- **Madan Mohan Malaviya** started **The Leader** (Allahabad, **1909**); **Motilal Nehru** started **The Independent** (**1919**).
- **Jawaharlal Nehru** founded the **National Herald** (**1938**); **Ganesh Shankar Vidyarthi** edited **Pratap** from **Kanpur**.
- **Bihar papers**: **Sachchidananda Sinha** was associated with the founding of **The Bihar Times** (**1894**) and **The Searchlight** (Patna, **1918**); **Rajendra Prasad** brought out the Hindi weekly **Desh**.
- **Lala Lajpat Rai** wrote **Unhappy India** (**1928**) as a reply to **Katherine Mayo's Mother India**.
- **Tilak** wrote **Gita Rahasya** and **The Arctic Home in the Vedas**.
- **Gandhi's** autobiography is **The Story of My Experiments with Truth**.
- **Jawaharlal Nehru** wrote **The Discovery of India** (in **Ahmednagar Fort** jail), **Glimpses of World History** and **An Autobiography**.
- **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad** wrote **India Wins Freedom**.
- **Rajendra Prasad** wrote **India Divided**, **Atmakatha** and **Mahatma Gandhi and Bihar**.
- **Swami Sahajanand Saraswati** wrote his autobiography **Mera Jeevan Sangharsh** and brought out the paper **Hunkar** from Patna.
- **V.D. Savarkar** wrote **The Indian War of Independence, 1857**; **R.C. Dutt** wrote **The Economic History of India**.
- **Bankim Chandra Chatterjee's** novel **Anandamath** contains the song **"Vande Mataram"**; **Muhammad Iqbal** wrote **"Sare Jahan Se Achchha"**.
- The poem **"Sarfaroshi ki Tamanna"** was written by **Bismil Azimabadi** of **Patna**; **Ram Prasad Bismil** made it famous.
- The **Indian National Liberal Federation** was formed in **1918** by Moderates who left the Congress over the **Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms**; its leaders were **Surendranath Banerjea, Tej Bahadur Sapru and C.Y. Chintamani**.
- **Home Rule Leagues (1916)**: one founded by **Tilak** and the other by **Annie Besant**.
- The **Khaksar** movement was founded by **Allama Inayatullah Mashriqi** at **Lahore** in **1931**.
- **B.R. Ambedkar** founded the **Independent Labour Party** in **1936** and the **Scheduled Castes Federation** in **1942**.
- The **Justice Party** was founded in **1916** at Madras by **T.M. Nair and P. Theagaraya Chetty**; **E.V. Ramasamy Naicker (Periyar)** began the **Self-Respect Movement** in **1925**.
- The **Communist Party of India** was first formed at **Tashkent** in **1920** by **M.N. Roy**; the **Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh** was founded at **Nagpur** in **1925** by **K.B. Hedgewar**.

## What the exams ask
- Match the leader with the title (Deshbandhu, Punjab Kesari, Andhra Kesari, Bihar Kesari, Frontier Gandhi).
- Match the newspaper or journal with its editor (Al-Hilal, Comrade, Bengalee, Indian Opinion, Bahishkrit Bharat).
- Who wrote the book (Unhappy India, India Wins Freedom, India Divided, Poverty and Un-British Rule in India).
- Match the organisation with its founder (Khaksars, Independent Labour Party, Forward Bloc, Liberal Federation).
- "Which of the following pairs is correct?", mixing a title, a session and a founder in one question.
- To which state a lesser-known freedom fighter belonged (Ambabai of Karnataka, Matangini Hazra of Bengal).
