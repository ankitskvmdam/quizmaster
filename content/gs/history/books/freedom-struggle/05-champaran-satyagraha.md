## Gist
Champaran is in north Bihar, at the foot of the Nepal hills. In the 19th century European planters took land on lease there, much of it from the **Bettiah Raj**, and made the peasants grow **indigo**. Under the **tinkathia system** a peasant had to grow indigo on **3 kathas out of every 20** of his holding and sell it to the planter at the planter's price.

When German synthetic dye killed the indigo trade, the planters found a new way to make money. They freed peasants from growing indigo only if they paid a higher rent (**sharahbeshi**) or a lump sum (**tawan**). On top of this came many illegal cesses. Peasants had resisted before, but without success.

**Raj Kumar Shukla**, a cultivator of Champaran, met Gandhi at the **Lucknow session of the Congress in 1916** and would not leave him until he agreed to come. Gandhi reached Bihar in **April 1917**. At **Motihari** the district magistrate ordered him to leave Champaran. Gandhi refused, told the court he was ready to accept the penalty, and the government had to withdraw the case. This was his **first act of civil disobedience in India**.

Gandhi and a team of Bihar lawyers, among them **Rajendra Prasad, Brajkishore Prasad, Mazharul Haque and Anugrah Narayan Sinha**, then recorded the statements of thousands of peasants. The government appointed the **Champaran Agrarian Committee** in **June 1917** with Gandhi as a member. On its report the **Champaran Agrarian Act, 1918** abolished the tinkathia system, and the planters agreed to refund **25 per cent** of the money they had taken illegally.

Champaran made Gandhi a national leader and gave him the co-workers who later led the Congress in Bihar. He also began constructive work there by opening village schools and teaching sanitation. BPSC asks about Champaran in almost every paper.

## Key facts
- The **Champaran Satyagraha** took place in **1917** in the Champaran district of north **Bihar**.
- It was **Gandhi's first satyagraha in India** and his **first use of civil disobedience** in India.
- The movement was about **indigo** cultivation forced on peasants by **European planters**.
- **Tinkathia system**: a peasant had to grow indigo on **3 kathas out of 20** of his land, that is **3/20** of the holding.
- Much of the planters' land was held on lease from the **Bettiah Raj**.
- **German synthetic dye** made indigo unprofitable, so the planters demanded more money for releasing peasants from indigo.
- **Sharahbeshi** was the enhanced rent, and **tawan** was the lump sum, that planters took for freeing peasants from growing indigo.
- The **Indigo Revolt of Bengal (1859–60)** was an earlier peasant struggle against planters; **Dinabandhu Mitra's** play **Neel Darpan** described it.
- **Raj Kumar Shukla**, an indigo cultivator of Champaran, drew Gandhi's attention to the peasants' plight and invited him to Champaran.
- Shukla met Gandhi at the **Lucknow session of the Congress (December 1916)**; **Brajkishore Prasad** raised the Champaran issue at that session.
- Gandhi reached **Patna** in **April 1917** with Raj Kumar Shukla, and went on to **Muzaffarpur**, where **J.B. Kripalani**, then a college teacher there, received him.
- Gandhi reached **Motihari**, the headquarters of Champaran district, on **15 April 1917**.
- The district magistrate **W.B. Heycock** ordered Gandhi to leave Champaran under **Section 144** of the Criminal Procedure Code; Gandhi refused to obey.
- Gandhi appeared in the court at **Motihari** on **18 April 1917**, pleaded guilty and offered to accept the penalty.
- The case against Gandhi was **withdrawn** on the orders of the Lieutenant-Governor of Bihar and Orissa, **Sir Edward Gait**.
- Gandhi's main co-workers: **Rajendra Prasad, Brajkishore Prasad, Mazharul Haque, Anugrah Narayan Sinha, J.B. Kripalani, Mahadev Desai and Narhari Parikh**.
- Other Bihar associates: **Ramnavami Prasad, Dharanidhar Prasad and Shambhu Sharan**.
- **Ram Manohar Lohia** and **Jayaprakash Narayan** were **not** part of the Champaran Satyagraha.
- **Batak Mian**, a cook, saved Gandhi's life in **1917** by refusing a planter's order to poison his food.
- The government set up the **Champaran Agrarian Committee** in **June 1917**, by agreement between the **government and Gandhi**.
- **Gandhi was a member** of the Champaran Agrarian Committee; its chairman was **F.G. Sly**.
- The committee gave its report in **October 1917** and recommended the abolition of the tinkathia system.
- The **Champaran Agrarian Act, 1918** abolished the **tinkathia system**.
- The planters agreed to refund **25 per cent** of the money they had taken illegally from the peasants.
- Gandhi opened his first village school in Champaran at **Barharwa Lakhansen** in **November 1917**; schools followed at **Bhitiharwa and Madhuban**.
- **Kasturba Gandhi** worked in Champaran villages, teaching cleanliness and helping to run the schools.
- The **Bhitiharwa Ashram** in West Champaran is a memorial of Gandhi's work.
- **Rajendra Prasad** wrote the book **Satyagraha in Champaran**.
- Gandhi's first three struggles in India: **Champaran (1917)**, first civil disobedience; **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918)**, first hunger strike; **Kheda (1918)**, first non-cooperation.
- Gandhi had returned to India from South Africa in **January 1915**; Champaran was his first major public action in India.
- Champaran is now two districts: **East Champaran (headquarters Motihari)** and **West Champaran (headquarters Bettiah)**.

## What the exams ask
- Who invited Gandhi or drew his attention to Champaran (Raj Kumar Shukla).
- How much land was under indigo in the tinkathia system (3/20).
- Who was, or was not, an associate of Gandhi in Champaran.
- Who saved Gandhi's life (Batak Mian).
- What the government did in response (Champaran Agrarian Committee, with Gandhi as a member).
- Which was Gandhi's first satyagraha or first civil disobedience in India, and in which year.
