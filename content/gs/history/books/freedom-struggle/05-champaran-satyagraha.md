## Why the peasants of Champaran suffered

The Champaran Satyagraha took place in 1917 in the Champaran district of north Bihar, at the foot of the Nepal hills. It was about indigo cultivation that European planters forced on the peasants. Champaran is now two districts: East Champaran (headquarters Motihari) and West Champaran (headquarters Bettiah).

1. In the 19th century European planters took land on lease in Champaran. Much of it was held on lease from the Bettiah Raj.
2. The planters made the peasants grow indigo under the tinkathia system.
3. German synthetic dye made indigo unprofitable and killed the indigo trade.
4. So the planters found a new way to make money. They freed peasants from growing indigo only if they paid sharahbeshi or tawan.
5. On top of this came many illegal cesses (extra levies).
6. The peasants had resisted before, but without success.

| Term | What it meant |
| --- | --- |
| Tinkathia system | A peasant had to grow indigo on 3 kathas (a small unit of land) out of every 20 of his holding, that is 3/20 of it. He had to sell it to the planter at the planter's price. |
| Sharahbeshi | The enhanced rent a planter took for freeing a peasant from growing indigo. |
| Tawan | The lump sum a planter took for the same release. |

- **Indigo Revolt of Bengal (1859–60):** This was an earlier peasant struggle against the planters. Dinabandhu Mitra's play Neel Darpan described it.

## Gandhi comes to Champaran (1916–1917)

1. **December 1916:** Raj Kumar Shukla, an indigo cultivator of Champaran, met Gandhi at the Lucknow session of the Congress. He drew Gandhi's attention to the peasants' plight and invited him to Champaran. He would not leave Gandhi until he agreed to come. Brajkishore Prasad raised the Champaran issue at the same session.
2. **April 1917:** Gandhi reached Patna with Raj Kumar Shukla. He went on to Muzaffarpur, where J.B. Kripalani, then a college teacher there, received him.
3. **15 April 1917:** Gandhi reached Motihari, the headquarters of Champaran district. The district magistrate, W.B. Heycock, ordered him to leave Champaran under Section 144 of the Criminal Procedure Code. Gandhi refused to obey.
4. **18 April 1917:** Gandhi appeared in the court at Motihari. He pleaded guilty and offered to accept the penalty. The case against him was then withdrawn on the orders of the Lieutenant-Governor of Bihar and Orissa, Sir Edward Gait.
5. **1917:** Batak Mian, a cook, saved Gandhi's life. He refused a planter's order to poison Gandhi's food.

## Inquiry and settlement (1917–1918)

Gandhi and a team of Bihar lawyers recorded the statements of thousands of peasants.

1. **June 1917:** The government set up the Champaran Agrarian Committee, by agreement between the government and Gandhi. Gandhi was a member of the committee. Its chairman was F.G. Sly.
2. **October 1917:** The committee gave its report. It recommended the abolition of the tinkathia system.
3. **1918:** The Champaran Agrarian Act, 1918 abolished the tinkathia system. The planters agreed to refund 25 per cent of the money they had taken illegally from the peasants.

## Constructive work

Gandhi began constructive work in Champaran by opening village schools and teaching sanitation.

- **November 1917:** Gandhi opened his first village school in Champaran at Barharwa Lakhansen. Schools at Bhitiharwa and Madhuban followed.
- **Kasturba Gandhi:** She worked in Champaran villages. She taught cleanliness and helped to run the schools.
- **Bhitiharwa Ashram:** This ashram in West Champaran is a memorial of Gandhi's work.

## Gandhi's co-workers

- **Main co-workers:** Rajendra Prasad, Brajkishore Prasad, Mazharul Haque, Anugrah Narayan Sinha, J.B. Kripalani, Mahadev Desai and Narhari Parikh. The first four were among the Bihar lawyers who recorded the peasants' statements.
- **Other Bihar associates:** Ramnavami Prasad, Dharanidhar Prasad and Shambhu Sharan.
- **Not part of it:** Ram Manohar Lohia and Jayaprakash Narayan were not part of the Champaran Satyagraha.
- **The book:** Rajendra Prasad wrote Satyagraha in Champaran.

## Why Champaran mattered

1. **First satyagraha:** It was Gandhi's first satyagraha in India and his first use of civil disobedience in India.
2. **First major public action:** Gandhi had returned to India from South Africa in January 1915. Champaran was his first major public action in India.
3. **A national leader:** Champaran made Gandhi a national leader.
4. **Leaders for Bihar:** It gave him the co-workers who later led the Congress in Bihar.
5. **First of three struggles:** Gandhi's first three struggles in India were Champaran (1917), his first civil disobedience; the Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918), his first hunger strike; and Kheda (1918), his first non-cooperation.

## What the exams ask

BPSC asks about Champaran in almost every paper.

- Who invited Gandhi or drew his attention to Champaran (Raj Kumar Shukla).
- How much land was under indigo in the tinkathia system (3/20).
- Who was, or was not, an associate of Gandhi in Champaran.
- Who saved Gandhi's life (Batak Mian).
- What the government did in response (the Champaran Agrarian Committee, with Gandhi as a member).
- Which was Gandhi's first satyagraha or first civil disobedience in India, and in which year.
