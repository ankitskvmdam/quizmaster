## Gist
In 1905 the province of Bengal was very large. It included today's West Bengal, Bangladesh, **Bihar** and **Orissa**, and had about **78 million** people. The Viceroy, **Lord Curzon**, said it was too big to govern and decided to cut it in two. The new province of **Eastern Bengal and Assam**, with its capital at **Dacca**, had a Muslim majority. The rest, with Bihar and Orissa, stayed as **Bengal** with its capital at **Calcutta**; here Bengali speakers were outnumbered by Hindi and Oriya speakers.

Indians saw the real aim: Bengal was the centre of nationalism, and the partition would weaken it and set Hindus against Muslims. **Surendranath Banerjea** said the announcement **"fell like a bombshell"**. On **7 August 1905** a great meeting at the **Calcutta Town Hall** passed the **boycott resolution**. This began the **Swadeshi Movement**: people gave up British cloth and goods and used things made in India. The partition came into force on **16 October 1905**, which Bengal kept as a day of mourning. People fasted, sang **Bande Mataram** and tied **rakhis** on one another's wrists.

Swadeshi and boycott were used as methods of struggle for the first time. The movement set up national schools, swadeshi mills, banks and shipping companies, and volunteer groups called **samitis**. **Lal, Bal and Pal** and **Aurobindo Ghosh** carried it outside Bengal and wanted to turn it into a struggle for **Swaraj**. The Moderates wanted to keep it within Bengal and within limits. This quarrel split the Congress at **Surat in 1907**.

The government answered with repression and with a policy of winning over others. The **Muslim League**, founded at **Dacca in 1906**, supported the partition. Leaders were jailed or deported, and by 1908 the open movement had died down. Some young men then turned to the revolver and the bomb.

In **1911**, at the **Delhi Durbar**, **King George V** announced that the partition was annulled and that the capital would move from Calcutta to **Delhi**. Bengal was united again, but **Bihar and Orissa** were made a separate province in **1912**, with **Patna** as its capital. This is how modern Bihar was born.

## Key facts
- **Bengal was partitioned in 1905** by the Viceroy **Lord Curzon** (Viceroy 1899–1905).
- Before 1905 the province of Bengal included **Bengal, Bihar and Orissa**, with a population of about **78 million**.
- The partition plan was first made public in **December 1903**; the government announced its decision in **July 1905**.
- The partition came into force on **16 October 1905**.
- The two provinces were **Eastern Bengal and Assam** (capital **Dacca**) and **Bengal**, with Bihar and Orissa (capital **Calcutta**).
- The official reason was **administrative convenience**; the real aim was to weaken Bengal, the centre of Indian nationalism, and to divide Hindus and Muslims.
- **H.H. Risley**, the Home Secretary, wrote: **"Bengal united is a power; Bengal divided will pull in several different ways."**
- **Surendranath Banerjea** said that the announcement of the partition **"fell like a bombshell"**.
- The **Swadeshi Movement** was started as a reaction to the Partition of Bengal.
- The **boycott resolution** was passed at the **Calcutta Town Hall** on **7 August 1905**; this was the formal start of the Swadeshi Movement.
- **Swadeshi and boycott** were adopted as methods of struggle **for the first time** during the movement against the Partition of Bengal.
- **16 October 1905** was observed as a **day of mourning**: people fasted, bathed in the Ganga, and sang **Bande Mataram**.
- **Rabindranath Tagore** suggested the tying of **rakhis** on that day as a sign of the unity of the two Bengals.
- Tagore wrote **"Amar Sonar Bangla"** during the movement; it is now the **national anthem of Bangladesh**.
- **Bande Mataram**, from **Bankim Chandra Chatterjee's** novel **Anandamath**, became the song of the movement.
- **Ananda Mohan Bose** laid the foundation of the **Federation Hall** at Calcutta on **16 October 1905**.
- Early leaders of the anti-partition agitation: **Surendranath Banerjea, Krishna Kumar Mitra and Prithwishchandra Ray**.
- Extremist leaders: **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** (Maharashtra), **Lala Lajpat Rai** and **Ajit Singh** (Punjab), **Bipin Chandra Pal** and **Aurobindo Ghosh** (Bengal), **V.O. Chidambaram Pillai** (Madras).
- The **Banaras session of the Congress (1905)** under **Gokhale** supported swadeshi and boycott for Bengal; the **Calcutta session (1906)** under **Dadabhai Naoroji** declared **Swaraj** the goal.
- **Swadesh Bandhab Samiti**: the best-known volunteer body, set up by **Ashwini Kumar Dutt** at **Barisal**.
- The police broke up the **Bengal Provincial Conference at Barisal** in **April 1906** with a lathi charge.
- The **Carlyle Circular (1905)** threatened students who joined the movement; the **Anti-Circular Society** was formed in reply.
- The **National Council of Education** was set up on **15 August 1906**; **Aurobindo Ghosh** was the first principal of the **Bengal National College**.
- **V.O. Chidambaram Pillai** started the **Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company** at **Tuticorin** in **1906**.
- **Acharya P.C. Ray's Bengal Chemicals** was a leading swadeshi enterprise.
- **Abanindranath Tagore** painted **Bharat Mata** in **1905** and led the Bengal school of art.
- Tagore's call for **atma shakti** (self-reliance) led to constructive work in villages.
- The **Simla Deputation** of Muslim leaders, led by the **Aga Khan**, met **Lord Minto** in **October 1906** and asked for separate electorates.
- The **All India Muslim League** was founded at **Dacca** in **December 1906**, with **Nawab Salimullah of Dacca** as host; it **supported the Partition of Bengal**.
- **Lord Minto** succeeded Curzon as Viceroy in **1905**; **John Morley** was the Secretary of State.
- The Congress split into Moderates and Extremists at **Surat in 1907**.
- **Lala Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh** were deported to **Mandalay** in **1907**; **Tilak** was sentenced to **six years** in **1908** and sent to **Mandalay**.
- The **Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act, 1909)** gave Muslims **separate electorates**.
- The Swadeshi Movement drew in **students, women and the urban middle class**, but it could not win over most peasants and most Muslims.
- In **Hind Swaraj**, Gandhi wrote that the **real awakening of India** took place after the Partition of Bengal.
- The partition was **annulled in 1911**, at the **Delhi Durbar** of **King George V**; **Lord Hardinge** was the Viceroy.
- At the same Durbar it was announced that the capital would shift from **Calcutta to Delhi**; the shift took place in **1912**.
- **Bihar and Orissa** were separated from Bengal and made a new province in **1912**, with **Patna** as the capital; Assam again became a separate province.
- **Sachchidananda Sinha** and **Mahesh Narayan** had led the demand for a separate Bihar; they started the paper **Bihar Times** in **1894**.
- **Orissa** was separated from Bihar in **1936**.
- Order of events: **Partition of Bengal (1905)** → **Muslim League (1906)** → **Surat Split (1907)** → **Morley-Minto Reforms (1909)** → **annulment of the partition (1911)**.

## What the exams ask
- Which movement began as a reaction to the Partition of Bengal (Swadeshi).
- When swadeshi and boycott were first used as methods of struggle.
- The year of the partition and the Viceroy who made it (1905, Lord Curzon); the year it was annulled (1911).
- Who supported the partition (the Muslim League).
- Which event came first: the Partition of Bengal is usually the earliest in the list.
- Who said or wrote what: "fell like a bombshell" (Surendranath Banerjea), "Amar Sonar Bangla" (Tagore).
