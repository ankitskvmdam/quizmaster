## Why Bengal was partitioned

1. Before 1905 the province of Bengal was very large. It included Bengal, Bihar and Orissa: today's West Bengal, Bangladesh, Bihar and Orissa. It had about 78 million people.
2. The Viceroy, Lord Curzon (Viceroy 1899–1905), said it was too big to govern. He decided to cut it in two.
3. **Official reason:** The government gave administrative convenience as the reason.
4. **Real aim:** Bengal was the centre of Indian nationalism. The real aim was to weaken Bengal and to divide Hindus and Muslims. Indians saw this aim.
5. **Risley's note:** H.H. Risley, the Home Secretary, wrote: "Bengal united is a power; Bengal divided will pull in several different ways."

## The partition (1903–1905)

1. **December 1903:** The partition plan was first made public.
2. **July 1905:** The government announced its decision. Surendranath Banerjea said that the announcement "fell like a bombshell".
3. **16 October 1905:** Lord Curzon's partition came into force.

## The Swadeshi Movement (1905–1908)

The Swadeshi Movement was started as a reaction to the Partition of Bengal. People gave up British cloth and goods and used things made in India. Swadeshi and boycott were adopted as methods of struggle for the first time in this movement.

### Course of the movement

1. **7 August 1905:** A great meeting at the Calcutta Town Hall passed the boycott resolution. This was the formal start of the Swadeshi Movement.
2. **16 October 1905:** The day the partition came into force was observed as a day of mourning. People fasted, bathed in the Ganga and sang Bande Mataram. Rabindranath Tagore suggested that people tie rakhis on one another's wrists, as a sign of the unity of the two Bengals. Ananda Mohan Bose laid the foundation of the Federation Hall at Calcutta on the same day.
3. **1905:** The Banaras session of the Congress, under Gokhale, supported swadeshi and boycott for Bengal.
4. **1905:** The Carlyle Circular threatened students who joined the movement. The Anti-Circular Society was formed in reply.
5. **April 1906:** The police broke up the Bengal Provincial Conference at Barisal with a lathi charge.
6. **1906:** The Calcutta session, under Dadabhai Naoroji, declared Swaraj the goal.
7. **1907:** The Congress split into Moderates and Extremists at Surat. Lal, Bal and Pal and Aurobindo Ghosh had carried the movement outside Bengal and wanted to turn it into a struggle for Swaraj. The Moderates wanted to keep it within Bengal and within limits, and this quarrel caused the split.

### Leaders

- **Early leaders of the anti-partition agitation:** Surendranath Banerjea, Krishna Kumar Mitra and Prithwishchandra Ray.
- **Extremist leaders:** Bal Gangadhar Tilak (Maharashtra), Lala Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh (Punjab), Bipin Chandra Pal and Aurobindo Ghosh (Bengal), and V.O. Chidambaram Pillai (Madras).

### Constructive work

The movement set up national schools, swadeshi mills, banks and shipping companies. It also formed volunteer groups called samitis.

- **Swadesh Bandhab Samiti:** It was the best-known volunteer body. Ashwini Kumar Dutt set it up at Barisal.
- **15 August 1906:** The National Council of Education was set up. Aurobindo Ghosh was the first principal of the Bengal National College.
- **1906:** V.O. Chidambaram Pillai started the Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company at Tuticorin.
- **Bengal Chemicals:** Acharya P.C. Ray's Bengal Chemicals was a leading swadeshi enterprise.
- **Atma shakti:** Tagore called for atma shakti (self-reliance). His call led to constructive work in villages.

### Songs and art

- **Bande Mataram:** This song, from Bankim Chandra Chatterjee's novel Anandamath, became the song of the movement.
- **Amar Sonar Bangla:** Tagore wrote it during the movement. It is now the national anthem of Bangladesh.
- **1905:** Abanindranath Tagore painted Bharat Mata. He led the Bengal school of art.

### Who joined

- The movement drew in students, women and the urban middle class.
- It could not win over most peasants and most Muslims.
- In Hind Swaraj, Gandhi wrote that the real awakening of India took place after the Partition of Bengal.

## The government's response (1905–1909)

The government answered with repression and with a policy of winning over others. Leaders were jailed or deported.

1. **1905:** Lord Minto succeeded Curzon as Viceroy. John Morley was the Secretary of State.
2. **October 1906:** The Simla Deputation of Muslim leaders, led by the Aga Khan, met Lord Minto. It asked for separate electorates (Muslims voting for Muslim candidates only).
3. **December 1906:** The All India Muslim League was founded at Dacca, with Nawab Salimullah of Dacca as host. It supported the Partition of Bengal.
4. **1907:** Lala Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh were deported to Mandalay.
5. **1908:** Tilak was sentenced to six years and sent to Mandalay. By 1908 the open movement had died down. Some young men then turned to the revolver and the bomb.
6. **1909:** The Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act, 1909) gave Muslims separate electorates.

## Annulment and a separate Bihar (1911–1936)

1. **1894:** Sachchidananda Sinha and Mahesh Narayan started the paper Bihar Times. They led the demand for a separate Bihar.
2. **1911:** King George V announced at the Delhi Durbar that the partition was annulled. Lord Hardinge was the Viceroy. At the same Durbar it was announced that the capital would shift from Calcutta to Delhi.
3. **1912:** The capital shifted to Delhi. Bengal was united again. Bihar and Orissa were separated from Bengal and made a new province, with Patna as the capital. Assam again became a separate province. This is how modern Bihar was born.
4. **1936:** Orissa was separated from Bihar.

## What the exams ask

- Which movement began as a reaction to the Partition of Bengal (Swadeshi).
- When swadeshi and boycott were first used as methods of struggle.
- The year of the partition and the Viceroy who made it (1905, Lord Curzon), and the year it was annulled (1911).
- Who supported the partition (the Muslim League).
- Putting events in order: Partition of Bengal (1905), Muslim League (1906), Surat Split (1907), Morley-Minto Reforms (1909), annulment of the partition (1911). The Partition of Bengal is usually the earliest event in the list.
- Who said or wrote what: "fell like a bombshell" (Surendranath Banerjea), "Amar Sonar Bangla" (Tagore).
