## Background: the war and the Congress (1937–40)

1. **1937:** Congress ministries took office in the provinces. Shri Krishna Sinha headed the Congress ministry of Bihar.
2. **September 1939:** The Second World War began. Viceroy Lord Linlithgow declared India a party to the war without consulting any Indian leader.
3. **October–November 1939:** The Congress ministries resigned in protest. The Congress said it would help Britain only if India was promised freedom and given a national government at once.
4. **22 December 1939:** The Muslim League observed the "Day of Deliverance".
5. **8 August 1940:** Lord Linlithgow made the August Offer. It promised dominion status only at some future date. It also promised an expanded Viceroy's Executive Council and a constitution-making body after the war. The Congress rejected it.

## The Individual Satyagraha (1940)

Gandhi did not want to disturb Britain's war effort with a mass movement. But he wanted to assert the right of free speech against the war. So the Individual Satyagraha was a limited, symbolic protest.

It began on 17 October 1940. Chosen persons, one by one, made an anti-war speech and courted arrest.

- **First:** Vinoba Bhave was the first individual satyagrahi. He began it at Pavnar, near Wardha, in Maharashtra.
- **Second and third:** Jawaharlal Nehru was the second, and Brahma Datt the third.
- **In Bihar:** The first individual satyagrahi was Shri Krishna Sinha.
- **"Delhi Chalo":** The satyagrahis were to march towards Delhi. So the movement was also called the "Delhi Chalo" Movement.

## The Cripps Mission (1942)

By early 1942 Japan had overrun South-East Asia and stood at India's door.

1. **March 1942:** Britain sent Sir Stafford Cripps, a member of the British War Cabinet, with a new offer. Winston Churchill was the Prime Minister. Cripps offered three things:
   - dominion status after the war;
   - a constitution-making body;
   - the right of any province to stay out of the Indian Union.
2. **April 1942:** The mission failed, and Cripps went back. Gandhi is said to have called the offer a "post-dated cheque".

Gandhi now felt that the British must leave at once.

## The Quit India Resolution (1942)

1. **14 July 1942:** The Congress Working Committee passed the Quit India resolution at Wardha.
2. **8 August 1942:** The All India Congress Committee passed the Quit India Resolution at Gowalia Tank Maidan, Bombay. The ground is now called August Kranti Maidan. Jawaharlal Nehru moved the resolution and Vallabhbhai Patel seconded it. Maulana Abul Kalam Azad was the Congress president.

In his speech of 8 August 1942 Gandhi gave the call "Do or Die" (Karo ya Maro). He said: "We shall either free India or die in the attempt."

The slogan "Quit India" is credited to Yusuf Meherally. The movement is also called the August Revolution (August Kranti). The Viceroy was Lord Linlithgow.

## The people's revolt (1942–44)

1. **9 August 1942:** Before dawn Gandhi and all the top leaders were arrested. Gandhi was held in the Aga Khan Palace, Poona. The members of the Congress Working Committee were held in Ahmednagar Fort.
2. **9 August 1942:** After the arrests, Aruna Asaf Ali hoisted the flag at Gowalia Tank Maidan.

Left without leaders, the people rose on their own. They attacked railway stations, post offices and police stations. Students and workers went on strike.

- **Underground leaders:** Jayaprakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia, Aruna Asaf Ali, Achyut Patwardhan and Sucheta Kripalani ran the movement from underground.
- **Congress Radio:** Usha Mehta ran this secret radio from Bombay.

### Parallel governments

In a few places the people set up parallel governments of their own.

| Place | Name | When | Led by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ballia (eastern U.P.) | — | August 1942, for about a week | Chittu Pandey |
| Tamluk (Midnapore, Bengal) | Jatiya Sarkar | December 1942 to September 1944 | — |
| Satara (Maharashtra) | Prati Sarkar | Lasted the longest | Nana Patil, Y.B. Chavan |
| Talcher (Orissa) | — | — | — |

There was no parallel government at Kheda.

### Martyrs

- **Matangini Hazra (1942):** Called "Gandhi Buri", she was shot dead while leading a procession at Tamluk.
- **Kushal Konwar (1943):** Kushal Konwar of Assam was hanged. He is known as the only martyr of the Quit India Movement to be hanged.
- **Kanaklata Barua:** This young girl of Assam was shot dead while trying to hoist the flag at a police station.

### Who stayed away

The Muslim League, the Hindu Mahasabha and the Communist Party of India did not support the Quit India Movement.

The government crushed the movement with great force. But the movement showed that British rule could not last.

## Quit India in Bihar

Bihar and eastern U.P. were the storm centres of the movement.

1. **9 August 1942:** Rajendra Prasad was arrested at Patna. He was kept in Bankipur Jail.
2. **11 August 1942:** In the Patna Secretariat firing, 7 students were shot dead while trying to hoist the flag on the Secretariat building. The district magistrate of Patna, W.G. Archer, ordered the firing. The Shaheed Smarak in Patna is their memorial.
3. **9 November 1942:** On the night of Diwali, Jayaprakash Narayan escaped from Hazaribagh Central Jail with 5 companions. Among them were Yogendra Shukla, Ramanandan Mishra and Suraj Narayan Singh.

- **Azad Dasta:** Jayaprakash Narayan organised this guerrilla band in the Terai of Nepal. Ram Manohar Lohia worked with him. When the two were caught in Nepal and held at Hanuman Nagar, the Azad Dasta freed them.
- **Siyaram Dal:** In Bhagalpur, the Siyaram Dal of Siyaram Singh carried on underground resistance.

## Leaders in jail, and release (1942–44)

1. **August 1942:** Mahadev Desai, Gandhi's secretary, died in the Aga Khan Palace.
2. **February 1943:** Gandhi went on a 21-day fast in the Aga Khan Palace.
3. **1943:** The Bengal Famine took place.
4. **October 1943:** Lord Wavell became Viceroy.
5. **February 1944:** Kasturba Gandhi died in the Aga Khan Palace.
6. **6 May 1944:** Gandhi was released on grounds of health.
7. **1944:** C. Rajagopalachari put forward the C.R. Formula, a plan for a settlement between the Congress and the Muslim League.
8. **September 1944:** The Gandhi-Jinnah talks on the C.R. Formula failed.
