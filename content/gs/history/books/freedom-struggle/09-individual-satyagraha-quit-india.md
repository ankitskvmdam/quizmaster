## Gist
When the Second World War began in **September 1939**, Viceroy **Lord Linlithgow** declared India at war without asking any Indian leader. The Congress ministries, which had been running the provinces since **1937**, resigned in protest. The Congress said it would help Britain only if India was promised freedom and given a national government at once. The Viceroy's **August Offer (1940)** promised only dominion status at some future date, and the Congress rejected it.

Gandhi did not want to disturb Britain's war effort with a mass movement, but he wanted to assert the right to speak against the war. So he began the **Individual Satyagraha** in **October 1940**: chosen persons, one by one, made an anti-war speech and courted arrest. **Vinoba Bhave** was the first, at **Pavnar** in Maharashtra, and **Jawaharlal Nehru** the second.

By early **1942** Japan had overrun South-East Asia and stood at India's door. Britain sent **Sir Stafford Cripps** in **March 1942** with a new offer, but it again promised dominion status only after the war and let provinces stay out of the Indian Union. The **Cripps Mission** failed. Gandhi now felt that the British must leave at once.

On **8 August 1942**, at **Gowalia Tank Maidan** in **Bombay**, the All India Congress Committee passed the **Quit India Resolution**, and Gandhi gave the call **"Do or Die"**. Before dawn the next day Gandhi and all the top leaders were arrested. Left without leaders, the people rose on their own. They attacked railway stations, post offices and police stations, students and workers went on strike, and in a few places the people set up **parallel governments**. Leaders such as **Jayaprakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia and Aruna Asaf Ali** ran the movement from underground.

Bihar and eastern U.P. were the storm centres. In **Patna**, **7** students were shot dead on **11 August 1942** while trying to hoist the flag on the Secretariat. **Jayaprakash Narayan** escaped from **Hazaribagh Jail** in **November 1942** and organised the **Azad Dasta**. The government crushed the movement with great force, but it showed that British rule could not last. Gandhi was released in **1944**.

## Key facts
- Congress ministries took office in the provinces in **1937**; **Shri Krishna Sinha** headed the Congress ministry of **Bihar**.
- The Second World War began in **September 1939**; Viceroy **Lord Linlithgow** declared India a party to the war without consulting Indian leaders.
- The **Congress ministries resigned** in **October–November 1939**; the Muslim League observed **22 December 1939** as the **"Day of Deliverance"**.
- **August Offer (8 August 1940)**: made by **Lord Linlithgow**; it promised **dominion status**, an expanded Viceroy's Executive Council and a constitution-making body after the war. The Congress rejected it.
- The **Individual Satyagraha** began on **17 October 1940**; it was a limited, symbolic protest to assert the **right of free speech against the war**.
- **Vinoba Bhave** was the **first** individual satyagrahi; he began it at **Pavnar**, near Wardha, in **Maharashtra**.
- **Jawaharlal Nehru** was the **second** individual satyagrahi and **Brahma Datt** the third.
- The satyagrahis were to march towards Delhi, so the movement was also called the **"Delhi Chalo" Movement**.
- In **Bihar**, the first individual satyagrahi was **Shri Krishna Sinha**.
- **Cripps Mission (March 1942)**: led by **Sir Stafford Cripps**, a member of the British War Cabinet; **Winston Churchill** was the Prime Minister.
- Cripps offered **dominion status after the war**, a **constitution-making body** and the right of any **province to stay out** of the Indian Union.
- Gandhi is said to have called the Cripps offer a **"post-dated cheque"**; the mission failed and Cripps went back in **April 1942**.
- The Congress Working Committee passed the Quit India resolution at **Wardha** on **14 July 1942**.
- The **Quit India Resolution** was passed by the All India Congress Committee at **Gowalia Tank Maidan, Bombay**, on **8 August 1942**; the ground is now called **August Kranti Maidan**.
- **Jawaharlal Nehru** moved the resolution and **Vallabhbhai Patel** seconded it; **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad** was the Congress president.
- **Gandhi** gave the call **"Do or Die"** (Karo ya Maro) in his speech of **8 August 1942**: "We shall either free India or die in the attempt."
- The slogan **"Quit India"** is credited to **Yusuf Meherally**.
- The movement is also called the **August Revolution (August Kranti)**; the Viceroy was **Lord Linlithgow**.
- Gandhi and the other leaders were arrested early on **9 August 1942**.
- **Gandhi** was held in the **Aga Khan Palace, Poona**; the members of the Congress Working Committee were held in **Ahmednagar Fort**.
- **Aruna Asaf Ali** hoisted the flag at **Gowalia Tank Maidan** on **9 August 1942** after the leaders had been arrested.
- **Usha Mehta** ran the secret **Congress Radio** from Bombay.
- Underground leaders: **Jayaprakash Narayan, Ram Manohar Lohia, Aruna Asaf Ali, Achyut Patwardhan and Sucheta Kripalani**.
- Parallel government at **Ballia** (eastern U.P.): set up in **August 1942** under **Chittu Pandey**; it lasted about a week.
- Parallel government at **Tamluk** (Midnapore, Bengal): the **Jatiya Sarkar**, from **December 1942 to September 1944**.
- Parallel government at **Satara** (Maharashtra): the **Prati Sarkar**, led by **Nana Patil** and **Y.B. Chavan**; it lasted the longest.
- A parallel government was also set up at **Talcher** in **Orissa**; there was **none at Kheda**.
- **Matangini Hazra**, called **"Gandhi Buri"**, was shot dead while leading a procession at **Tamluk** in **1942**.
- **Kushal Konwar** of **Assam** was hanged in **1943**; he is known as the only martyr of the Quit India Movement to be hanged.
- **Kanaklata Barua**, a young girl of **Assam**, was shot dead while trying to hoist the flag at a police station.
- **Patna Secretariat firing (11 August 1942)**: **7** students were shot dead while trying to hoist the flag on the Secretariat building; the **Shaheed Smarak** in Patna is their memorial.
- The district magistrate of Patna who ordered the firing was **W.G. Archer**.
- **Rajendra Prasad** was arrested at **Patna** on **9 August 1942** and kept in **Bankipur Jail**.
- **Jayaprakash Narayan** escaped from **Hazaribagh Central Jail** on **9 November 1942**, the night of Diwali, with **5** companions, among them **Yogendra Shukla, Ramanandan Mishra and Suraj Narayan Singh**.
- Jayaprakash Narayan organised the **Azad Dasta**, a guerrilla band, in the **Terai of Nepal**; Ram Manohar Lohia worked with him.
- When Jayaprakash Narayan and Lohia were caught in Nepal and held at **Hanuman Nagar**, the Azad Dasta freed them.
- In **Bhagalpur**, the **Siyaram Dal** of **Siyaram Singh** carried on underground resistance.
- The **Muslim League**, the **Hindu Mahasabha** and the **Communist Party of India** did not support the Quit India Movement.
- Gandhi went on a **21**-day fast in the Aga Khan Palace in **February 1943**.
- **Mahadev Desai**, Gandhi's secretary, died in the Aga Khan Palace in **August 1942**, and **Kasturba Gandhi** died there in **February 1944**.
- **Lord Wavell** became Viceroy in **October 1943**; the **Bengal Famine** took place in **1943**.
- Gandhi was released on **6 May 1944** on grounds of health.
- **C.R. Formula (1944)**: **C. Rajagopalachari's** plan for a settlement between the Congress and the Muslim League; the **Gandhi-Jinnah talks** of **September 1944** on it failed.

## What the exams ask
- Who gave the slogan "Do or Die", and during which movement.
- The first and second individual satyagrahis, and where Vinoba Bhave began.
- Where the parallel governments were set up, and what each was called.
- People of 1942: Aruna Asaf Ali, Usha Mehta, Matangini Hazra, Kushal Konwar.
- The Bihar angle: Jayaprakash Narayan's escape from Hazaribagh Jail, the Azad Dasta, the Patna Secretariat firing.
- Dates and places: Cripps Mission (1942), Gowalia Tank, Bombay (8 August 1942).
