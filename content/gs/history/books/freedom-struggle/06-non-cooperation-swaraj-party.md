## Gist
By 1920 Indians had two great grievances: the **Punjab wrong** (the Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh and the mild treatment of General Dyer) and the **Khilafat wrong** (the harsh terms forced on Turkey). Gandhi proposed **non-cooperation**: British rule stood on the cooperation of Indians, and it would fall if they withdrew it. The movement began on **1 August 1920**, the day **Tilak** died. The Congress approved it at a **special session at Calcutta** in September and confirmed it at **Nagpur** in December 1920. Gandhi promised **Swaraj within a year**.

People were asked to give up titles and to boycott government schools, law courts, legislative councils and foreign cloth. In their place came national schools, panchayats, the **charkha** and **khadi**. Students left colleges in thousands, and lawyers such as **Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das and Rajendra Prasad** gave up their practice. Foreign cloth was burnt in public. In the towns the middle classes joined in large numbers. The Congress boycotted the council elections, but the **Justice Party of Madras** contested them. In the villages the movement merged with peasant struggles in Awadh, Malabar and Andhra.

Gandhi was about to begin civil disobedience at **Bardoli** when, on **5 February 1922**, a crowd at **Chauri Chaura** in **Gorakhpur district** set fire to a police station and **22 policemen** died. Gandhi would not lead a violent movement and called it off. Many leaders were dismayed. Gandhi was arrested in **March 1922** and sentenced to six years.

The Congress now divided on what to do next. **C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru** wanted to enter the councils and wreck them from within; they were the **"pro-changers"**. **Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel and Rajendra Prasad**, the **"no-changers"**, wanted to go on with boycott and constructive work. At the **Gaya session of 1922** the no-changers won. Das and Nehru then formed the **Swaraj Party** on **1 January 1923**.

The Swarajists did well in the elections of **1923**. In the councils they outvoted the government again and again and showed that the reforms of 1919 were hollow. **Vithalbhai Patel** became the first Indian president of the Central Legislative Assembly in **1925**. After the death of **C.R. Das in 1925** the party weakened and split. In Bihar the Swaraj Dal was set up by **Shri Krishna Singh**.

## Key facts
- The **Non-Cooperation Movement** was launched on **1 August 1920**; **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** died the same day.
- Its three demands: redress of the **Punjab wrong**, redress of the **Khilafat wrong**, and **Swaraj**.
- The **Khilafat Committee** accepted Gandhi's plan of non-cooperation first; the Congress followed.
- The **special session at Calcutta (September 1920)**, under **Lala Lajpat Rai**, approved non-cooperation.
- The **Nagpur session (December 1920)**, under **C. Vijayaraghavachariar**, confirmed it.
- At Nagpur the Congress changed its goal to **Swaraj by peaceful and legitimate means**, set up a **Working Committee of 15 members**, and formed provincial committees on a **linguistic basis**.
- Gandhi promised **Swaraj within one year**.
- **Lord Chelmsford** was the Viceroy when the movement began; **Lord Reading** was the Viceroy from **1921**.
- Programme of boycott: **titles, government schools and colleges, law courts, legislative councils and foreign cloth**.
- Constructive programme: **national schools, panchayats, charkha and khadi, Hindu-Muslim unity and removal of untouchability**.
- Gandhi returned the **Kaiser-i-Hind** medal; **Jamnalal Bajaj** gave up the title of **Rai Bahadur**.
- Lawyers who gave up their practice: **Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, C. Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel, Rajendra Prasad, Saifuddin Kitchlew and Asaf Ali**.
- National institutions founded: **Jamia Millia Islamia** (at Aligarh, 1920), **Kashi Vidyapith, Gujarat Vidyapith and Bihar Vidyapith**.
- **Subhas Chandra Bose** resigned from the Indian Civil Service and became principal of the **National College, Calcutta**.
- The **Tilak Swaraj Fund** collected more than its target of **one crore rupees**.
- In the towns the **middle classes** took part: thousands of **students** left government schools, **headmasters and teachers resigned**, and **lawyers gave up their practice**.
- The council elections were boycotted in most provinces, but the **Justice Party of Madras**, the party of the non-Brahmans, **contested** them.
- The **Prince of Wales** came to India in **November 1921** and was met with a hartal.
- The **Ali brothers** were arrested in **1921**.
- The **Ahmedabad session (1921)**: **C.R. Das** was president-elect but in jail, so **Hakim Ajmal Khan** presided; Gandhi was given sole authority to lead the movement.
- Peasant struggles of the time: the **Awadh Kisan Sabha** under **Baba Ramchandra**, the **Eka Movement**, and the **Moplah rebellion** in **Malabar (1921)**.
- **Bihar**: **Mazharul Haque** founded the **Sadaqat Ashram** at **Patna** and started the paper **The Motherland** in **1921**.
- **Bihar Vidyapith** was opened at **Patna** in **1921**; **Rajendra Prasad** gave up his legal practice to join the movement.
- **Chauri Chaura** is in **Gorakhpur district**, Uttar Pradesh; on **5 February 1922** a crowd burnt the police station and **22 policemen** were killed.
- Gandhi called off the Non-Cooperation Movement after the **Chauri Chaura incident**; the Congress Working Committee confirmed it at **Bardoli** on **12 February 1922**.
- **Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, Subhas Bose and Lala Lajpat Rai** criticised the withdrawal.
- Gandhi was arrested on **10 March 1922** and sentenced to **six years** by Judge **Broomfield** at Ahmedabad; he was released in **February 1924** on grounds of health.
- **Pro-changers** (for council entry): **C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru, Vithalbhai Patel and Hakim Ajmal Khan**.
- **No-changers** (against council entry): **C. Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel, Rajendra Prasad and M.A. Ansari**.
- At the **Gaya session (December 1922)**, with **C.R. Das** as president, the proposal for council entry was defeated.
- The **Swaraj Party** (Congress-Khilafat Swaraj Party) was founded on **1 January 1923** by **C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru**; **Das was its president** and **Motilal Nehru its secretary**.
- **Shri Krishna Singh** established the **Swaraj Dal in Bihar**.
- The **special session at Delhi (September 1923)**, under **Maulana Azad**, allowed Congressmen to contest the elections.
- In the elections of **November 1923** the Swarajists won **42 of the 101 elected seats** in the Central Legislative Assembly and a clear majority in the **Central Provinces**.
- The **Flag Satyagraha (Jhanda Satyagraha)** of **1923** was held at **Nagpur**.
- **C.R. Das** became Mayor of Calcutta in **1924**, with **Subhas Bose** as chief executive officer.
- The government appointed the **Muddiman Committee (1924)** to look into the working of dyarchy.
- At the **Belgaum session (1924)**, presided over by **Gandhi**, the Swarajists were accepted as the Congress's wing in the councils.
- **Vithalbhai Patel** was elected president (Speaker) of the **Central Legislative Assembly** in **1925**, the first Indian to hold the post.
- **C.R. Das**, known as **"Deshbandhu"**, died in **June 1925**.
- The **Responsivists**, who wanted to cooperate with the government and accept office, were **Lala Lajpat Rai, Madan Mohan Malaviya, N.C. Kelkar and M.R. Jayakar**.
- The Swarajists helped to defeat the **Public Safety Bill** in **1928**.
- The Khilafat issue ended when Turkey abolished the Caliphate in **1924**.

## What the exams ask
- Why and when Gandhi called off the movement (Chauri Chaura, February 1922) and where Chauri Chaura is (Gorakhpur district).
- Who founded the Swaraj Party and when (C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru, 1923); who set up the Swaraj Dal in Bihar (Shri Krishna Singh).
- Statement questions on who took part in the towns and on the Justice Party, which contested the council elections.
- Which session did what: Calcutta and Nagpur (1920), Gaya (1922), Belgaum (1924).
- Who was a pro-changer and who was a no-changer.
