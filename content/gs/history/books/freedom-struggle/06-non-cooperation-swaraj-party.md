| Session | President | What it decided |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calcutta, special (September 1920) | Lala Lajpat Rai | Approved non-cooperation. |
| Nagpur (December 1920) | C. Vijayaraghavachariar | Confirmed non-cooperation. Changed the goal to Swaraj by peaceful and legitimate means. Set up a Working Committee of 15 members, and provincial committees on a linguistic basis. |
| Ahmedabad (1921) | Hakim Ajmal Khan, since the president-elect, C.R. Das, was in jail | Gave Gandhi sole authority to lead the movement. |
| Gaya (December 1922) | C.R. Das | Defeated the proposal for council entry. |

## Why non-cooperation

By 1920 Indians had two great grievances.

1. **Punjab wrong:** the Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh and the mild treatment of General Dyer.
2. **Khilafat wrong:** the harsh terms forced on Turkey.
3. **Gandhi's answer:** British rule stood on the cooperation of Indians. It would fall if they withdrew that cooperation.
4. **Three demands:** The movement demanded redress of the Punjab wrong, redress of the Khilafat wrong, and Swaraj.

The Khilafat issue ended in 1924, when Turkey abolished the Caliphate.

## The launch (1920)

1. **Khilafat Committee first:** The Khilafat Committee accepted Gandhi's plan of non-cooperation first. The Congress followed.
2. **1 August 1920:** The Non-Cooperation Movement was launched. Bal Gangadhar Tilak died the same day. Lord Chelmsford was the Viceroy.
3. **September 1920:** The special session at Calcutta approved non-cooperation.
4. **December 1920:** The Nagpur session confirmed it.

Gandhi promised Swaraj within one year. The programme had two parts:

- **Boycott:** of titles, government schools and colleges, law courts, legislative councils and foreign cloth.
- **Constructive programme:** national schools, panchayats, the charkha (spinning wheel) and khadi (hand-spun, hand-woven cloth), Hindu-Muslim unity and the removal of untouchability.

## The movement at its height (1920–1921)

### In the towns

- **Middle classes:** In the towns the middle classes took part in large numbers. Thousands of students left government schools and colleges. Headmasters and teachers resigned.
- **Lawyers who gave up their practice:** Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, C. Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel, Rajendra Prasad, Saifuddin Kitchlew and Asaf Ali.
- **Titles given up:** Gandhi returned the Kaiser-i-Hind medal. Jamnalal Bajaj gave up the title of Rai Bahadur.
- **Subhas Chandra Bose:** He resigned from the Indian Civil Service and became principal of the National College, Calcutta.
- **Foreign cloth:** It was burnt in public.
- **Tilak Swaraj Fund:** It collected more than its target of one crore rupees.
- **Council elections:** They were boycotted in most provinces. But the Justice Party of Madras, the party of the non-Brahmans, contested them.

### National institutions

- **1920:** Jamia Millia Islamia was founded at Aligarh.
- **Others:** Kashi Vidyapith, Gujarat Vidyapith and Bihar Vidyapith.

### In Bihar

- **1921:** Mazharul Haque founded the Sadaqat Ashram at Patna and started the paper The Motherland.
- **1921:** Bihar Vidyapith was opened at Patna.
- **Rajendra Prasad:** He was among the lawyers who gave up their practice to join the movement.

### In the villages

In the villages the movement merged with peasant struggles in Awadh, Malabar and Andhra.

- **Awadh:** the Awadh Kisan Sabha under Baba Ramchandra, and the Eka Movement.
- **Malabar:** the Moplah rebellion (1921).

### Events of 1921

- **1921:** Lord Reading became the Viceroy.
- **1921:** The Ali brothers were arrested.
- **November 1921:** The Prince of Wales came to India and was met with a hartal.

## Chauri Chaura and the withdrawal (1922)

1. **Bardoli:** Gandhi was about to begin civil disobedience at Bardoli.
2. **5 February 1922:** At Chauri Chaura, in Gorakhpur district, Uttar Pradesh, a crowd set fire to the police station. 22 policemen were killed.
3. **12 February 1922:** Gandhi would not lead a violent movement, and called it off after the Chauri Chaura incident. The Congress Working Committee confirmed the withdrawal at Bardoli.
4. **Criticism:** Many leaders were dismayed. Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, Subhas Bose and Lala Lajpat Rai criticised the withdrawal.
5. **10 March 1922:** Gandhi was arrested. Judge Broomfield at Ahmedabad sentenced him to six years.
6. **February 1924:** Gandhi was released on grounds of health.

## Pro-changers and no-changers (1922)

The Congress now divided on what to do next.

| Group | What they wanted | Leaders |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pro-changers | Council entry: to enter the councils and wreck them from within. | C.R. Das, Motilal Nehru, Vithalbhai Patel, Hakim Ajmal Khan |
| No-changers | No council entry: to go on with boycott and constructive work. | C. Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel, Rajendra Prasad, M.A. Ansari |
| Responsivists | To cooperate with the government and accept office. | Lala Lajpat Rai, Madan Mohan Malaviya, N.C. Kelkar, M.R. Jayakar |

At the Gaya session (December 1922) the no-changers won, and council entry was defeated.

## The Swaraj Party (1923–1928)

In the councils the Swarajists outvoted the government again and again. They showed that the reforms of 1919 were hollow.

1. **1 January 1923:** C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru founded the Swaraj Party (Congress-Khilafat Swaraj Party). Das was its president and Motilal Nehru its secretary.
2. **September 1923:** The special session at Delhi allowed Congressmen to contest the elections.
3. **November 1923:** In the elections the Swarajists won 42 of the 101 elected seats in the Central Legislative Assembly. They won a clear majority in the Central Provinces.
4. **1923:** The Flag Satyagraha (Jhanda Satyagraha) was held at Nagpur.
5. **1924:** C.R. Das became Mayor of Calcutta, with Subhas Bose as chief executive officer.
6. **1924:** The government appointed the Muddiman Committee to look into the working of dyarchy.
7. **1925:** Vithalbhai Patel was elected president (Speaker) of the Central Legislative Assembly. He was the first Indian to hold the post.
8. **June 1925:** C.R. Das, known as "Deshbandhu", died. After his death the party weakened and split.
9. **1928:** The Swarajists helped to defeat the Public Safety Bill.

- **Bihar:** Shri Krishna Singh established the Swaraj Dal in Bihar.

## What the exams ask

- Why and when Gandhi called off the movement (Chauri Chaura, February 1922), and where Chauri Chaura is (Gorakhpur district).
- Who founded the Swaraj Party and when (C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru, 1923), and who set up the Swaraj Dal in Bihar (Shri Krishna Singh).
- Statement questions on who took part in the towns, and on the Justice Party, which contested the council elections.
- Which session did what: Calcutta and Nagpur (1920), Gaya (1922), Belgaum (1924).
- Who was a pro-changer and who was a no-changer.
