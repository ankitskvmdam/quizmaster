## Gist
By 1945 the war was ending and Britain knew it could not hold India much longer. The question was to whom it would hand over power. The **Muslim League** under **Jinnah** had demanded a separate state since its **Lahore Resolution of 1940**, and Jinnah answered Quit India with the cry **"Divide and Quit"**. In **June 1945** the Viceroy, **Lord Wavell**, called the leaders to the **Shimla Conference** to form a new Executive Council of Indians. It failed because Jinnah insisted that the League alone would name every Muslim member.

In **July 1945** the **Labour Party** under **Clement Attlee** came to power in Britain. Elections were held in India in the winter of **1945–46**: the Congress swept the general seats and the League the Muslim seats. In **March 1946** Attlee sent three ministers, the **Cabinet Mission**, to work out how power would be transferred. Its plan of **May 1946** rejected Pakistan and proposed a loose **Union** with provinces placed in **three groups**, a **Constituent Assembly** to write the constitution, and an **Interim Government**.

Both parties first accepted the plan and then fell out over what it meant. The League withdrew its acceptance and called for **Direct Action** on **16 August 1946**. Terrible riots followed in **Calcutta**, then in **Noakhali** and **Bihar**. The **Interim Government** headed by **Jawaharlal Nehru** took office on **2 September 1946**; the League joined it in October but only to obstruct it. The **Constituent Assembly** met on **9 December 1946**, and the League stayed away.

On **20 February 1947** Attlee announced that the British would leave India by **June 1948**, and sent **Lord Mountbatten** as the last Viceroy. Mountbatten quickly decided that partition was the only way out. His **plan of 3 June 1947** provided for two dominions, with **Punjab and Bengal** to be divided. The Congress accepted it to avoid further bloodshed.

The British Parliament passed the **Indian Independence Act** in **July 1947**. **Pakistan** came into being on **14 August** and **India** became free on **15 August 1947**. **Sir Cyril Radcliffe** drew the boundary lines in Punjab and Bengal. Freedom came with partition, mass killings and the migration of millions.

## Key facts
- The Muslim League passed the **Pakistan Resolution** at its **Lahore session** in **March 1940**, with **Jinnah** as president; **Fazlul Haq** moved it.
- **M.A. Jinnah** raised the cry **"Divide and Quit"** in reply to "Quit India", at the League's **Karachi session** in **1943**.
- **C.R. Formula (1944)** and the **Gandhi-Jinnah talks** of September 1944 failed to bring the Congress and the League together.
- **Desai-Liaquat Pact (1945)**: a plan of **Bhulabhai Desai** and **Liaquat Ali Khan** for an interim government with equal Congress and League nominees; it came to nothing.
- **Lord Wavell** was Viceroy from **October 1943 to March 1947**.
- **Wavell Plan (June 1945)**: all members of the Viceroy's Executive Council, except the Viceroy and the Commander-in-Chief, were to be Indians, with **equal numbers of caste Hindus and Muslims**.
- The **Shimla Conference** met on **25 June 1945** to discuss the Wavell Plan; **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad** led the Congress as its president.
- The Shimla Conference failed because **Jinnah** claimed that the **Muslim League alone** should nominate all the Muslim members.
- The **Labour Party** came to power in Britain in **July 1945**; **Clement Attlee** became Prime Minister and **Lord Pethick-Lawrence** the Secretary of State for India.
- In the elections of **1945–46** the Congress won most of the general seats and the Muslim League almost all the Muslim seats.
- The **Cabinet Mission** reached India in **March 1946**; its members were **Lord Pethick-Lawrence** (chairman), **Sir Stafford Cripps** and **A.V. Alexander**.
- The Cabinet Mission came to work out a **constitutional arrangement for the transfer of power**.
- The Cabinet Mission Plan was announced on **16 May 1946**; it **rejected the demand for Pakistan**.
- The plan proposed a **Union of India** with only **defence, foreign affairs and communications**; all other powers were to rest with the provinces.
- The provinces were put in three groups: **Section A** (Madras, Bombay, U.P., **Bihar**, Central Provinces, Orissa), **Section B** (Punjab, NWFP, Sind) and **Section C** (Bengal, Assam).
- The **Constituent Assembly** was to have **389** members: **292** from the provinces, **4** from the chief commissioners' provinces and **93** from the princely states.
- Members of the Constituent Assembly were to be **elected by the provincial legislative assemblies**, not directly by the people.
- The Muslim League withdrew its acceptance of the plan in **July 1946** and observed **Direct Action Day** on **16 August 1946**.
- Direct Action Day led to the **Great Calcutta Killings**; riots spread to **Noakhali** (East Bengal) and **Bihar** in **October–November 1946**.
- **Gandhi** toured **Noakhali** on foot for peace, and came to **Bihar** in **March 1947** for the same purpose.
- The **Interim Government** took office on **2 September 1946**; **Jawaharlal Nehru** was the **Vice-President of the Viceroy's Executive Council** and held **External Affairs**.
- In the Interim Government **Vallabhbhai Patel** held **Home and Information and Broadcasting**, and **Baldev Singh** held **Defence**.
- **Rajendra Prasad** held **Food and Agriculture**, and **Jagjivan Ram** held **Labour**; both were from **Bihar**.
- **C. Rajagopalachari** held **Education**, **Asaf Ali** held **Railways** and **John Matthai** held **Industries and Supplies**.
- The **Muslim League** joined the Interim Government on **26 October 1946**; **Liaquat Ali Khan** became the **Finance** member.
- Other League members: **I.I. Chundrigar** (Commerce), **Abdur Rab Nishtar** (Communications), **Ghazanfar Ali Khan** (Health) and **Jogendra Nath Mandal** (Law).
- The **Constituent Assembly** first met on **9 December 1946**; **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha** of **Bihar** was its temporary chairman.
- **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** was elected permanent president of the Constituent Assembly on **11 December 1946**.
- **Jawaharlal Nehru** moved the **Objectives Resolution** on **13 December 1946**; the Muslim League boycotted the Assembly.
- **Attlee's statement (20 February 1947)**: the British would withdraw from India latest by **June 1948**.
- **Lord Mountbatten** replaced Wavell in **March 1947**; he was the **last Viceroy** of India.
- **Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947)**: India to be divided into two dominions; it is also called the **3rd June Plan**.
- Under the plan the assemblies of **Punjab and Bengal** voted on partition, and referendums were held in the **NWFP** and in **Sylhet** district of Assam.
- **J.B. Kripalani** was the Congress president at the time of independence.
- The **Indian Independence Act, 1947** received the royal assent on **18 July 1947**; it provided for the partition of India into two dominions, **India and Pakistan**, from **15 August 1947**.
- Under the Act **British paramountcy over the princely states lapsed**, and the office of the **Secretary of State for India** was abolished.
- Each dominion's Constituent Assembly was to act as its legislature, and the **Government of India Act, 1935** was to continue until a new constitution was made.
- **Sir Cyril Radcliffe** headed both **Boundary Commissions**, for **Punjab** and for **Bengal**; the boundary is called the **Radcliffe Line**.
- **Pakistan** came into being on **14 August 1947** with **Jinnah** as its first Governor-General and **Liaquat Ali Khan** as its first Prime Minister.
- India became independent on **15 August 1947**; **Lord Mountbatten** was the first Governor-General of free India and **Jawaharlal Nehru** the first Prime Minister.
- **C. Rajagopalachari** became Governor-General in **June 1948**; he was the first and only Indian to hold the office.
- On **15 August 1947** **Gandhi** was in **Calcutta**, working to stop communal riots.
- Order of events: **Cripps Mission (March 1942)**, **Quit India Resolution (August 1942)**, **Wavell as Viceroy (October 1943)**, **Shimla Conference (June 1945)**, **Cabinet Mission (March 1946)**, **Direct Action Day (August 1946)**, **Interim Government (September 1946)**, **Attlee's statement (February 1947)**, **Mountbatten Plan (June 1947)**.

## What the exams ask
- Who was, or was not, a member of the Cabinet Mission.
- Who held which portfolio in the Interim Government (Rajendra Prasad, Liaquat Ali Khan, Jagjivan Ram).
- Dates: Direct Action Day, Attlee's statement and its deadline, the Mountbatten Plan.
- Which came first among the Cripps Mission, Quit India, Wavell and the Cabinet Mission.
- Who headed the Boundary Commissions, and which Act created the two dominions.
- The Bihar angle: Sachchidananda Sinha and Rajendra Prasad in the Constituent Assembly, Bihar in Section A.
