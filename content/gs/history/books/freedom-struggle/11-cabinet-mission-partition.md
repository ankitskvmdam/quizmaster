## Background: the League's demand (1940–45)

By 1945 the war was ending, and Britain knew it could not hold India much longer. The question was to whom it would hand over power.

1. **March 1940:** At its Lahore session the Muslim League passed the Pakistan Resolution, also called the Lahore Resolution. Jinnah was the president, and Fazlul Haq moved the resolution. From then on the League demanded a separate state.
2. **1943:** At the League's Karachi session, M.A. Jinnah answered "Quit India" with the cry "Divide and Quit".
3. **1944:** The C.R. Formula (1944) and the Gandhi-Jinnah talks of September 1944 failed to bring the Congress and the League together.
4. **1945:** Bhulabhai Desai and Liaquat Ali Khan drew up the Desai-Liaquat Pact. It planned an interim government with equal numbers of Congress and League nominees. It came to nothing.

## Wavell, Shimla and the elections (1945–46)

Lord Wavell was Viceroy from October 1943 to March 1947.

1. **June 1945:** Under the Wavell Plan, all members of the Viceroy's Executive Council were to be Indians, except the Viceroy and the Commander-in-Chief. Caste Hindus and Muslims were to be equal in number.
2. **25 June 1945:** Wavell called the leaders to the Shimla Conference to discuss the plan. Maulana Abul Kalam Azad led the Congress as its president. The conference failed because Jinnah claimed that the Muslim League alone should nominate all the Muslim members.
3. **July 1945:** The Labour Party came to power in Britain. Clement Attlee became Prime Minister, and Lord Pethick-Lawrence the Secretary of State for India.
4. **Winter of 1945–46:** Elections were held in India. The Congress won most of the general seats. The Muslim League won almost all the Muslim seats.

## The Cabinet Mission (1946)

1. **March 1946:** Attlee sent three ministers, the Cabinet Mission, to India. Its members were Lord Pethick-Lawrence (chairman), Sir Stafford Cripps and A.V. Alexander. It came to work out a constitutional arrangement for the transfer of power.
2. **16 May 1946:** The Cabinet Mission Plan was announced. It rejected the demand for Pakistan.

### What the plan proposed

- **A loose Union:** The Union of India was to hold only defence, foreign affairs and communications. All other powers were to rest with the provinces.
- **Three groups of provinces:** The provinces were placed in three sections, shown in the table below.
- **A Constituent Assembly:** It was to write the constitution. It was to have 389 members: 292 from the provinces, 4 from the chief commissioners' provinces and 93 from the princely states. The provincial legislative assemblies were to elect them, not the people directly.
- **An Interim Government:** The plan proposed this too.

| Section A | Madras, Bombay, U.P., Bihar, Central Provinces, Orissa |
| Section B | Punjab, North-West Frontier Province (NWFP), Sind |
| Section C | Bengal, Assam |

## Direct Action and the riots (1946)

Both parties first accepted the plan. Then they fell out over what it meant.

1. **July 1946:** The Muslim League withdrew its acceptance of the plan.
2. **16 August 1946:** The League observed Direct Action Day. It led to the Great Calcutta Killings.
3. **October–November 1946:** The riots spread to Noakhali (East Bengal) and Bihar.

Gandhi toured Noakhali on foot for peace. In March 1947 he came to Bihar for the same purpose.

## The Interim Government (1946)

1. **2 September 1946:** The Interim Government headed by Jawaharlal Nehru took office. Nehru was the Vice-President of the Viceroy's Executive Council.
2. **26 October 1946:** The Muslim League joined the Interim Government, but only to obstruct it.

## The Constituent Assembly (1946)

1. **9 December 1946:** The Constituent Assembly first met. Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha of Bihar was its temporary chairman. The Muslim League boycotted the Assembly.
2. **11 December 1946:** Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected its permanent president.
3. **13 December 1946:** Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution.

## Mountbatten and partition (1947)

1. **20 February 1947:** In Attlee's statement, the British said they would withdraw from India by June 1948 at the latest. Attlee sent Lord Mountbatten to India.
2. **March 1947:** Lord Mountbatten replaced Wavell. He was the last Viceroy of India. He quickly decided that partition was the only way out.
3. **3 June 1947:** The Mountbatten Plan, also called the 3rd June Plan, provided for dividing India into two dominions. Punjab and Bengal were to be divided. The Congress accepted it to avoid further bloodshed.

Under the plan the assemblies of Punjab and Bengal voted on partition. Referendums were held in the NWFP and in Sylhet district of Assam.

### The Indian Independence Act, 1947

- **18 July 1947:** The British Parliament had passed the Act, and it received the royal assent. It provided for the partition of India into two dominions, India and Pakistan, from 15 August 1947.
- **The princely states:** British paramountcy over them lapsed.
- **The Secretary of State for India:** This office was abolished.
- **Until a new constitution:** Each dominion's Constituent Assembly was to act as its legislature. The Government of India Act, 1935 was to continue.

### The Radcliffe Line

Sir Cyril Radcliffe headed both Boundary Commissions, for Punjab and for Bengal. The boundary he drew is called the Radcliffe Line.

## Independence (1947–48)

1. **14 August 1947:** Pakistan came into being. Jinnah was its first Governor-General, and Liaquat Ali Khan its first Prime Minister.
2. **15 August 1947:** India became independent. Lord Mountbatten was the first Governor-General of free India, and Jawaharlal Nehru the first Prime Minister. J.B. Kripalani was the Congress president.
3. **15 August 1947:** Gandhi was in Calcutta, working to stop communal riots.
4. **June 1948:** C. Rajagopalachari became Governor-General. He was the first and only Indian to hold the office.

Freedom came with partition, mass killings and the migration of millions.

- Who was, or was not, a member of the Cabinet Mission.
- Who held which portfolio in the Interim Government (Rajendra Prasad, Liaquat Ali Khan, Jagjivan Ram).
- Dates: Direct Action Day, Attlee's statement and its deadline, the Mountbatten Plan.
- Which came first among the Cripps Mission, Quit India, Wavell and the Cabinet Mission.
- Who headed the Boundary Commissions, and which Act created the two dominions.
- The Bihar angle: Sachchidananda Sinha and Rajendra Prasad in the Constituent Assembly, Bihar in Section A.
