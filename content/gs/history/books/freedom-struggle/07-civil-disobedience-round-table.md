## The Simon Commission (1927–28)

In November 1927 the British government appointed the Simon Commission, also called the Indian Statutory Commission. Its task was to review how the Government of India Act, 1919 had worked.

The commission had 7 members, all of them British. Sir John Simon was its chairman. Clement Attlee, later Prime Minister of Britain, was one of the members.

1. **December 1927:** At its Madras session the Congress decided to boycott the commission. Almost all Indian parties boycotted it too, because it had no Indian member. Indians of almost every party felt insulted that no Indian was thought fit to sit on it.
2. **3 February 1928:** The commission landed at Bombay. It was met with hartals (strikes with shops shut), black flags and the cry "Simon Go Back". The slogan is credited to Yusuf Meherally.
3. **30 October 1928:** Lala Lajpat Rai led a protest against the commission at Lahore. He was injured in a police lathi-charge.
4. **17 November 1928:** Lala Lajpat Rai died.

## From the Nehru Report to Purna Swaraj (1928–30)

1. Lord Birkenhead, the Secretary of State for India, had challenged Indians to produce a constitution that all parties would accept.
2. **August 1928:** The Nehru Report was the answer. A committee of the All Parties Conference drafted it, with Motilal Nehru as chairman. It was the first Indian attempt to draft a constitution. It asked for four things:
   - dominion status (self-government within the British Empire);
   - joint electorates (all communities voting together), with reserved seats for minorities;
   - fundamental rights;
   - provinces formed on a linguistic basis.
3. **March 1929:** M.A. Jinnah put forward his Fourteen Points in reply to the Nehru Report.
4. **31 October 1929:** In the Irwin Declaration the Viceroy, Lord Irwin, said that dominion status was the goal of British policy. He also promised a Round Table Conference.
5. **December 1929:** The government gave no clear answer. So the Congress, at its Lahore session, demanded Purna Swaraj (complete independence).
6. **26 January 1930:** The Congress observed Purna Swaraj Day.
7. **January 1930:** Gandhi placed 11 demands before Lord Irwin. One of them was the abolition of the salt tax. The government ignored them.

## The Dandi March (1930)

Gandhi chose salt as the issue for the new movement. The salt tax hurt every Indian, rich or poor.

1. **12 March 1930:** Gandhi left Sabarmati Ashram, Ahmedabad, on foot. He had 78 chosen followers with him.
2. **5 April 1930:** The march reached Dandi, on the coast of Gujarat, after about 240 miles and 24 days. Dandi is now in Navsari district.
3. **6 April 1930:** Gandhi broke the salt law at Dandi. This began the Civil Disobedience Movement. The Viceroy was Lord Irwin.
4. **5 May 1930:** Gandhi was arrested and sent to Yerawada Jail, Poona.

Subhas Chandra Bose compared the Dandi March to Napoleon's march to Paris on his return from Elba.

## The movement across India (1930)

Across India people made salt and broke the salt law. The movement also took three other forms:

- **Forest satyagraha:** People broke forest laws in Maharashtra, Karnataka and the Central Provinces.
- **Picketing:** People stood outside liquor and foreign-cloth shops to stop the trade.
- **No-tax campaigns:** People refused to pay taxes.

The movement in the regions:

- **Peshawar (April 1930):** Soldiers of the Garhwal Rifles, led by Chandra Singh Garhwali, refused to fire on an unarmed crowd.
- **Dharasana (May 1930):** Abbas Tyabji was arrested. Sarojini Naidu then led the raid on the Dharasana salt works, with Imam Sahib and Manilal Gandhi. The American journalist Webb Miller reported the beating of the satyagrahis.
- **Tamil Nadu:** C. Rajagopalachari led a salt march from Tiruchirappalli to Vedaranyam.
- **Malabar:** K. Kelappan led a salt march from Calicut to Payyanur.
- **North-West Frontier Province:** Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan led the movement with his Khudai Khidmatgars, also called the Red Shirts. He was known as "Frontier Gandhi" and "Badshah Khan".
- **North-east:** Rani Gaidinliu, a young Naga girl, led a revolt against British rule. Jawaharlal Nehru gave her the title "Rani".

## The movement in Bihar (1930)

Bihar has no sea coast. So the main form of the movement there was refusal to pay the chowkidari tax (a tax to pay village watchmen).

- **Champaran and Saran:** These were the first districts in Bihar to start the salt satyagraha.
- **Patna:** Salt was made at Nakhas Pond under the leadership of Ambika Kant Sinha.
- **Garhpura:** Shri Krishna Sinha broke the salt law here. Garhpura is now in Begusarai district.
- **Bihpur (1930):** Rajendra Prasad was injured in a lathi-charge during the Bihpur Satyagraha in Bhagalpur district.
- **Barhee, Munger:** The agitation against the chowkidari tax was strongest in the Barhee area of Munger.

## The Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931)

1. **November 1930:** While the Congress leaders were in jail, the British held the First Round Table Conference without the Congress. It could not achieve much.
2. **January 1931:** So the Viceroy released Gandhi from prison. The Congress leaders then met at Allahabad to plan the next step. Talks with the Viceroy followed.
3. **5 March 1931:** The Gandhi-Irwin Pact, also called the Delhi Pact, was signed.

What the pact said:

- **The Congress:** It agreed to suspend civil disobedience and to attend the Second Round Table Conference.
- **The government:** It agreed to release political prisoners not convicted of violence. It also let people on the coast make salt for their own use.
- **Not secured:** The pact did not secure the commutation (change to a lighter sentence) of Bhagat Singh's death sentence. Nor did it secure a public inquiry into police excesses.

In April 1931 Lord Willingdon replaced Lord Irwin as Viceroy.

## The three Round Table Conferences (1930–32)

All three conferences were held in London.

| Conference | Dates | Congress | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First | November 1930 to January 1931 | Did not attend | King George V opened it. Prime Minister Ramsay MacDonald presided. |
| Second | September to December 1931 | Gandhi as its sole representative | It failed over separate electorates for minorities. |
| Third | November to December 1932 | Did not attend | — |

- **All three:** B.R. Ambedkar and Tej Bahadur Sapru took part in all three conferences. Gandhi attended only the second.
- **SS Rajputana:** Gandhi sailed to the second conference on this ship.
- **Result:** The discussions of the three conferences led to a White Paper (a statement of government proposals) in 1933. Then came the Government of India Act, 1935.

## The second phase and the Poona Pact (1931–34)

1. **December 1931:** Gandhi came home empty-handed from the second conference.
2. **January 1932:** The movement was resumed. Gandhi was arrested on 4 January 1932. The government crushed the movement with mass arrests.
3. **16 August 1932:** The British Prime Minister, Ramsay MacDonald, announced the Communal Award. It gave separate electorates to the depressed classes.
4. **20 September 1932:** Gandhi, in Yerawada Jail, began a fast unto death against the award.
5. **24 September 1932:** The Poona Pact between Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar ended the fast. Madan Mohan Malaviya signed it on behalf of the caste Hindus.
6. **1932:** Gandhi set up the Harijan Sevak Sangh.
7. **1933:** Gandhi started the weekly Harijan.
8. **1934:** The movement had slowly lost force. It was finally withdrawn.

Under the Poona Pact the depressed classes gave up separate electorates. In return their reserved seats in the provincial legislatures were raised from 71 to 147. These seats were to be filled by joint electorates.

The Civil Disobedience Movement thus had two phases: 1930–31 and 1932–34.

## What the exams ask

- The numbers of the Dandi March: 12 March to 6 April 1930, 24 days, 78 followers.
- The year of each Round Table Conference (1930, 1931, 1932), and who attended: Gandhi only the second, Ambedkar all three.
- Who signed which pact: the Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931); the Poona Pact (Gandhi and Ambedkar, 1932).
- Why the Simon Commission was boycotted: it had no Indian member.
- The regional leaders of the salt satyagraha: Rajagopalachari, Kelappan, Sarojini Naidu, Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan.
- The Bihar angle: the chowkidari tax, Nakhas Pond, Bihpur.
