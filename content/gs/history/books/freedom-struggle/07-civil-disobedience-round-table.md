## Gist
In **November 1927** the British government appointed the **Simon Commission** to report on how the Government of India Act, 1919 had worked. All its members were British. Indians of almost every party felt insulted that no Indian was thought fit to sit on it, and greeted it in **1928** with black flags and the cry **"Simon Go Back"**. Indian leaders then wrote their own scheme, the **Nehru Report (1928)**, which asked for **dominion status**. When the government gave no clear answer, the Congress at Lahore in December 1929 demanded **Purna Swaraj** and observed **26 January 1930** as Purna Swaraj Day.

Gandhi chose **salt** as the issue for the new movement, because the salt tax hurt every Indian, rich or poor. On **12 March 1930** he left **Sabarmati Ashram** with **78** followers and walked for **24 days** to **Dandi** on the Gujarat coast, where he broke the salt law on **6 April 1930**. This began the **Civil Disobedience Movement**. People made salt, picketed liquor and foreign-cloth shops, broke forest laws and refused to pay taxes. In landlocked Bihar the main form was refusal of the **chowkidari tax**.

While the Congress leaders were in jail, the British held the **First Round Table Conference** in London (**November 1930**) without the Congress. It could not achieve much, so the Viceroy released Gandhi and signed the **Gandhi-Irwin Pact** on **5 March 1931**. The Congress suspended the movement, and Gandhi went to the **Second Round Table Conference** (**September to December 1931**) as its sole representative. The conference broke down over separate electorates for minorities. Gandhi came home empty-handed, the movement was resumed in **January 1932**, and the government crushed it with mass arrests.

In **August 1932** the British Prime Minister **Ramsay MacDonald** announced the **Communal Award**, which gave separate electorates to the depressed classes. Gandhi, then in **Yerawada Jail**, began a fast unto death against it. The **Poona Pact** of **24 September 1932** between Gandhi and **B.R. Ambedkar** ended the fast: the depressed classes gave up separate electorates and got many more reserved seats instead.

The **Third Round Table Conference** (**November to December 1932**) was again held without the Congress. The three conferences led to the Government of India Act, 1935. The Civil Disobedience Movement slowly lost force and was withdrawn in **1934**. Exams ask the dates, the places, who attended which conference and who signed which pact.

## Key facts
- The **Simon Commission** (Indian Statutory Commission) was appointed in **November 1927** to review the working of the **Government of India Act, 1919**.
- It had **7** members, all British, with **Sir John Simon** as chairman; **Clement Attlee**, later Prime Minister of Britain, was one of them.
- Almost all Indian parties boycotted the commission because it had **no Indian member**; the Congress decided on the boycott at its **Madras session (December 1927)**.
- The commission landed at **Bombay** on **3 February 1928** and was met with hartals and the slogan **"Simon Go Back"**; the slogan is credited to **Yusuf Meherally**.
- **Lala Lajpat Rai** was injured in a police lathi-charge while leading a protest against the commission at **Lahore** on **30 October 1928** and died on **17 November 1928**.
- **Lord Birkenhead**, the Secretary of State for India, had challenged Indians to produce a constitution that all parties would accept.
- The **Nehru Report (August 1928)** was the answer: it was drafted by a committee of the **All Parties Conference** with **Motilal Nehru** as chairman, and was the first Indian attempt to draft a constitution.
- The Nehru Report asked for **dominion status**, **joint electorates** with reserved seats for minorities, **fundamental rights** and provinces formed on a **linguistic** basis.
- **M.A. Jinnah** put forward his **Fourteen Points** in **March 1929** in reply to the Nehru Report.
- In the **Irwin Declaration** of **31 October 1929**, Viceroy **Lord Irwin** said that dominion status was the goal of British policy and promised a Round Table Conference.
- **26 January 1930** was observed as **Purna Swaraj Day** by the Congress, after the Lahore session of December 1929.
- In **January 1930** Gandhi placed **11 demands** before Lord Irwin, among them the **abolition of the salt tax**; the government ignored them.
- The **Dandi March** began from **Sabarmati Ashram, Ahmedabad**, on **12 March 1930**; Gandhi had **78** chosen followers with him.
- The march covered about **240 miles** in **24 days** and reached **Dandi**, on the coast of Gujarat (now in **Navsari** district), on **5 April 1930**.
- Gandhi broke the salt law at Dandi on **6 April 1930**; this began the **Civil Disobedience Movement**. The Viceroy was **Lord Irwin**.
- **Subhas Chandra Bose** compared the Dandi March to **Napoleon's march to Paris** on his return from Elba.
- Gandhi was arrested on **5 May 1930** and sent to **Yerawada Jail**, Poona.
- The raid on the **Dharasana salt works** (**May 1930**) was led by **Sarojini Naidu**, with Imam Sahib and Manilal Gandhi, after **Abbas Tyabji** was arrested; the American journalist **Webb Miller** reported the beating of the satyagrahis.
- In Tamil Nadu **C. Rajagopalachari** led a salt march from **Tiruchirappalli to Vedaranyam**.
- In Malabar **K. Kelappan** led a salt march from **Calicut to Payyanur**.
- In the North-West Frontier Province **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** ("Frontier Gandhi", "Badshah Khan") led the movement with his **Khudai Khidmatgars**, also called the **Red Shirts**.
- At **Peshawar** in **April 1930**, soldiers of the **Garhwal Rifles** led by **Chandra Singh Garhwali** refused to fire on an unarmed crowd.
- **Rani Gaidinliu**, a young **Naga** girl, led a revolt against British rule in the north-east; **Jawaharlal Nehru** gave her the title **"Rani"**.
- Other forms of the movement: **forest satyagraha** in Maharashtra, Karnataka and the Central Provinces, picketing of liquor and foreign-cloth shops, and no-tax campaigns.
- In **Bihar**, **Champaran and Saran** were the first districts to start the salt satyagraha.
- In **Patna**, salt was made at **Nakhas Pond** under the leadership of **Ambika Kant Sinha**.
- Bihar has no sea coast, so the main form of the movement there was refusal to pay the **chowkidari tax**; the agitation was strongest in the **Barhee** area of **Munger**.
- **Shri Krishna Sinha** broke the salt law at **Garhpura** (now in Begusarai district), and **Rajendra Prasad** was injured in a lathi-charge during the **Bihpur Satyagraha** in Bhagalpur district in **1930**.
- The **First Round Table Conference** was held in **London** from **November 1930 to January 1931**; **King George V** opened it and Prime Minister **Ramsay MacDonald** presided. The **Congress did not attend**.
- Gandhi was released from prison in **January 1931**; the Congress leaders then met at **Allahabad** to plan the next step, and talks with the Viceroy followed.
- The **Gandhi-Irwin Pact** (Delhi Pact) was signed on **5 March 1931**.
- Under the pact the Congress agreed to **suspend civil disobedience** and attend the **Second Round Table Conference**; the government agreed to **release political prisoners not convicted of violence** and to let people on the coast **make salt for their own use**.
- The pact did **not** secure the commutation of **Bhagat Singh's** death sentence or a public inquiry into police excesses.
- The **Second Round Table Conference** was held in London from **September to December 1931**; **Gandhi** was the **sole representative of the Congress**. He sailed on the ship **SS Rajputana**.
- **Lord Willingdon** had replaced Lord Irwin as Viceroy in **April 1931**.
- The second conference failed on the question of **separate electorates for minorities**; Gandhi returned in **December 1931**, the movement was resumed, and he was arrested on **4 January 1932**.
- The **Communal Award** was announced by British Prime Minister **Ramsay MacDonald** on **16 August 1932**; it gave **separate electorates** to the **depressed classes**.
- Gandhi began a **fast unto death** against the award in **Yerawada Jail** on **20 September 1932**.
- The **Poona Pact** was signed on **24 September 1932** between **Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar**; **Madan Mohan Malaviya** signed on behalf of the caste Hindus.
- By the Poona Pact the depressed classes gave up **separate electorates**; their reserved seats in the provincial legislatures were raised from **71** to **147**, to be filled by **joint electorates**.
- The **Third Round Table Conference** was held in London from **November to December 1932**; the Congress did not attend.
- **B.R. Ambedkar** and **Tej Bahadur Sapru** took part in **all three** Round Table Conferences; **Gandhi** attended only the **second**.
- The discussions of the three conferences led to a White Paper in **1933** and then to the **Government of India Act, 1935**.
- Gandhi set up the **Harijan Sevak Sangh** in **1932** and started the weekly **Harijan** in **1933**.
- The Civil Disobedience Movement had two phases, **1930–31** and **1932–34**; it was finally withdrawn in **1934**.

## What the exams ask
- Numbers of the Dandi March: 12 March to 6 April 1930, 24 days, 78 followers.
- The year of each Round Table Conference (1930, 1931, 1932) and who attended (Gandhi only the second; Ambedkar all three).
- Who signed which pact: Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931), Poona Pact (Gandhi and Ambedkar, 1932).
- Why the Simon Commission was boycotted (no Indian member).
- Regional leaders of the salt satyagraha: Rajagopalachari, Kelappan, Sarojini Naidu, Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan.
- The Bihar angle: the chowkidari tax, Nakhas Pond, Bihpur.
