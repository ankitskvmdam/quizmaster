## Early life and the Congress (1897–1939)

1. **23 January 1897:** Subhas Chandra Bose was born at Cuttack in Orissa. His birthday is observed as Parakram Diwas.
2. **1920:** Bose stood fourth in the Indian Civil Service examination.
3. **1921:** He resigned to join the national movement. He worked in Bengal under his political guru, Chittaranjan Das (C.R. Das).
4. **1938:** Bose was Congress president at the Haripura session.
5. **1939:** At Tripuri he won again. He defeated Pattabhi Sitaramayya, whom Gandhi had backed.
6. **April 1939:** Bose could not work with the Gandhian leadership. He resigned as Congress president, and Rajendra Prasad took his place.
7. **May 1939:** Bose founded the Forward Bloc, at first as a group within the Congress.

Bose wrote The Indian Struggle and an unfinished autobiography, An Indian Pilgrim.

## From the Forward Bloc to Germany (1939–43)

Bose believed that Britain's difficulty in the Second World War was India's opportunity. India, he held, should take the help of Britain's enemies.

1. **March 1940:** Bose and Swami Sahajanand Saraswati held the Anti-Compromise Conference at Ramgarh (then in Bihar). It met at the same time as the Congress session there.
2. **July 1940:** Bose was arrested. He was later kept under house arrest in his house in Calcutta.
3. **January 1941:** He escaped from Calcutta in disguise, as a Pathan named Ziauddin. His nephew Sisir Bose drove him to Gomoh (then in Bihar), where he boarded a train.
4. **The journey:** He went by way of Peshawar and Kabul to Berlin. He used an Italian passport in the name of Orlando Mazzotta.

### In Germany

- **Free India Centre:** Bose set it up in Berlin.
- **Azad Hind Radio:** He broadcast over it.
- **Free India Legion:** He raised it in Germany from Indian prisoners of war. It was also called the Indian Legion or the Tiger Legion.
- **"Netaji":** This honorific was first given to Bose in Germany.

In February 1943 Bose left Germany by submarine.

## The first INA (1942)

Meanwhile Japan had overrun South-East Asia.

1. **1942:** Captain Mohan Singh formed the first Indian National Army (INA, Azad Hind Fauj). He raised it from Indian prisoners of war taken by Japan in Malaya and Singapore.
2. **1942:** Rash Behari Bose led the Indian Independence League. He had lived in Japan since 1915. The League's conferences were held at Tokyo and Bangkok in 1942.
3. **December 1942:** Mohan Singh fell out with the Japanese and was arrested. The first INA broke up.

## Subhas Bose and the INA (1943–45)

1. **July 1943:** Subhas Bose reached Singapore. There Rash Behari Bose handed over the leadership of the Indian Independence League and the INA to him. Subhas Bose took command of the INA.
2. **August 1943:** He became the INA's Supreme Commander.
3. **21 October 1943:** Bose proclaimed the Provisional Government of Free India (Arzi Hukumat-e-Azad Hind) at Singapore. It was recognised by 9 countries, including Japan, Germany and Italy. It declared war on Britain and the United States.
4. **December 1943:** Japan had handed over the Andaman and Nicobar Islands to the Provisional Government. Bose hoisted the flag at Port Blair. He renamed the Andaman Islands Shaheed Dweep and the Nicobar Islands Swaraj Dweep.
5. **January 1944:** The headquarters of the INA and the Provisional Government moved to Rangoon.
6. **14 April 1944:** The INA fought beside the Japanese on the Imphal–Kohima front in 1944. On this day Colonel Shaukat Malik hoisted the INA flag at Moirang in Manipur.
7. **July 1944:** In a broadcast from Azad Hind Radio, Bose addressed Gandhi as the "Father of the Nation". He asked for Gandhi's blessings.
8. **August 1945:** The campaign had failed, and the INA retreated. Japan surrendered.
9. **18 August 1945:** Bose is reported to have died in an air crash at Taipei (Taihoku) in Formosa (Taiwan).

### The INA's units and slogans

- **Rani of Jhansi Regiment:** This was the women's regiment of the INA. Captain Lakshmi Swaminathan (later Lakshmi Sahgal) commanded it.
- **Brigades:** The INA brigades were named after Gandhi, Nehru, Azad and Subhas.

| Slogan | What it was |
| --- | --- |
| "Jai Hind" | A greeting, adopted at the Free India Centre in Germany |
| "Chalo Delhi" (Dilli Chalo) | Bose's battle cry to the INA |
| "Give me blood and I will give you freedom" (Tum mujhe khoon do, main tumhe azadi dunga) | Bose's call to Indians in South-East Asia |
| "Ittehad, Etemad, Qurbani" (unity, faith, sacrifice) | The motto of the INA |
| "Kadam Kadam Badhaye Ja" | The marching song of the INA |

## The INA trials and the naval revolt (1945–46)

The INA lost the war but won the country.

### The INA trials

1. **November 1945:** The British began trying INA officers at the Red Fort, Delhi. The first trial was of Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Kumar Sehgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon. The whole nation rose in their support.
2. **12 November 1945:** INA Day was observed.

- **The defence:** Bhulabhai Desai led the defence. Tej Bahadur Sapru, Kailash Nath Katju, Jawaharlal Nehru and Asaf Ali were also on the defence committee.
- **The sentences:** The three officers were sentenced to transportation for life. But the Commander-in-Chief, Claude Auchinleck, remitted the sentences. The government had to set the officers free.
- **The Viceroy:** Lord Wavell was Viceroy at the time of the trials.

### The Royal Indian Navy revolt

1. **18 February 1946:** The ratings of HMIS Talwar at Bombay went on strike. Vallabhbhai Patel and Jinnah persuaded them to surrender.

The trials, and the naval revolt that followed, showed the British that they could no longer rely on Indian soldiers.

## What the exams ask

- Who founded it: the Forward Bloc, the Free India Legion, the first INA (Mohan Singh).
- Where it happened: "Netaji" (Germany), the Provisional Government (Singapore), the INA flag (Moirang), the trials (Red Fort).
- Slogans of Bose: "Chalo Delhi", "Jai Hind", "Give me blood and I will give you freedom".
- Numbers and names: nine countries recognised the Provisional Government; Shaheed and Swaraj islands.
- The INA trials: the three officers tried, and the lawyer who defended them (Bhulabhai Desai).
