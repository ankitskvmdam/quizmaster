## At a glance: Bills in the Lok Sabha

| Bill | Proposal | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026 | Raise the maximum strength of the Lok Sabha from 550 to 850 | Defeated |
| Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation (Amendment) Bill, 2026 | Make Amaravati the sole permanent capital of Andhra Pradesh, with effect from 2 June 2024 | Passed |
| Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Bill, 2026 | Amend 784 provisions in 80 Central Acts; decriminalise (stop treating as crimes) 717 of them | Passed |

## Census 2027

- **1 April 2026:** Phase I of Census 2027 began. This phase is the houselisting and housing census. It runs till 30 September 2026.
  - Census 2027 is India's 16th census and the 8th since Independence.
  - It is the first digital census. People can count themselves online, which is called self-enumeration.
  - President Droupadi Murmu was the first citizen to complete self-enumeration.
  - The houselisting phase has 33 questions.

## Parliament

- **Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026:** The Union Government introduced it along with the Delimitation Bill and the Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill.
  - Its aim was a fresh delimitation that could bring in women's reservation by 2029.
  - Of the proposed 850 seats, 815 would come from States and 35 from Union Territories.
  - The Bill failed to get the special majority in the Lok Sabha. It got 298 votes, but 352 were needed.
  - The two other Bills were then withdrawn.
- **Harivansh:** He was re-elected unopposed as Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha for a third consecutive term. He is the first nominated member to hold the post.
- **CAPF (General Administration) Act, 2026:** It covers the central armed police forces (CAPF). It reserves posts in them for Indian Police Service (IPS) officers.
  - At the Inspector General level, 50 per cent of posts are reserved.
  - At the Special DG and DG (Director General) levels, 100 per cent of posts are reserved.
- **K.C. Venugopal:** He was reappointed Chairman of the Public Accounts Committee for 2026–27.

## Court rulings

- **Safe highways:** The Supreme Court held that safe travel on National Highways is part of the right to life under Article 21. It barred new dhabas and commercial structures within the highway right-of-way, the strip of land kept for the highway.
- **Disabled-friendly prisons:** The Supreme Court asked a committee headed by Justice S. Ravindra Bhat to prepare a plan for such prisons.
- **Menstrual leave:** The Karnataka High Court directed the State to implement its menstrual leave policy for women employees. The policy gives one day a month.

## Languages

- **10 April 2026:** It was Sindhi Bhasha Diwas. On this day Vice-President C.P. Radhakrishnan released the Sindhi version of the Constitution in two scripts, Devanagari and Persian.
  - Sindhi was added to the Eighth Schedule by the 21st Amendment Act, 1967.
- **Khasi and Garo:** The Meghalaya Cabinet approved an ordinance making them official languages of the State, along with English. The ordinance uses Article 345.

## States and Union Territories

- **Ladakh:** The Lieutenant Governor, Vinai Kumar Saxena, approved five new districts: Nubra, Sham, Changthang, Zanskar and Drass. Until then Ladakh had only two districts, Leh and Kargil.
- **Petroglyph Conservation Park:** The foundation stone of India's first such park was laid at Sindhu Ghat, Leh. Petroglyphs are carvings on rock.
- **Telangana:** The State declared cancer a notifiable disease. So every diagnosed case must now be reported.

## Institutions and rules

- **NCERT:** The National Council of Educational Research and Training (NCERT) was given the status of an institution deemed to be university, under the distinct category. So it can now award degrees.
- **Online gaming:** The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) notified the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Rules, 2026. They are in force from 1 May 2026. The regulator is the Online Gaming Authority of India.
- **National Building Construction Standards 2026:** The Bureau of Indian Standards published them. They replaced the National Building Code 2016.
