## Climate and clean energy

- **Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) for 2031–35:** The Cabinet approved India's next climate promise. It will be submitted to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). By 2035 India aims for:
  - 60 per cent of installed electricity capacity from non-fossil sources;
  - a 47 per cent cut from the 2005 level in the emissions intensity of GDP, that is, emissions per unit of GDP;
  - an additional carbon sink of 3.5–4.0 billion tonnes of CO2 equivalent. A carbon sink absorbs carbon dioxide from the air.
- **Green ammonia and green methanol:** The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy notified standards for both under the National Green Hydrogen Mission.
  - Green ammonia may emit at most 0.38 kg of CO2 equivalent per kg.
  - Green methanol may emit at most 0.44 kg.

## Air pollution

- **World Air Quality Report 2025:** This report of IQAir ranked India the sixth most polluted country. Pakistan was the most polluted.
  - Loni in Uttar Pradesh was the world's most polluted city.
  - New Delhi was the most polluted capital for the 8th year running.
- **Winter study:** A study of winter 2025-26 by the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air (CREA) found Ghaziabad the most polluted Indian city. Chamarajanagar in Karnataka was the cleanest.

## Biodiversity reports and treaties

- **Seventh National Report:** India submitted it to the Convention on Biological Diversity.
  - It tracks 23 national biodiversity targets through 142 indicators.
  - Only two of the 23 targets are on track.
  - It puts India's forest and tree cover at 25.17 per cent of its area.
- **Nagoya Protocol:** The protocol is on sharing the benefits of biological resources. India has issued 3,561 of the world's 6,311 compliance certificates under it, more than any other country.
- **Migratory species:** A UN update on the State of the World's Migratory Species found 49 per cent of the populations of species listed under the Convention on Migratory Species (CMS) in decline. That is nearly half.
- **CMS COP15:** The Conference of the Parties (COP) to the CMS was held in March 2026 at Campo Grande, Brazil. The Hudsonian godwit, a shorebird, was among 42 species proposed for protection.

## Wildlife and protected areas

- **Project Great Indian Bustard:**
  - The project entered its fourth year of captive breeding in Rajasthan, with 70 birds in captivity.
  - A Great Indian Bustard chick hatched in Kutch, Gujarat, after a decade. It came from the 'jumpstart' method, in which an egg from the breeding centre is placed in a wild bird's nest. It was the first inter-State effort of its kind.
- **Fireflies:** Scientists published the first full checklist of India's fireflies. It lists 92 species in 27 genera. The Western Ghats is the richest region.
- **National Chambal Sanctuary:** The Supreme Court took up on its own the issue of illegal sand mining in the sanctuary. It is India's only tri-State protected area and the main home of the gharial.
- **Kappatagudda Wildlife Sanctuary:** The Karnataka High Court ordered that 55 sq km left out earlier be added to this sanctuary in Gadag district.
- **Olive Ridley turtles:** Nearly 20,000 Olive Ridley turtle eggs were protected on Hope Island in Kakinada Bay, Andhra Pradesh.
- **Bhavasagara:** This centre of the Centre for Marine Living Resources and Ecology (CMLRE), Kochi, was made India's first National Repository for Deep-Sea Fauna.
- **Vela carli:** A freshwater crab of this species was recorded in Silent Valley National Park. It had both male and female features, a condition called gynandromorphy.

## Green cover and geo-heritage

- **National Highways Green Cover Index (2025-26):** The National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) released the first such index. It was prepared with the National Remote Sensing Centre of the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO).
- **Kalinjar Fort:** The Geological Survey of India declared the Kalinjar Fort area in Banda district, Uttar Pradesh, a national geo-heritage site.
